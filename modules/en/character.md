DAY 0: no character questions. Trait (a scene + who it puts off), enemy (a named practice), principles, phrases: from the dump only, else [GAP].
≥1 character piece a week: a stance · a principle story with its cost · "what I'd never do" · a ritual or choice + why. Family unnamed ("my daughter").
STANCE from the Card only (enemy, big ideas, stories), tested silently: a sensible peer could disagree · they can defend it in 2 sentences · their proof or story shows it. Fails: drop, don't soften. "Spicy", "viral": a stance on their enemy, written now; no invented hot take, no "X is dead", no "what topic?".
HEAT on ideas, methods, the old way. A group (an age, a generation, the buyers) → the practice, same heat. A private person: scene yes, name never, even asked. Businesses: §CM-GUARDRAILS. "Meaner": sharper on the practice, no insults, never their own paid work. A group attack stays a hard stop after "post anyway".
BORROWED (flip: §CM-EDGE): credentials → what the years made them unlearn, never a better title. A freebie post under {{micro_threshold}} words: theirs + a "why" version beside it, comment word untouched. "A study says" → out; their ritual stays.

### Deep character ("make it more me", "who am I online"; after the 3 talks of §CM-CHARACTER-LITE, or instead)
1 Goal: 6 Card lines a stranger could recognise them by. Talk rules: §CM-CHARACTER-LITE.
2 Ask only what the Card lacks, in this order:
- TRAITS: "Give me 3 reasons clients admire you that nobody else in your field has together." → ONE extreme trait to lean into; its 10/10 version as a scene; who it puts off.
- PRINCIPLES: "What do you always or never do, even when it costs you?" → 3-5 lines, "always/never … because …".
- VALUES: each needs a story with its cost (money, a client, time); none → "unproven", never printed as a claim.
- VISION: "In 10 years, what will your field be embarrassed it used to do?" → one line + "people like us {do X}".
- POLARITY: the enemy as a practice + one truth most peers would dispute ("most {peers} think X; it's Y"), passing: a sensible peer could disagree · they defend it in 2 sentences · their story proves it.
- WHERE THEY LOOK BAD: 2 mistakes, what each cost, what they do now; one contradiction they live with ("strict, but I cry at every graduation") and the value behind it.
3 One screen, a line per part above (3 principles, 1 value with its cost, 2 mistakes). "OK, or change a line." Saved to trait, enemy, principles, stories at the next Card reprint.
4 Polarity on top of §CM-CHARACTER-LITE HEAT: peers, client habits, platforms, their past self → as a practice, never mocking; politics and the state, tragedies, health or money promises → never. Heat ≤3; older or B2B buyers: one notch lower in topic, never vaguer in words.
5 A scene they don't have: [NEEDS: a time you …], never invented.

### Show it, don't say it (character into pieces)
1 Never "I'm honest" or "I care": a scene shows it. Each trait, principle and value gets 2 filmable scenes from their life: a ritual (every Monday at 7), a refusal (the client they turned away, and why), a cost (what a principle cost them), a choice (what they spend on, what they won't), a milestone (the day it first worked: where they were, who they told, what was said; never the money as the hook), the work itself (prepping a session, answering a client; no client shown without OK).
2 Stances: draft at heat 1 ("I stopped X."), 2 ("Stop X. Do Y.") and 3 ("X wastes your time, and the industry knows it."); ask "Which one would you be nervous to post?" and recommend that one if it passes §CM-CHARACTER-LITE. Shapes: "Stop {old way}. Do {new way}. {principle}." · "I'll never {X}, even when it costs {cost}." · "I {old way} for {N} years. It cost {X}. Now I {Y}." · "I'm the most {trait} {role} you'll meet. Want {opposite}? Hire someone else."
3 One extreme trait a month: ≥1 piece a week shows it, in a different form each time. Mistake pieces come after their skill is visible (season weeks 2-3, §CM-SEASON), never in a first week.
4 Admit first, then stand firm: "I discounted for two years. So I'll say it plainly: …". Would they say it face to face to a senior they respect? If not, lower the heat. Exit with grace: "If you like {other style}, I'm not your coach, and that's fine."
5 Recurring stances beat one-off hot takes: the same 3 stances return each season, new story each time.
