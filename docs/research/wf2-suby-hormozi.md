## A. Sabri Suby

### 1) Identity and business model
- **Who he is:** Sabri Suby, from Australia. He founded the agency King Kong in 2014 "from his bedroom with $50 and a laptop" (SmartCompany). He wrote *Sell Like Crazy* (2019) and has been a judge on Shark Tank Australia since the 2023 revival on Network 10.
- **Wealth and audience:** Forbes AU (2023) reported him worth about A$40M with about 50K YouTube subscribers. A vidIQ snippet put him at about 336K subscribers in April 2025. I could not get his Instagram count.
- **What he sells:**
  - **Done-for-you agency work** (SEO, ads, funnels, conversion optimisation). The main call to action is a free 30-minute strategy session. It is backed by a performance guarantee: "beat your existing conversion rate by 30% within 90 days or we work for free."
  - **His book, courses and programmes,** plus investing through Shark Tank.
  - **Claims on his site:** "$10.2B reported client revenue," "200,000 customers."
- **Audience:** small and medium business owners, including coaches, consultants, service businesses and e-commerce brands.

### 2) Content philosophy
- **Most of the market isn't buying yet.** He splits any market into 3% buying now, 17% gathering information, 20% aware of the problem but not acting, and 60% unaware. Content and lead magnets exist to win the other 97% before they are ready to buy.
- **Give before you ask** ("value-based marketing"). The goal is for prospects to think: "If this is what they give away free, imagine the paid product."
- **Selling is serving,** and it is the founder's #1 job.
- **"Sales is seduction":** you earn the sale in steps, like dating. First you earn their time with content, then their contact details, then they watch a sales video (VSL), then a call, then the close.
- **What you say matters more than how you say it.** He believes "one good hook is worth more than a thousand of the most masterfully crafted copy." He writes at a 6th-grade reading level, checked with the Hemingway app.
- **"A compelling offer beats a convincing argument."** His test: "If the offer doesn't keep you up at night, it isn't strong enough."
- **If sales calls are full of objections, the marketing is broken.**
- **Founder-led content.** With no budget, post 3–4 TikToks or Reels a day. Use a sort-by-views tool to find top creators' best videos, transcribe them, and study the patterns.
- **Research what the market is starving for** and give them that, not what you find interesting.

### 3) His content system
His "system" is a funnel more than an organic content calendar. *Sell Like Crazy* has 8 phases:
1. Identify your Dream Buyer (Halo Strategy)
2. Create the bait (the HVCO, his free lead magnet)
3. Capture leads (landing page)
4. Godfather Offer
5. Traffic
6. Magic Lantern nurture
7. Sales conversion
8. Automate and multiply (email)

- **Content forms he uses:** the HVCO (report, cheat sheet, video, checklist, calculator — the book lists 29 types), ads written like news stories, VSLs, plain-text emails, organic short-form video, and long blog posts.
- **Cadence:** 3–4 short videos a day when starting with no budget. In email, roughly two value emails for every sales email. In the nurture sequence, 80% value and 20% pitch.
- **Follow-up:** at least 12 touches across SMS, mail, email, phone and in person.
- **Repurposing:** an old LinkedIn article of his turns a blog into a slideshow, then compiles popular posts into a 20–40 page free ebook. He doesn't teach a modern batching workflow; his content is produced by the agency team.

### 4) Signature frameworks, with fill-in templates for a coach

**a) Dream Buyer Avatar (9 questions) [book]**
1. Where do they hang out?
2. Where do they get their information?
3. What are their biggest frustrations and challenges?
4. What are their hopes, dreams and desires?
5. What are their biggest fears?
6. How do they prefer to communicate?
7. What exact phrases and language do they use?
8. What does a day in their life look like?
9. What makes them happy?

*Template:* "My Dream Buyer is a [role/stage] who hangs out in [groups/podcasts], learns from [sources], is frustrated by '[quote in their words]', secretly wants [dream], fears [fear], and spends a typical [day] doing [routine]."

**b) The Halo Strategy**
In the book, the Halo Strategy is a **research method**, not a retargeting tactic. The founder may be thinking of "surround the buyer" (staying in front of prospects everywhere online). In Sabri's system that job is done by retargeting, the Magic Lantern nurture and the 12-touch follow-up, not by the Halo Strategy.

Steps:
0. Find the 20% of clients who bring 80% of revenue, and the "Power 4%" within them.
1. Open a spreadsheet.
2. Record their age, location and how they talk.
3. List their pains, hopes, dreams, barriers and uncertainties.
4. Run 2–3 keywords through AnswerThePublic.
5. Mine Reddit, Quora, Facebook groups, Amazon reviews, YouTube comments and support complaints.
6. List what they complain about; those complaints point to gaps you can fill.

*Template:* "Top clients: ___. They search: ___. Complaint quotes: '___'. Gaps nobody fills: ___."

**c) HVCO (High-Value Content Offer)**
- Answer the market's #1 "hair on fire" question.
- The headline must signal credibility, value and curiosity. Write it like a news story, and model magazine covers. Example model: "What everybody ought to know about…"
- Opt-in page: headline, subhead, "fascination" bullets, an image of the deliverable, and a form asking only for name and email.
- *Template:* "Free [cheat sheet/video]: The [number]-Step [Name] That Helps [Dream Buyer] [Result] Without [Pain] — even if [Objection]."

**d) Godfather Offer (7 components)**
1. A rationale for the deal
2. Build up the value before the price
3. Pricing strategy
4. Payment terms
5. Bonuses ("premiums")
6. A strong guarantee
7. Real scarcity

A free consultation counts only if it has value on its own.

*Template:* "[Programme] gets you [specific result] in [timeframe]. You also get [bonuses worth $X]. If [measurable condition] isn't met, [risk reversal]. Only [N] spots because [real reason]."

**e) 17-step "Secret Selling System"**
1. Call out the audience
2. Headline
3. Subheadline
4. Agitate the problem
5. Solution
6. Credentials and social proof
7. Benefits
8. Intrigue bullets
9. Godfather Offer
10. Bonuses
11. Value stack
12. Price
13. Scarcity
14. Guarantee
15. Clear call to action
16. Warning
17. P.S.

He says it works for landing pages, VSLs, blog posts and webinars.

**f) Magic Lantern Technique**
After someone opts in, send 2–3 videos or emails that lower skepticism. Keep them 80% value and 20% pitch, tease the next piece, and offer a no-pressure consult.

Email rules:
- **Get it delivered.**
- **Get it opened:** plain text, a personal sender name, subject lines of 4–5 words in lowercase.
- **Get it clicked:** facts, pattern interrupts and stories, then a call to action that tells rather than asks.

**g) VSL built from sales calls**
1. Record your sales calls.
2. Transcribe them.
3. Have AI list the top 30 objections and questions.
4. Write a VSL script that answers every one.
5. Show it right after opt-in, in place of a plain thank-you page.

**h) Other tools**
- **"Sell like a doctor" call:** find their why, ask open questions, get their desired outcome, uncover past failures, pitch in their own words, give a 2-minute pitch, state the price, then stay silent.
- **Competitor audit:** log competitors' ads, offers and prices, call their sales lines, then deliberately do the opposite.
- **Hero mechanism:** a named reason why your method works.

### 5) Hook and script structures
- **Hooks he uses:**
  - Audience callouts ("ATTENTION [AUDIENCE]").
  - News-style headlines with a big promise.
  - Pain-agitate-solve.
  - Pain language mined from reviews and comments.
- **Example outline 1 — cold "value" ad pushing the free lead magnet:**
  1. "Attention [coaches doing X]"
  2. A curiosity headline
  3. Mirror their pain in their own words
  4. The real reason it keeps happening (the mechanism)
  5. One proof point
  6. "I put it all in a free [cheat sheet]"
  7. Bullets
  8. "Click below to get it now"
- **Example outline 2 — VSL:**
  1. Callout plus big promise
  2. Agitate the problem
  3. Founder story and credibility
  4. Answer the top 5 objections
  5. Case study
  6. Offer and guarantee
  7. "Book your call"
  8. Warning
  9. P.S. recap

### 6) How content turns into sales
1. An ad or organic post sends people to the HVCO opt-in.
2. They land on a VSL page.
3. The Magic Lantern sequence nurtures them.
4. They book a free strategy session (his Godfather Offer, with a guarantee).
5. A "doctor" style sales call closes them.
6. The 12-touch follow-up picks up the rest, and people who didn't opt in get retargeted.

He has said email "drives more revenue than all my other advertising channels combined."

### 7) Worth taking for Content Machine
- **The 3/17/20/60 split maps onto the three content types:** Entertainment for the 60% unaware, Educational for the 20% and 17%, Converting for the 3%.
- **Dream Buyer 9Q plus Halo mining** gives a ready-made, AI-friendly RESEARCH module (paste in Reddit threads and reviews, pull out the exact language).
- **The HVCO as the anchor of every content series:** each series points at one free asset.
- **The Magic Lantern as a literal domino sequence,** which matches the founder's belief that trust comes from a series.
- **Objections pulled from call transcripts become a converting-content series.**
- **The 17-step structure as the skeleton for converting scripts, ads and VSLs.**
- **"Transcribe the top creators in your niche" as a hook-research routine.**
- **A Godfather Offer builder.**

### 8) What doesn't fit a DIY coach
- His system leans on paid ads, unit economics, a test budget of $500–$1K or more, and email deliverability tech.
- 17-step long-form pages are heavy work.
- His aggressive hype tone ("Hit the damn button," "Lord of the Internet") can hurt trust for a coach.
- Performance guarantees are risky when results depend on the client doing the work.
- Hero mechanisms are mostly built for products.
- He is thin on organic cadence, series formats and repurposing, so that part has to come from Hormozi.

## B. Alex Hormozi

### 1) Identity and business model
- **Who he is:** Alex Hormozi, founder of Acquisition.com, a holding company he runs with Leila Hormozi. He wrote *$100M Offers* (2021), *$100M Leads* (2023) and *$100M Money Models* (2025). He is co-owner of Skool, and earlier ran Gym Launch and Prestige Labs.
- **Scale:**
  - Instagram: about 5.0M followers (CreatorDB, October 2026).
  - YouTube: about 4.3M subscribers, 1.1B views and about 5,300 videos, with 91% of views coming from Shorts (OutlierKit).
  - Podcast: *The Game*.
- **Business model:** his content is customer acquisition for his books, Acquisition.com workshops and portfolio companies. He doesn't depend on ad revenue, which is why he can give away premium material.
- **Audience:** small business owners and aspiring entrepreneurs.

### 2) Content philosophy
- **"Give until they ask"** and **"give in public, ask in private."**
- **"Do the thing and talk about it."** Build credibility by doing real work first.
- **Document real experience,** which AI can't copy.
- **Make content for strangers and "assume nothing":** introduce yourself every time, explain every reference, repost winners.
- **"Value per second, not seconds of value."** Also: "There's no such thing as too long, only too boring."
- **Narrow beats broad.** Broad topics raised engagement but lowered opt-ins and book sales, so he went back to business-only content.
- **Education beats edutainment for his buyers.** Long videos convert far better than Shorts. He tracks revenue, not views.
- **"An ounce of pre-work is worth a pound of post."**
- **Proof beats promise:** 80% of the top 50 Gym Launch ads were built on proof.
- **The stat that backs the founder's domino belief:** 78% of gym clients had watched at least two long-form pieces before booking a call.

### 3) His content system
- **Core Four lead channels:** warm outreach, cold outreach, posting free content, and paid ads. Master one before adding the next.
- **Deep then wide:** max out one platform before adding another (or the reverse, as a deliberate choice).
- **Workflow (from his Think Media interview), five steps:**
  1. **Test** — tweet about 5 times a day and see which ideas get traction.
  2. **Record** — one full day a month for direct-to-camera long-form (4–8 pieces), plus half a day for Shorts.
  3. **Inject** — record two versions of each call to action and splice them into the middle and end.
  4. **Contextualize** — adapt each piece to the platform.
  5. **Distribute.**
- **Volume and team:**
  - Output went from 7 to 80 pieces a week.
  - Five editors (Twitter, YouTube, LinkedIn, podcast, Reels/TikTok), costing about $40K a month.
  - In six months YouTube grew from 70K to 300K and Twitter from 10K to 100K.
- **Earlier phase:** 100 Shorts filmed in one sitting every 90 days. The top 10% were analysed and doubled down on.
- **How the formats split the work:** "Shorts give breadth, longs give depth."
- **Recycling winners:** the same clip was posted 3 times in 3 weeks. Each core idea becomes a quote card (his best-reaching format on LinkedIn), a 60-second clip and a text post.
- **Habits:**
  - About 100 minutes a day on content (the "Rule of 100").
  - Measure absolute and relative growth every month.
  - One call to action per piece.
  - Repeat your core messages often.
- **Not verified:** podcast episode 845, "The Content Accordion," is about trading off quantity vs quality and narrow vs broad. I couldn't retrieve the full mechanics.

### 4) Signature frameworks, with fill-in templates for a coach

**a) Content unit: Hook → Retain → Reward**
- **Hook** = Topic + Headline + Format.
  - **Topic sources:** far past (life lessons), recent past (your calendar and conversations), present (what you're thinking now), trending, and manufactured (experiments and challenges).
  - **Headline components:** recency, relevance, celebrity, proximity, conflict, unusual, ongoing.
- **Retain** = a list, steps or a story.
- **Reward** = deliver what the hook promised.
- *Template:* "[Topic from this week's client calls] + [headline angle: conflict/unusual] → '[N] mistakes / [N] steps / the story of [client]' → payoff: [one clear takeaway]."

**b) The "content matrix"**
I found no Hormozi framework literally called a "content matrix." The closest working matrix in *$100M Leads* is 5 topic sources × 7 headline components × 3 retain formats. That gives roughly 105 angle combinations for any one idea, which makes it a strong ideation engine.

**c) "More, Better, New"** (scaling an existing channel, in this order)
1. **More:** do much more of what already works.
2. **Better:** fix the bottleneck so it converts better.
3. **New:** add new platforms or formats only after the first two stop paying off.

For a coach this works as a weekly review: double the winning format, improve its weakest step, then test one new thing.

**d) Proof-Promise-Plan intro** (for long-form)
- **Proof:** "I've [result] with [N clients]."
- **Promise:** "By the end you'll [outcome]."
- **Plan:** "We'll cover [3 steps]."

**e) Lead magnet [book plus a Claude-skill summary]**
A lead magnet is "a complete solution to a narrow problem."
- **3 types:** reveal a problem (a diagnostic), a sample or trial, or one step of a multi-step process.
- **Delivery forms:** software, information, a service, or a physical item.
- **7 steps:**
  1. Pick the problem and who has it
  2. Work out the solution
  3. Choose how to deliver it
  4. Test names
  5. Make it easy to consume
  6. Make it genuinely good
  7. Tell them the next step
- He also says: "Give away the secrets, sell the implementation." [book]

**f) Give:Ask ratio**
TV runs about 3.5:1 give to ask. Asks come in two forms:
- **Integrated:** a short ad inside a valuable piece.
- **Intermittent:** an occasional standalone offer post.

**g) Rule of 100 / "Open to Goal"**
Do 100 primary actions a day (outreach, minutes of content, or ad spend) until you hit your goal.

**h) Proof hierarchy, strongest to weakest**
1. Live, raw, unedited results
2. Stories from clients who match your target customer
3. Educational content showing expertise
4. Unrelated or dated testimonials

**i) Title formula**
"If I [audience situation], I'd do this." Example: "If I Started a Business in 2026, I'd Do This." Titles in this pattern hit 3–6x his channel average.

### 5) Hook and script structures
- **Long-form (from the Think Media transcript):** Hook → Question → Story → Framework → Explanation.
- **Shorts:** Hook → the pre-tested tweet line → Example → Explanation.
- **Calls to action:** placed about 30% of the way into a video and tied to that video's topic.
- **Example short outline for a coach:**
  1. "Most coaches lose clients in week 2. Here's why." (conflict hook)
  2. "I've onboarded 300 clients." (proof)
  3. 3-step list
  4. One-line payoff
  5. Optional: "Comment ONBOARD for my checklist."
- **Example long-form outline:**
  1. Proof, Promise and Plan in the first 60 seconds
  2. Content unit 1 (story)
  3. Call to action at about 30%, to the matching lead magnet
  4. Content units 2–3 (steps and list)
  5. Recap, then the next video

### 6) How content turns into sales
- He matches each call to action to the topic: a business-building video points to the workshop, an offer-tactics video points to the book.
- Lead magnets, book launches and Skool sit behind the content.
- He gives in public and asks in private (DMs and applications).
- Proof-heavy pieces are the ones that convert.
- Long-form viewers are far more likely to buy, which is behind the 78% "watched 2+ long-form pieces before booking" figure.

### 7) Worth taking for Content Machine
- **The Hook-Retain-Reward content unit as the template every script uses.**
- **The five topic sources as the IDEATION engine.** "Recent past = your calendar and client calls" gives a coach endless ideas.
- **The seven headline components as a hook checklist.**
- **Proof-Promise-Plan for long-form intros.**
- **A DIY version of Test → Record → Inject → Contextualize → Distribute for the SYSTEMIZE module:**
  1. Test ideas as text posts.
  2. Batch-film the winners once a month.
  3. Record two call-to-action variants.
  4. Adapt for each platform.
  5. Distribute and recycle winners.
- **Built-in habits:** a Give:Ask governor in the content calendar, "assume nothing," the Rule of 100, and a More/Better/New weekly review.
- **The lead magnet 7-step builder.**
- **The proof hierarchy for converting content.**
- **The 78% statistic as hard evidence for the founder's domino and series thesis.**

### 8) What doesn't fit a DIY coach
- His volume and team (80 pieces a week, about $40K a month, 100 Shorts per sitting) are far beyond a solo coach.
- He relies on celebrity and large-scale proof, and can ignore ad revenue because of his book business.
- **His pure-education stance conflicts with the founder's Entertainment content type.** He dropped edutainment because his buyers are business owners. For coaches, relatable entertainment may still work at the top of the funnel, but this needs testing.
- "Give until they ask" can be too slow for a coach who needs cash flow, so the pack needs explicit asks.
- His Shorts editing style needs an editor.
