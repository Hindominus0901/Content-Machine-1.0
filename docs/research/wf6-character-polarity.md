# Research brief: making character the attractor, and using polarity safely and profitably

**Scope:** research only. No files were created or edited. Sources are labelled **[DATA]** (peer-reviewed research, surveys or legal texts) or **[PRACTITIONER]** (creators, consultants, trade press). All quotes are short; everything else is paraphrased.

## 1) Key insights

1. **Polarity filters people and builds loyalty. It does not reliably grow you faster.** [DATA] Luo, Wiles and Raithel (HBR 2013; data on more than 3M consumers and 2,600 brands) found that polarizing brands earn somewhat lower but steadier stock returns, because a super-loyal core never leaves. Kraft leaned into Miracle Whip's love-it-or-hate-it image and saw social mentions rise 631% and sales rise 14%. An academic study of 1,238 brand lovers and haters (Osuna Ramírez, Glasgow 2020) found five drivers of polarization:
   - brand strength
   - uniqueness
   - association with important issues
   - ideological fit
   - community

   The same study found two-sided effects: fans defend the brand and haters retaliate. **Implication:** sell polarity as a way to attract and keep the *right* people, not as a virality hack.

2. **Polarizing language spreads fastest inside your own tribe. Hostility toward a group spreads fastest of all, and it is toxic.** [DATA]
   - Each moral-emotional word raised retweets by about 20%, mostly *within* like-minded networks (Brady et al., PNAS 2017).
   - Each word about an out-group raised the odds of a share by 67%, the strongest predictor measured (Rathje, Van Bavel & van der Linden, PNAS 2021).
   - Each negative word in a headline raised click-through by 2.3% (Robertson et al., *Nature Human Behaviour* 2023).
   - Yet people say divisive and outrage content *should not* go viral, while nuanced and educational content should (Rathje et al., 2024).

   **Implication:** attack ideas, practices and "the old way", never people or groups. That keeps the in-group effect without the backlash.

3. **Picking a fight with a big "system" works.** [DATA] Paharia, Avery and Keinan (JMR 2014; lab, field and more than 10,000 Yelp reviews) found that support for a small brand *rises* when it frames itself against a large, dominant rival. Buyers want their purchase to make a point. The same logic applies when a coach positions against an industry norm or a mass-market approach.

4. **Showing off money or perks repels the people you want to attract. Deliberate quirks attract them.** [DATA]
   - *Status-signals paradox* (Garcia, Weaver & Chen 2019): about two-thirds of people thought a luxury car would help them make new friends. Would-be friends actually preferred the person with the basic car.
   - *Red sneakers effect* (Bellezza, Gino & Keinan, JCR 2014): someone who breaks a norm *on purpose* is seen as higher-status and more competent, because it signals autonomy.
   - [DATA] Edelman 2025: 64% buy, choose or avoid brands based on their beliefs.

   This supports the founder's thesis directly. Values and quirks build the relationship. Money flexes build envy and distance.

5. **Admitting where you look bad makes you more believable.** [DATA]
   - *Pratfall effect* (Aronson 1966): a mistake makes a *competent* person more likable, and an average person less likable.
   - Two-sided messages that admit weaknesses are more credible, especially to sceptical buyers (Eisend meta-analysis, 2006).

   [PRACTITIONER] Hormozi: being believable beats being persuasive. You can only know someone is honest if they had a chance to lie and told the truth at a cost to their status.

6. **"Admirable" and "likable" are two separate dimensions people judge you on.** [DATA] People judge brands the way they judge people: on warmth (good intentions) and competence (ability). High warmth plus high competence produces *admiration*, purchase intent and loyalty (Kervyn, Fiske & Malone 2012). In content terms:
   - Competence proof covers the "admirable" stage.
   - Values, quirks and admitted mistakes cover the "likable" stage.
   - You need both.

7. **Character spreads through identity: "people like us do things like this."** [PRACTITIONER]
   - Godin: you choose who "us" is. Saying "it's not for you" is a form of respect, and aiming for the mass market makes you average.
   - Matt Gray: brands people *become* spread through belief and identity, while brands people *buy from* need constant promotion. His four steps are a common enemy, the audience's future self, proprietary language, and spaces where people change.
   - Hanlon's *Primal Branding* lists seven parts of a brand's belief system: creation story, creed, icons, rituals, non-believers, sacred words, leader. That list doubles as a character checklist.

8. **Being yourself only works on top of real skill.** [PRACTITIONER] Hormozi (Ep. 999, Sept 2026):
   - Stack rare *contradictory* traits.
   - Stop hiding your quirks: "be violently ourselves".
   - Accept that some people will be put off.
   - Being genuinely good is the *minimum*.

   Dan Koe's "niche of one" makes the same point: in an AI flood, you are the only thing that can't be copied.

10. **Vietnam: a blunt persona can work, but insulting people is costly.**
   - [PRACTITIONER/press] Shark Bình built a "Shark phũ" / "cá mập đanh đá" persona with signature phrases such as "ngáo giá" and "Em đến đây để đùa shark à". By Season 5 he had shifted to a mentoring tone. A later "lùa gà bitcoin" controversy damaged trust.

## 2) Methods and templates, with worked coach examples

### A. Excavating character without a "formal brand sheet"

The output is a **Character Card**: 1 extreme trait, 3 values each backed by a story where holding it cost something, 3 refusals, 1 enemy (old way vs new way), 1 contrarian truth, 3 signature stances, rituals and quirks, a vision (who the audience becomes), and signature words taken from clients.

| # | Method | Basis | Core prompts |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Story-based life interview** | [DATA] McAdams Life Story Interview | Describe your life as book chapters. What was your high point, low point and turning point? Who are your heroes and villains? What is the next chapter? |
| 2 | **Values in action, proven by cost** | [DATA] critical-incident and laddering methods; founder's "flipped paycheck" | Tell me about a time a principle cost you money or a client. Where do your money and time go that a stranger would find odd? |
| 3 | **Refusals and enemy** | [PRACTITIONER] Matt Gray | What do you refuse to do even though it would pay? What does your industry complain about privately but never say publicly? |
| 4 | **Contrarian truth** | [PRACTITIONER] Thiel | Fill in: "Most [peers] believe X; the truth is closer to Y." It must be defensible with evidence or a mechanism, not attitude. |
| 5 | **Rituals, taste and quirks** | [DATA] red sneakers; [PRACTITIONER] Hormozi X-factor, Hanlon rituals | Daily non-negotiables? What would you never wear, use or say? Which interest do you hide? Which two traits of yours "don't go together"? |
| 6 | **One extreme trait** | [PRACTITIONER] Soo Wei Goh (founder-supplied); Hormozi on rarity | Pick the trait you'd turn up to 10, for example bluntness, discipline, frugality, playfulness or obsession with detail. Test: would fans and critics both name it? |
| 7 | **Buyer mirror / client "why me" interview** | [DATA] laddering ("why is that important?" until you reach a value); [PRACTITIONER] JTBD switch interview | When did you first realize the old way wasn't working? What almost stopped you? Why me and not someone else? Why does that matter to you? Record their exact words, which become signature keywords. |
| 8 | **Friends and family perception** | [DATA] Reflected Best Self Exercise (Roberts et al., HBR 2005) | Ask 10 to 20 people for 2 or 3 stories of you at your best. Add: What do you tease me about? What do I never shut up about? What would you warn a stranger about? |

### B. Content templates, with a worked English example

**Persona:** *Jake*, a fitness coach for desk-bound founders.
- Extreme trait: monk-like discipline.
- Enemy: "fitness as punishment" and 6-week shred programs.
- Contrarian truth: "Most founders think they need more motivation. They need fewer decisions."

1. **Stance post (polarize the idea, offer an exit with dignity)**
   - Hook: "If your trainer celebrates you puking, fire them."
   - Body: the old way vs the new way, plus one story where Jake looked bad: "I ran bootcamps like that for 3 years and lost 70% of clients by month two."
   - Boundary: "If you love being yelled at, I'm not your coach, and that's fine."
   - Invitation to people who share the view.
2. **Values-in-action story:** "The day I refused a $9k client." The client wanted crash weight loss before a wedding. Jake explains the principle and what it cost him.
3. **Buyer mirror:** "I asked 12 clients why they picked me. Zero said 'results'. Nine said 'he doesn't make me feel guilty'." The phrase "no guilt" becomes a signature keyword.
4. **Lifestyle that shows values (not a flex):** "Where my money goes": a cheap car, but an expensive sleep coach and his kids' swim lessons. This applies the founder's flipped paycheck idea in a way that avoids the status-signals paradox.
5. **Quiet truth / "post the one that scares you":** "Most fitness coaches are out of shape mentally. I was one."
6. **Signature stances on rotation (Matt Gray's point-of-view campaigns):** three big ideas, each run for 4 to 6 weeks in different formats:
   - "Fewer decisions > more motivation"
   - "Boring is the program"
   - "No guilt, no gaps"

### C. Worked Vietnamese example

**Persona:** *Chị Lan*, a business coach for spa owners.
- Extreme trait: obsessive about numbers.
- Enemy: "chạy khuyến mãi để giữ khách" (running discounts to keep customers).
- Xưng hô: "mình – các chủ spa" / "chúng ta".

- **Stance, softened first and then firm:** "Mình từng giảm giá 50% suốt 2 năm và suýt đóng cửa. Nên giờ mình nói thẳng: khuyến mãi không giữ được khách, chỉ giữ được người săn sale." Admitting her own mistake first protects face, and the stance still lands.
- **Enemy as a practice, not a person:** say "lối cũ: đua giá" (the old way: price wars). Never name a competing spa or another coach.
- **Exit with dignity:** "Nếu chị đang muốn tăng khách bằng giảm giá, nội dung của mình chắc không hợp — không sao cả."
- **Buyer mirror:** "Mình hỏi 10 học viên vì sao chọn mình…" Then quote their actual words, for example "chị ấy nói thật, không vẽ".

### D. Polarity safety check (the gate before publishing)

1. **Target:** the post attacks an idea, practice, system or the old way. It does not attack an identity group or a named person. [DATA] Rathje 2021 and 2024
2. **Defensible:** backed by evidence or personal experience, not attitude. [PRACTITIONER] Thiel
3. **Two-sided:** includes your own mistake or a limitation. [DATA] pratfall effect; Eisend
4. **Exit with dignity:** includes a line like "not for you, and that's fine". [PRACTITIONER] Godin
5. **Face test (Vietnam):** would you say it to a respected senior peer in person?
6. **Claims:** no promised results. Use input-based wording such as "results vary". [PRACTITIONER] Hormozi; [DATA] Vietnam Advertising Law 2025
7. **A recurring stance, not a one-off hot take or drama:** Vietnamese press reports that audiences tire of repeated drama.
8. **Skill underneath:** does the post teach or prove anything? [PRACTITIONER] Hormozi: being good is the minimum

## 3) Implications for the Content Machine

1. **Add a "Character Excavation" skill** to replace the formal brand sheet:
   - a 45 to 60 minute AI-led interview combining methods 1 to 6
   - async templates for friends and family (method 8) and for clients (method 7)
   - output: the Character Card
   - suggested refresh every 90 days
2. **Extend the Edge Check** with a fifth pillar, **Character & Polarity**, or build it into the existing four:
   - *Signature keywords:* take them from client "why me" transcripts and from your signature stances, so the audience's own words feed your language.
   - *Authenticity:* require a story where the value cost something, or an admitted mistake.
   - *Authority:* pair every result with a cost or limitation (two-sided).
   - *New check:* the safety check in 2D, scored pass/fail.
3. **Weekly ideation mix that fits 1 to 2 hours:** 1 stance post, 1 values-in-action story, 1 buyer-mirror or proof post, and 1 value or how-to post told through a stance.
4. **Campaign mode:** store 3 big ideas or stances in the Character Card. The ideation step rotates each one for 4 to 6 weeks across formats, which makes repetition deliberate.
5. **Flex filter:** flag posts that show only income screenshots, cars or luxury. Prompt the user to rewrite them as "why I spend on this" values content.
6. **"Scares you" prompt:** produce 3 drafts at different intensity levels, ask which one the user is nervous to post, and recommend that one if it passes the safety check.
7. **Funnel tags** on every idea:
   - *Admirable* (competence and warmth): results that show their cost, proof
   - *Likable* (warmth): quirks, rituals, mistakes
   - *Trust*: two-sided truth, buyer mirror
   - *Convert*: invitation, "for us, not for them"
8. **Resonance metrics instead of vanity metrics:**
   - comments and DMs that repeat your phrases back
   - "this is exactly me" replies
   - sales-call answers to "why me"
   - unfollows after a stance post, treated as a healthy filter
9. **Vietnamese edition:**
   - a xưng hô choice made at setup, kept consistent. Options: mình–bạn (peer, warm), tôi–anh chị (respectful B2B), chúng ta (tribe, the "people like us" register), anh/chị–em (warm but risky across ages). Tôi–các bạn can sound like lecturing. This is [PRACTITIONER] and my own inference, not tested data.
   - a built-in "admit first, then stand firm" pattern
   - a rule against naming people or brands
   - compliance checklist under the Advertising Law in force from 1 Jan 2026: influencers must check product documents, may only promote what they have used or understand, and must disclose ads before and during the promotion. False claims can bring fines, criminal liability and compensation.
   - a note on the October 2025 draft cultural code of conduct (Ministry of Culture), which proposes a "cooperation limitation" mechanism for KOLs who violate it.
   - framing based on transparency and evidence. Dân trí (Sep 2026) explains the collapse of several KOL businesses (Hoàng Hường, Quang Linh Vlog, Hằng Du Mục and others) as trust resting on personal passion with no proof behind it.
   - [DATA] NielsenIQ Vietnam (2024): about 50% of purchases are influenced by KOLs/KOCs. The trust drivers are knowledge, core values and authenticity, engaging content, and communication style.
