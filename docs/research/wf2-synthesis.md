# Content Machine 1.0: Synthesis of the Reference-Creator Research

**Creators covered:** Iman Gadzhi, Charlie Morgan, Sabri Suby, Alex Hormozi, Nik Setting, Soo Wei Goh, Brian Luebben, Alex Garcia (House of AG, plus the Cut30 team), Mark Satterfield (gentlerainman), Robert Oliver (robthebank), and the Vietnam market brief. Creators are named by surname throughout.

**How solid the evidence is:** Many frameworks were rebuilt from video titles, descriptions and summaries because transcripts were blocked. Section 6 says which ones.

## 1. Consensus principles, written as rules the machine must enforce

**R1. Every piece has a job and a tracked next step. Success means buyers, not views.**
- **Who agrees:**
  - Gadzhi: "profit per follower"; he measures revenue per view.
  - Hormozi: tracks revenue, not views.
  - Setting: counts inbound leads per reel.
  - Goh: content should "sign clients, not just get views."
  - Morgan: tags every video's link (`?el=`) to see which video produced each lead.
  - Satterfield: every piece has a specific job.
  - Garcia: impact metrics (saves, shares, DMs) over engagement metrics.
- **How the machine enforces it:**
  - Every output gets a content type (Educational, Entertainment or Converting) and a job label: Attention, Relevance, Credibility, Differentiation or Follow-up.
  - Every output has exactly one CTA.
  - The weekly review asks for DMs, keyword comments, leads, saves and shares per piece. Views come second.

**R2. No orphan pieces. Every piece belongs to a series and points to the next one.**
- **Who agrees:**
  - Gadzhi: the 7-11-4 heuristic, and feeder videos that all link to one cornerstone video.
  - Hormozi: 78% of gym clients had watched 2 or more long-form pieces before booking.
  - Luebben: buyers binge 3–4 hours of episodes within 2 days, then book a call.
  - Goh: "by the time someone DMs you, they've already decided."
  - Suby: the Magic Lantern sequence and 12-touch follow-up.
  - Garcia: shows create familiarity, then expectation, then habit.
  - Setting: one named method appears in every piece.
  - Vietnam: "Phần 1/2/3" numbered series are already the norm.
- **How the machine enforces it:** Ideation outputs series, not loose idea lists. Every script ends with a pointer to the next piece.

**R3. Short-form earns attention and depth earns trust, so every series needs one depth anchor.**
- **Who agrees:**
  - Hormozi: "Shorts give breadth, longs give depth."
  - Gadzhi: long-form viewers convert better.
  - Luebben: "You will not make dollars off short-form."
  - Goh: always pushes Instagram viewers to YouTube long-form.
  - Setting: YouTube long-form nurtures leads.
  - Morgan: long-form is where the defensible value is.
  - Satterfield: daily story episodes work as nurture.
- **How the machine enforces it:** A series cannot ship without one anchor, which can be any of:
  - a video of 8 minutes or more
  - a podcast episode
  - a long post of 800 words or more
  - a livestream

  The short pieces are cut from the anchor or point to it.

**R4. Show proof instead of making promises, and never fabricate any.**
- **Who agrees:**
  - Hormozi: his proof hierarchy, and 80% of his top 50 Gym Launch ads were built on proof.
  - Setting: "proof is louder than claims."
  - Morgan, Setting and Goh each run a dedicated case-study channel or playlist.
  - Satterfield: the Credibility Library, and "telling people you're an expert ≠ letting them see it."
  - Suby: proof steps inside his 17-step structure.
  - Luebben: build in public.
- **How the machine enforces it:**
  - Research produces a Proof Inventory.
  - A converting script cannot be finalized while its proof slot is empty.
  - Composite or illustrative stories are labelled as such.
  - The machine never invents numbers, testimonials or statistics.

**R5. Research the buyer's own words before writing anything.**
- **Who agrees:**
  - Suby: the 9 Dream Buyer questions, Halo complaint mining, and objections pulled from sales-call transcripts.
  - Goh: the Buyer Mirror form.
  - Luebben: 100 free calls; 3 hopes and 3 roadblocks; "sell to who you were 3 years ago."
  - Garcia and Cut30: top comments, FAQs, comments on competitors' posts, AnswerThePublic.
  - Satterfield: reuse what the prospect already told you.
- **How the machine enforces it:** Research outputs a Language Bank of verbatim pains, desires, fears and objections. Every hook must reuse at least one phrase from it word for word.

**R6. The offer and a named method come before any content.**
- **Who agrees:**
  - Goh: "fix your offer before you touch your content."
  - Setting: "Profile Funnel" appears in every title.
  - Suby: the Godfather Offer and a "hero mechanism."
  - Satterfield: one central idea, repeated.
  - Garcia: a 3–5-word narrative.
  - Luebben: validate the offer through calls first.
  - Morgan: "find a group, find their problem, solve it, sell the solution."
- **How the machine enforces it:** Onboarding blocks Ideation until the user has three things:
  - a one-sentence offer
  - a named method with 3 steps
  - a 3–5-word narrative

**R7. Give away the "what", sell the implementation, and enforce a give-to-ask ratio.**
- **Who agrees:**
  - Hormozi: a give-to-ask ratio of about 3.5:1; "give in public, ask in private."
  - Setting and Luebben: give the information, sell the implementation.
  - Morgan: product-level free value.
  - Suby: 2 value emails for every sales email; 80/20 value-to-pitch in nurture.
- **How the machine enforces it:** The calendar holds a give-to-ask ratio between 3:1 and 4:1. Every piece carries a soft CTA, and each series has exactly one direct ask.

**R8. The hook decides whether anyone watches, so write the packaging before the content.**
- **Who agrees:**
  - Suby: "one good hook is worth more than a thousand of the most masterfully crafted copy."
  - Gadzhi: packaging first; the title qualifies the audience.
  - Hormozi: Hook = Topic + Headline + Format.
  - Goh: low views mean "a curiosity problem, not a value problem."
  - Garcia: a title hook plus a visual hook in the first 3 seconds.
  - Satterfield: the headline "has to earn the next 10 seconds."
  - Oliver: the on-screen text carries the whole hook.
- **How the machine enforces it:**
  - It generates 5–10 hooks per script, scores them, and the user picks one.
  - Every video gets an on-screen text hook.
  - Every hook passes Satterfield's two-sentence curiosity test.

**R9. Document real work instead of performing, and keep production low.**
- **Who agrees:**
  - Morgan: grew from 39K to 100K subscribers in about 2 months after switching to raw phone video.
  - Luebben: front camera, posted 90 seconds later.
  - Setting: documented over performative content.
  - Goh: "stop performing, start documenting."
  - Hormozi: "do the thing and talk about it."
  - Oliver: raw selfie video.
  - Gadzhi: relatability beats expertise.
- **How the machine enforces it:**
  - Every template can be shot on a phone in one location.
  - Ideation draws on the user's week: calendar, client calls, DMs.

**R10. Repeat and recycle what wins, and turn one idea into many formats.**
- **Who agrees:**
  - Gadzhi: re-titles and re-thumbnails old videos.
  - Hormozi: the same clip posted 3 times in 3 weeks; More/Better/New.
  - Garcia: carousels repackage winning videos; Trial Reels.
  - Goh: "One video planned = ten made"; repackage proven outliers.
  - Satterfield: one idea becomes a video, email, post, podcast and article.
  - Luebben: 10 shorts cut from each podcast episode.
- **How the machine enforces it:** Every anchor is broken into 5 or more derivative pieces, and there is a monthly recycling step.

**R11. One CTA per piece, matched to how ready the viewer is. The default low-friction step is a comment or DM keyword.**
- **Who agrees:**
  - Hormozi: one CTA, matched to the topic, about 30% of the way in.
  - Satterfield: two CTAs by readiness, MORE (general) vs CONFIDENCE (focused).
  - Setting: comment-keyword CTAs and a DM-keyword bio.
  - Luebben: ManyChat keywords that send people to episodes.
  - Gadzhi: soft CTA within 90 seconds, strong CTA at the end.
  - Morgan: the first line of every description is a CTA.
  - Vietnam: comment, then inbox, then add on Zalo.
- **How the machine enforces it:** Each series has one keyword and one gate (lead magnet), used in every piece.

**R12. Own the relationship by routing every series into a channel the user controls.**
- **Who agrees:**
  - Luebben: email is the only owned audience; social is "borrowed."
  - Garcia: "Social is rented land; email is owned land."
  - Suby: email "drives more revenue than all my other advertising channels combined."
  - Satterfield: quiet follow-up by email.
  - Vietnam: Zalo plays this role.
- **How the machine enforces it:** Every series ends in a private track: email in the English edition, Zalo or Messenger in the Vietnamese edition.

**R13. Personality is why people stay, so re-introduce yourself to strangers in every piece.**
- **Who agrees:**
  - Luebben: "They'll follow you for the education, but they'll stay because of your personality"; his identity-line CTA.
  - Goh: pathos beats expertise; his "Likeable" stage.
  - Hormozi: "assume nothing" and introduce yourself every time.
  - Garcia: character-driven shows.
  - Satterfield: curated personality.
  - Oliver: a recognizable persona.
- **How the machine enforces it:** The Voice Card holds a one-line identity statement used in every piece, and the content mix reserves a personality layer.

**R14. Post consistently and test in volume until a signal appears, then double down on it.**
- **Who agrees:**
  - Hormozi: 100 Shorts every 90 days, then double down on the top 10%.
  - Cut30: publish 100 videos; about 1 in 10 becomes a hit; test each format 2–4 times.
  - Luebben: make 100 videos before hiring anyone.
  - Morgan: about 3 videos a week.
  - Gadzhi: competence comes from volume.
- **How the machine enforces it:** It keeps a running published-count toward 100. A format is judged only after 3 attempts.

## 2. Conflicts and the recommended default for a DIY coach

| Tension | Camp A | Camp B | Default for a time-poor DIY coach |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Volume vs. quality** | Hormozi (80 pieces a week), Suby (3–4 shorts a day), Luebben (2 shorts a day plus 5 podcasts), Goh (100+ a month), Cut30 ("publish 100") | Garcia ("the aim should never be volume"), Gadzhi ("could go a year without posting" thanks to rehashing), Satterfield (one idea, many forms), Morgan (about 3 a week) | **Minimum viable cadence:** one batch session of 2–3 hours produces 1 anchor plus 4–6 derivatives a week. "100 pieces" is a 6-month milestone, not a weekly quota. The quality gate is hook + proof + CTA, not production value. |
| **Raw vs. polished** | Morgan, Luebben, Oliver, Setting (documented content) | Goh (premium visual identity; "don't look like a course creator"), Setting (cinematic reels), Gadzhi ($100K trailers) | **Raw phone footage with a consistent identity:** same set, caption style, fonts and series title card. For a solo coach, consistency is the affordable signal of quality. |
| **Niche vs. broad** | Hormozi (going broad cut opt-ins), Satterfield ("Whales Not Minnows"), Garcia ("specificity is the strongest signal") | Goh ("from puddle to ocean"), Luebben (about two-thirds off-niche; grow broad first), Gadzhi (3:1 broad growth content) | **Niche-adjacent reach:** broad identity and emotion topics the ideal client shares (Garcia's "busy dads, not CrossFit bros"). No off-niche lifestyle content. Every reach idea must pass "would my buyer watch this?" |
| **Education vs. entertainment** | Hormozi dropped edutainment for business buyers | Luebben (education, lifestyle, humor rotation), Garcia (Performer archetype, relatable top-of-funnel), Goh (POV shorts), Oliver | **Keep Entertainment, capped at about 20–30% of pieces.** It must be tied to the client's world, not the coach's lifestyle. Track inbounds; drop a format that gets zero after 10 tries. |
| **"Give until they ask" vs. asking early** | Hormozi, Luebben (about 2 years before an offer), Goh (patient selling) | Suby, Gadzhi (deadline offers), Setting (an Offer pillar at 20%), Morgan | **Ask from week 1, within limits:** a soft keyword CTA on every piece and one direct ask per series, roughly every 2 weeks. Coaches need cash flow. |
| **Funnel complexity** | Suby (lead magnet → VSL → Magic Lantern → call → 12 touches), Gadzhi (VSL plus SDRs) | Morgan ("funnels suck"; while running a full funnel), Setting (the profile as the funnel, not a VSL) | **Simplest path:** comment keyword → DM or email → one lead magnet → booking or checkout. No VSL, webinar or setters in v1. VSL is an optional Ads add-on. |
| **Scarcity** | Gadzhi ("today only", daily price rises), Suby (scarcity, but real) | Satterfield (quiet follow-up), Goh (patience), Vietnamese distrust | **Real deadlines only:** a cohort start, real capacity or a bonus expiry, always with the reason stated. Fake timers are banned. |
| **Income and lifestyle flex** | Gadzhi, Goh ("$408k/mo at 20"), Setting, Morgan | Satterfield, Luebben (the underdog), the Vietnamese "nổ" (bragging) taboo | **No income flex and no luxury.** Use specific client results with consent, context and "results vary". |
| **Platform breadth** | Goh ("be everywhere"), Gadzhi (30+ accounts) | Hormozi (go deep, then wide), Luebben (2-Year Rule) | **One primary platform plus one owned channel.** Cross-post without edits only. Add a platform only through More → Better → New. |
| **Expert vs. beginner stance** | Satterfield (show judgment and interpret), Hormozi (proof-led authority) | Luebben (Enthusiastic Beginner), Gadzhi ("relatability beats expertise") | **The user picks a stance at onboarding.** Default for non-experts is a guide who documents real work: teach judgment through cases, and run a build-in-public series. |
| **Organic vs. paid** | Suby (ads), Setting (paid profile-visit ads), Hormozi (Core Four) | Morgan, Luebben, Goh (organic until recently) | **Organic first.** The Ads module only repurposes proven organic winners into a "value ad" for the lead magnet, with a small test budget. |
| **Who picks clips** | Gadzhi and Goh (clipping teams) | Luebben (offshore clipping "never works"; the founder picks) | **The user picks clips** with an AI "clip finder" prompt that scores moments in a transcript. |

## 4. The Domino trust model

### 4a. How the reference creators move a stranger to a buyer

| Creator | Observed path | Main trust lever |
|---|---|---|
| Gadzhi | Short-form → main account → mix of Growth and Nurture long-form → cornerstone VSL → checkout or call → follow-up | 7-11-4: about 7 hours, 11 touchpoints, 4 places |
| Suby | Content or ad → free lead magnet → VSL → Magic Lantern → strategy session → 12-touch follow-up | Market split: 60% unaware, 20% aware but not acting, 17% gathering information, 3% buying now |
| Hormozi | Shorts for breadth → long-form for depth (2+ pieces) → CTA at about 30% to a matching lead magnet → private ask | Proof plus a give-to-ask ratio |
| Luebben | Guest appearance or reel → link in bio → 3–4-hour binge within 2 days → 15-minute call | Depth in a short window |
| Goh | Reach → middle-of-funnel "value debt" → bottom-of-funnel case studies → DM → post-booking video → call | Admirable → Likeable → Credible |
| Setting | Reel → profile (built as the funnel) → comment keyword → DM → setter → call; the case-study channel "before you book" | One named method, everywhere |
| Garcia | Show → familiarity → expectation → habit | Repetition of a fixed format |
| Satterfield | Attention → Relevance → Credibility → Differentiation → Follow-up | Judgment, plus patient follow-up |
| Vietnam | Phần 1 → Phần 2 → comment → inbox → Zalo → livestream or free workshop → 1-to-1 consultation → VietQR payment | Part-numbered series plus live interaction |

**The shared sequence**, in the order a viewer moves through it:
1. **Recognition:** "that's so me."
2. **Reframe:** "the real reason is…"
3. **Depth:** "this person really knows."
4. **Choice:** "this approach beats the others." This is Gadzhi's Sale 1.
5. **Possibility:** "someone like me did it, and I can do step 1." This is Sale 2.
6. **Doubt removal:** objections are answered.
7. **Low-risk step:** a comment keyword or the lead magnet.
8. **Private nurture.**
9. **An ask with a real reason to act now.**
10. **Post-sale reassurance:** this produces a testimonial, which becomes the next series' proof.

### 4b. Proposed model: the Domino Series

**What a Domino Series is:** a 14-day unit built around ONE core problem (one roadblock from the 100-idea grid). It has one named method, one depth anchor, one gate (lead magnet plus keyword) and one offer.

**The Series Brief card**, filled in before any script is written:
- **Core problem:** the buyer's own words, from the Language Bank.
- **Named method:** the method name plus 3 steps.
- **Gate:** the lead magnet and its keyword.
- **Offer:** what it is, who it's for and who should skip it, the price, and the real reason for the deadline.
- **Proof asset:** a case study, a Day N log, or an illustrative story (labelled).
- **Top 3 objections:** taken from the Objection Bank.
- **Series markers:** the title card, opening line, part numbering and hashtag.

**The seven public dominoes:**

| # | Domino | Type | Job | Who it's for (Suby split) | Default format | Template from |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | **Spark** | Entertainment | Attention | 60% unaware | 15–30s POV / IYKYK / one-liner / ten-shot | #7, #16 |
| D2 | **Reframe** | Educational | Relevance | 20% aware but not acting | 30–60s reel or carousel | #17 |
| D3 | **Cornerstone** (depth anchor) | Educational | Credibility | 20% aware + 17% gathering information | 8–20 min video, podcast, 800–1,500-word post, or 45-min live | #13, #14, #15 |
| D4 | **Verdict** (Sale 1) | Converting, editorial style | Differentiation | 17% gathering information | Report-card carousel or insider exposé | #18, #19 |
| D5 | **Proof** (Sale 2) | Converting | Credibility (peer) | 17% gathering information | Case study, Day N entry, or peer story | #12, #20 |
| D6 | **Doubt-killers** (×2) | Educational / Converting feeders | Follow-up | 17% → 3% | "[Objection]? Watch this." | #2, #9 |
| D7 | **Ask** | Converting, direct | Conversion | 3% buying now | Offer post + live Q&A + broadcast | #23 |

**Private track,** triggered by the gate keyword (#22), over days 0–7:
- **P1:** deliver the gift and ask one question.
- **P2:** a case story.
- **P3:** a mini-lesson.
- **P4:** an invitation to a call or live session.
- **P5:** the offer, with the real deadline.

After the private track:
- **Non-buyers** move into Quiet Follow-Up, then into the next series.
- **Buyers** get a reassurance message and the Buyer Mirror form. Their answers become D5 of the next series.

**Linking rules** (what makes the pieces fall into each other):
1. The named-method phrase appears in every piece.
2. The same series markers appear on every piece: title card, "Part N" or "Phần N", opening line and hashtag. This applies Garcia's fixed elements.
3. Every piece ends by pointing to the next domino or to D3.
4. There is one keyword for the whole series.
5. Every piece contains a one-line identity re-introduction.
6. Proof captured in one series feeds D5 of the next.

**Standard 14-day schedule** (one 2–3-hour batch session):

| Day | Piece |
|---|---|
| 1 | D1 Spark |
| 2 | D2 Reframe |
| 3 | D3 Cornerstone |
| 5 | D4 Verdict |
| 7 | D5 Proof |
| 9 | D6a Doubt-killer |
| 10 | D6b Doubt-killer |
| 11 | A cut-down of D3 |
| 12–14 | D7 Ask (post, live session and broadcast) |

- **Touches:** about 10 public plus 5 private, so roughly 15 touches across at least 4 places (short-form platform, depth platform, email or Zalo, DM). That satisfies the 7-11-4 heuristic for touches and places. The "7 hours" builds up across several series.
- **Give-to-ask:** about 6:1 in public, and about 3–4:1 once the private track is counted.
- **Batching:** film D3 first, then cut D2, D4 and D6 from it. D1 and D5 are separate 15-minute shoots.

**Lite schedule** (about 2 hours a week): D3 + D1 + D2 + D5 + D7, plus a 3-message private track. That is about 8 touches per series; the 11-touch target is reached across two consecutive series.

**Optional layer:** a weekly Signature Show episode (#10), which builds habit between series.

**Chaining series together:**
- Order the series along the buyer's journey from Point A to Point B: 10 roadblocks give about 20 weeks of series.
- Every 3–6 months, run a "launch series" instead, modelled on Gadzhi's mini-launches: a free 3–5-part series, then a deadline offer.

**Adapting to the user's stage:**
- **No clients yet:** D5 becomes a Day N build-in-public entry, a beta client, or the user's own documented result. Composite stories are labelled "illustrative".

### Trust and tone in a low-trust market
- **Why it matters:** the Kera prosecutions; only 13% favour influencer reviews; 54% trust ordinary users more; 67% say they can spot fake reviews.
- **What to strip from the English mechanisms:**
  - Income and age flex titles (Goh, Setting, Morgan).
  - Luxury lifestyle posts.
  - "Today only" and daily price rises (Gadzhi).
  - Suby's hype tone.
  - "Cam kết 100%" (100% guarantees).

  - Public pricing. "Giá ib" (price on request) annoys buyers.

  - No planted comments ("seeding").
  - A "chống lùa gà" (anti-scam-vibe) style guide.

### Compliance layer (required in every converting script)

**How the rules change specific English mechanisms:**

| English mechanism | Vietnamese change |
|---|---|
| Report Card (#18) | Compare *approaches*. Direct comparison with competitors is banned. |

| Comment → Zalo lead capture | Needs consent wording under the Personal Data Protection Law. Buying customer lists is illegal. |
