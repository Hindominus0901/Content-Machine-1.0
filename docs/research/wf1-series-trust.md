# Content Machine 1.0 brief: how trust builds over many pieces (the domino effect) and how to keep a steady posting rhythm

**Note on method:** this session had already used all of its web searches, so this brief rests on primary pages I could fetch directly. Claims I could not check here are marked "not re-verified".

---

## 1) Key insights

**1. Repeated exposure builds liking. The returns level off, and the gains come from varying the format.**
- *DATA.* Zajonc (1968) found that simple exposure raises liking. Bornstein's 1989 meta-analysis covered 208 experiments and found an effect size of r = 0.26.
  - Liking peaks at about **10–20 exposures**.
  - Brief exposures produce the strongest effect.
  - A delay between exposure and judgment makes the effect stronger.
  - Too many repeats of the *same* stimulus cause satiation.
- **What the system does with this:** the same core idea appears many times, each time in a different format, hook or story. Copy-pasting the same piece wears it out.

**2. About three distinct exposures move someone from "what is this?" to "this applies to me".**
- *DATA / theory.* In Krugman's three-exposure model (Journal of Advertising Research, 1972):
  - Exposure 1 = "What is it?"
  - Exposure 2 = "What of it?" (is it relevant to me?)
  - Exposure 3 = a reminder at the point of decision. Later exposures repeat the third.
- **What the system does with this:** every core belief in a series gets at least 3 pieces: (a) name it, (b) make it personal, (c) remind them when they are ready to act.

**3. Repetition also raises how true a claim feels.**
- *DATA.* In the illusory-truth effect (Hasher, Goldstein & Toppino 1977), people's confidence in repeated statements rose from 4.2 to 4.6 to 4.7 across three sessions. Statements that were not repeated stayed flat.
- Fazio (2015) found the effect holds even when people know the correct answer.
- **What the system does with this:**
  - Give every user 3–5 "signature beliefs" and repeat them on purpose, worded differently each time.
  - These beliefs must be true. This is an ethics guardrail the pack should state outright.

**4. Spacing pieces out works better than posting them all at once.**
- *DATA.* Cepeda et al. (2006) found spaced practice beat massed practice in **259 of 271** comparisons.
- **What the system does with this:**
  - Spread a series over days or weeks rather than posting 7 pieces in one day.
  - Bring key ideas back weekly.

**5. Relatable, personal content builds trust. It is not filler.**
- *DATA.* Parasocial research, starting with Horton & Wohl (1956), shows repeated mediated exposure reduces uncertainty about a person, and that leads to liking.
- Sokolova & Kefi surveyed 1,209 people. Younger buyers weighted parasocial attachment to an influencer *above* credibility when deciding to buy.
- **What the system does with this:** the founder's "entertainment" category (POV, "if you know you know", daily life) is how people come to feel they know you. It is not just a reach tactic.

**6. Most buyers are not ready to buy today. Content has to keep you remembered until they are.**
- *DATA.* LinkedIn B2B Institute / Ehrenberg-Bass (the 95-5 rule): about **95% of potential buyers are out of market** at any given moment.
- 96% of B2B marketers expected the main effect of a campaign within 2 weeks, which is the wrong expectation.
- **What the system does with this:**
  - Judge content over 90+ days, not per post.
  - Recurring, recognisable formats are what keep you remembered.

**7. Buyers choose a shortlist before they ever contact you.**
- *DATA.* 6sense 2025 Buyer Experience Report (about 4,000 B2B buyers):
  - Buyers make first contact **61% of the way through** their journey.
  - **95% of wins come from the day-one shortlist.**
  - The vendor contacted first wins 77–81% of the time.
  - 94% of buyers use AI chat tools (LLMs) during their buying process.
- **What the system does with this:** content gets you onto the shortlist. Converting content has to exist *before* the prospect reaches out. It is not something to write afterwards.

**8. People who have consumed a lot of your content close at much higher rates.**
- *DATA, single company, practitioner-reported.* At Marcus Sheridan's River Pools, prospects who read **30+ pages closed at about 80%**, against **about 25%** for those who read only a few pages (IMPACT, "Assignment Selling").
- **What the system does with this:**
  - Build a "Start Here" binge path.
  - *Assign* it to leads before sales calls.

**9. Buyers loop between exploring and evaluating, and six biases tip the decision.**
- *DATA.* Google's "Messy Middle" (July 2020) tested **310,000 simulated purchase scenarios**.
- Buyers loop through *exploration* and *evaluation*. Six biases decide the outcome: category heuristics, power of now, social proof, scarcity, authority, power of free.
- Results:
  - A fictional cereal brand took **28%** of preference from the shopper's favourite brand using 5-star reviews plus a 20% free bonus.
  - A fictional insurer reached **87%** preference when all six biases were applied.
- **What the system does with this:** converting content should deliberately include at least 2 of the 6 biases.

**10. Posting more often helps up to a point. The biggest jump is from 1–2 to 3–5 posts a week.**
- *DATA.* Buffer, Instagram (Aug 2025): 2.1 million posts from 102,000 accounts, using fixed-effects models that compare each account with its own baseline.

  | Posts per week | Weekly follower growth | Reach per post (vs 1–2 posts/week) |
  |---|---|---|
  | 1–2 | +0.12% | baseline |
  | 3–5 | +0.26% (more than double) | about +12% |
  | 6–9 | +0.44% | about +18% |
  | 10+ | +0.66% | about +24% |

- *DATA.* Buffer, LinkedIn (Aug 2025): 2 million+ posts from 94,000 accounts. Extra impressions per post, compared with the lowest-frequency group:
  - 2–5 posts/week: +1,182
  - 6–10 posts/week: +5,001
  - 11+ posts/week: +16,946
  - Buffer calls **2–5 posts a week the sustainable sweet spot**.
- *PRACTITIONER / platform guidance (Hootsuite, 2025):*
  - Hootsuite recommends 3–5 posts a week on Instagram and TikTok.
  - TikTok officially suggests 1–4 posts a day.
  - LinkedIn officially suggests daily posting.
  - Business accounts averaged 5.5 LinkedIn posts a week in Q1 2025.
- **Minimum the system should set:** 3 posts a week on one main platform.

**11. "7-11-4" is a rule of thumb, not a finding.**
- *PRACTITIONER folklore.* The claim is 7 hours of content, 11 touchpoints, 4 locations. It is widely attributed to Google, but I could not find a primary Google source.
- The nearest real Google data is the 2011 Zero Moment of Truth (ZMOT) study: shoppers used **10.4 sources** before deciding, up from 5.3 in 2010 (not re-verified in this session).
- **How to use it:** as a *design target*, not as a promise.

**12. Hours of bingeable content usually comes from a back catalog, not this month's posts.**
- *Calculation.* One month of 4 long-form pieces at 20 minutes each, 20 shorts at 1 minute each and 4 emails at 4 minutes each adds up to about **2 hours**.
- Reaching "7 hours" therefore requires a library someone can binge. Example: one 20-minute long-form piece a week reaches 7 hours in about 21 weeks.
- **What the system does with this:** besides posting, the pack must produce long-form pillar pieces and keep them findable.

**13. Open loops work by pulling people forward to the next part, not by making it memorable.**
- *DATA.* Ghibellini & Meier's 2025 meta-analysis found **no reliable memory advantage for unfinished tasks** (the Zeigarnik effect).
- It did find a reliable **tendency to resume** an unfinished task (the Ovsiankina effect).
- **What the system does with this:**
  - "Part 2 tomorrow" endings help only if Part 2 is easy to find: pinned, in a playlist, or linked.
  - The cliffhanger brings people back; it does not make the content stick.

## 2) Frameworks & templates

### A. The Domino Belief Ladder (proposed core framework for the Content Machine)

- **Sources:**
  - Krugman's 3 exposures and the mere-exposure effect (DATA).
  - Schwartz's 5 awareness stages, *Breakthrough Advertising* (1966) (PRACTITIONER).
  - The "false beliefs" and Epiphany Bridge ideas popularised by Russell Brunson (PRACTITIONER, not re-verified).
- **How it works:**
  - A buyer needs about 5–7 beliefs in place before buying.
  - Each piece of content knocks over one belief, so each domino is one belief.
  - Pieces are ordered by awareness stage: Unaware → Problem-aware → Solution-aware → Product-aware → Most-aware.
- **Template** (fill in once per offer):
  1. "I have a problem: ____ (and it's costing me ____)." → *Problem-aware*
  2. "It's not my fault / the real cause is ____." → *Problem-aware*
  3. "The usual fix (____) doesn't work because ____." → *Solution-aware*
  4. "A better approach exists: ____ (named method)." → *Solution-aware*
  5. "This person ([name]) is the one who can guide me, because ____ (proof)." → *Product-aware*
  6. "I can do this even though ____ (objection: time, money, past failure)." → *Product-aware*
  7. "Now is the right time because ____." → *Most-aware*
- **Each piece record holds:** `belief #`, `stage`, `type (Edu/Ent/Conv)`, `hook`, `one takeaway`, `link-forward line`.

### B. Give-Give-Give-Ask / Jab-Jab-Jab-Right-Hook

- **Sources:**
  - Gary Vaynerchuk, *Jab, Jab, Jab, Right Hook* (2013) (PRACTITIONER).
  - Alex Hormozi, *$100M Leads* (2023), the "give until they ask" principle (PRACTITIONER; exact ratio not re-verified).
- **How it works:**
  - Platform-native value pieces (the "jabs") earn attention.
  - Then a clear ask (the "right hook").
- **Mapping to the founder's 3 types:**
  - Educational + Entertainment = jabs.
  - Converting = the hook.
- **Template:** for every 3–4 give pieces, publish 1 ask piece. During a launch window, compress this to 1:1.

### C. Recurring series formats (the series library)

All of these are PRACTITIONER formats. Each is designed to raise repeat exposure, which is the mere-exposure mechanism.

| Format | How it works | Fill-in template |
|---|---|---|
| **Named recurring show** | Same name, same day, same structure each week | "[Show name] #[n]: [specific problem] in 60 seconds" |
| **Numbered series** | A fixed number of parts that together form one whole | "[N] mistakes that keep [audience] stuck at [plateau] — #[n]: ____" |
| **Mini-course in posts** | One framework taught one step per post | "How to [outcome] without [pain] — Step [n] of [N]: ____" |
| **Challenge** | Audience performs one daily action and reports back | "Day [n]/[N] of the [name] challenge: today do ____. Comment DONE." |
| **Build-in-public** | Weekly progress on a real goal, with real numbers | "Week [n] of [goal]: what I tried, the number, what I'm changing" |
| **Client teardown / case file** | One client problem, diagnosis and fix (anonymised) | "Case #[n]: [client type] had [symptom]. Real cause: ____. Fix: ____. Result: ____." |
| **Part 1 / Part 2 cliffhanger** | Split one story or idea at its peak | Closing line: "Part 2: the [one thing] that [surprising result]. It's pinned / in the playlist." |

**Linking rule:** every piece ends with a *link-forward* line that names the next domino. Every series has a home (a pinned post, playlist, or a "Start Here" highlight or post) so the resume tendency in insight 13 has somewhere to go.

### D. Assignment Selling → "Start Here" binge path

- **Source:** Marcus Sheridan and IMPACT (PRACTITIONER, with company data).
- **How it works:**
  - Curate the 7–10 pieces that knock over beliefs 1–7.
  - Send them to every lead before the call ("Watch these 3 before we talk").
  - IMPACT's suggested assets include an intro/bio video, an expectations video, a self-assessment, a pricing explainer, comparisons, and the "80% video", which answers the questions that come up on 80% of sales calls.
- **Template email:** "Before our call on [date], watch these 3 (about 20 minutes total): 1) [belief 2 piece] 2) [method piece] 3) [case study]. You'll get 2x more out of our time."

### E. Messy Middle bias checklist (for converting pieces)

- **Source:** Google, July 2020 (DATA).
- **Rule:** each converting script includes at least 2 of the following:
  - **Category heuristics:** spell out price, time to result and the process in 3 steps.
  - **Power of now:** "first win in 7 days".
  - **Social proof:** a named result.
  - **Authority:** credentials or media mentions.
  - **Scarcity:** a real cohort cap or deadline.
  - **Power of free:** a free audit, template or bonus.

### F. Pillar → micro-content repurposing

- **Source:** GaryVee content model (PRACTITIONER, with his reported numbers). One keynote became 30+ pieces in one case and 64 in another, for over 35 million total views. Micro-content drives people back to the pillar.
- **Template** (1 pillar, about 15–20 minutes of talking, produces 8–12 assets):
  - 3–5 vertical clips, one per sub-point.
  - 1 carousel (the framework).
  - 1 LinkedIn text post (the story).
  - 1 email (pillar summary plus a link).
  - 1 quote graphic or tweet thread.
  - 1 "Part 2" teaser.
  - 1 converting clip ("this is step 2 of what we do in [offer]").

### G. Feedback loop: More, Better, New

- **Source:** Hormozi, "More Better New" (a module in his Leads course; PRACTITIONER).
- **Steps:**
  1. **More:** if a piece beats your trailing 10-post median by 2x, make 3 follow-ups:
     - same idea with a new hook
     - same hook in a new format
     - a sequel or "Part 2"
  2. **Better:** improve the weakest part of the script (usually the hook or the first 3 seconds).
  3. **New:** only about 20% of output tests brand-new formats.
- **Metrics per content type** (practitioner synthesis):

| Type | Job | Watch | Signal to double down |
|---|---|---|---|
| Entertainment / relatable | Reach + "they get me" | Reach to non-followers, average % watched, **shares/sends per reach**, follows per 1,000 reach | Shares above your median. (Instagram has publicly emphasised sends as a reach signal; not re-verified.) |
| Educational | Authority + retention | **Saves**, comments asking questions, profile visits, watch time | Saves per reach above median → turn it into a mini-course series |
| Converting | Leads and sales | Keyword comments or DMs, link clicks, booked calls, **"what did you watch before booking?"** answers | Any piece named on 2+ intake forms → pin it to the "Start Here" path |

### H. Worked example 1: 7-day domino

**Dana** is a pricing consultant for small creative agencies. Her offer is a 6-week "Value Pricing Sprint" for $3,000.

| Day | Type / stage | Piece | Link-forward line |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ent / Unaware | POV skit: "POV: the client says 'can you just add one more page?' for the 4th time" | "Tomorrow: why this keeps happening (it's not the client)." |
| 2 | Edu / Problem | "Scope creep isn't a client problem. It's an hourly-pricing problem. Here's why." (belief 2) | "Next: the fix most agencies try, and why it fails." |
| 3 | Edu / Solution | "Why 'stricter contracts' don't stop scope creep" (belief 3) | "Thursday: the 3-tier menu I use instead." |
| 4 | Edu / Solution | Mini-course part 1: "The 3-tier menu" (belief 4, named method) | "Tomorrow: what happened when an agency actually used it." |
| 5 | Edu-proof / Product | Case file: "8-person agency: $8k → $14k average project in 60 days" (belief 5) | "Saturday: the exact pricing-call script." |
| 6 | Conv (editorial) | "Steal my pricing-call script" — the full script, ending with "this is step 2 of the 5 steps in the Sprint" (beliefs 5–6) | "Tomorrow: 3 Sprint spots open for November." |
| 7 | Conv (direct response) | Offer piece: who it's for, 3-step process, result, a free 20-minute price audit for the first 3, deadline. CTA: "Comment PRICE." (belief 7 + 4 biases) | Pin days 2, 4 and 5 as "Start Here." |

### I. 14-day blueprint (two arcs)

- **Days 1–7, "Problem arc":** 3 relatable/POV pieces, 3 educational "why it happens" or myth-bust pieces, 1 soft converting piece (a lead magnet such as a checklist or self-assessment, using the power of free).
- **Days 8–14, "Method arc":**
  - A 5-part mini-course (days 8–12), one step a day.
  - Day 13: case study plus an objection-handling FAQ.
  - Day 14: direct-response offer with a deadline.
- **Repeat rule:** the method name appears in at least 6 of the 14 pieces. Signature belief #2 appears at least 3 times in different formats (Krugman and illusory-truth effect).

### J. 30-day blueprint (one awareness stage per week)

**Worked example 2:** Minh is a career coach helping mid-level engineers reach senior level. Offer: a $1,500 "Promotion Packet" program.

| Week | Stage / beliefs | Mix | Anchor pieces |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Unaware → Problem (beliefs 1–2) | 50% Ent / 40% Edu / 10% Conv | "IYKYK: the 1:1 where your manager says 'keep doing what you're doing'"; "Why great code doesn't get you promoted" |
| 2 | Problem → Solution (3–4) | 30 / 60 / 10 | Numbered series "5 promotion myths"; long-form pillar on the "Impact Ledger" method |
| 3 | Solution → Product (5–6) | 20 / 50 / 30 | 3 client case files; "How to write your promo packet, part 1/2"; objection piece "No time? 20 minutes a week" |
| 4 | Product → Most-aware (7) | 20 / 30 / 50 | Live Q&A, testimonials, FAQ carousel, 2 direct-response offer posts, deadline reminder |

**Rules for the 30-day blueprint:**
- Each week includes 1 long-form pillar (about 20 minutes), which builds toward the "7 hours" library.
- Every pillar is cut into micro-content (Framework F).
- **Challenge variant:** run weeks 2–3 as a "14-day promo-ready challenge" with daily actions and DONE comments.

### K. Weekly cadence templates

The planning, scripting and repurposing steps below are AI-assisted by the pack.

**"Minimum Viable Machine": 2–3 hours a week.** Output is 3–4 short videos, 1 text post or carousel and 1 email. This clears Buffer's 3-to-5-posts-a-week threshold.

| Block | Time | Task |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | 30 min | Research: mine comments, DMs and sales-call notes; pick this week's domino(s) from the series plan |
| Mon | 30 min | Script with AI: 1 core story/educational script plus 3 shorts (1 Ent, 1 Edu, 1 Conv) from the templates |
| Tue | 60 min | Batch-record everything in one sitting |
| Thu | 20 min | Repurpose: core script → email plus 1 text post or carousel |
| Fri | 15 min | Scoreboard: log metrics, flag any 2x winners, note the next domino |

**"Growth Machine": 5–8 hours a week.** Output is 1 long-form pillar, 5–7 shorts, 2 carousels or text posts, 1 newsletter and daily engagement.

| Block | Time | Task |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | 90 min | Research and ideation: audience language, top competitor posts, FAQ bank; set the week's series slot |
| Tue | 2 h | Script the pillar (outline, not word-for-word) plus 7 shorts plus 2 carousels |
| Wed | 2 h | Batch-record the pillar and the shorts |
| Thu | 60 min | Repurpose (Framework F); write the newsletter; update the "Start Here" path |
| Daily | 15 min | Reply to comments and DMs; leave thoughtful comments on 5–10 niche peers (PRACTITIONER) |
| Fri | 30 min | Scoreboard and More/Better/New decisions for next week |

**Monthly, 1 hour:** pick next month's 30-day blueprint, refresh the signature beliefs, and retire any formats that have underperformed for 4 weeks.

## 3) What this means for the Content Machine design

1. **Make the series the basic unit, not the single post.** The planner should always produce a Domino Series Plan (7, 14 or 30 days). Each row should carry:
   - belief #
   - awareness stage
   - type (Edu/Ent/Conv)
   - job (reach, trust or convert)
   - hook
   - takeaway
   - link-forward line
   - a "repeat-of" field to track the 3 exposures
2. **Fill in the belief ladder before any scripting.** One onboarding prompt should extract the offer, the 7 beliefs and 3–5 signature beliefs. Every script prompt should then reference one belief. This connects reach content to sales.
3. **Build in a repetition engine.** The planner automatically schedules each signature belief at least 3 times per 30 days, in different formats and spread across weeks. The basis is Krugman, the spacing effect, the illusory-truth effect, and the satiation limit on mere exposure.
4. **Ship a library of 2–3 named recurring formats** (Framework C). Users choose names that become recognisable brand assets, which fits the 95-5 memory argument.
5. **Set default content mixes by phase,** which users can change:
   - Audience-building: 40 Ent / 45 Edu / 15 Conv
   - Steady state: 30 / 50 / 20
   - Launch week: 20 / 30 / 50
   - These defaults are a recommendation based on the give:ask principle, not on data.
6. **Set a cadence floor.** 3 posts a week on **one** main platform plus 1 email a week. Expand only after 8 consistent weeks. Use the 2–3 hour and 5–8 hour templates as onboarding choices.
7. **Include a pillar module.** One weekly long-form piece feeds micro-content and builds the bingeable library. Show a "trust hours" counter: total minutes of bingeable content, aiming for 7 hours or more.
8. **Make "Start Here" and assignment selling deliverables.** The pack should output a curated binge path, a pre-call email template and an intake question ("What did you watch before booking?").
9. **Write converting content against a checklist.** Every converting script must name at least 2 Messy Middle biases and close a specific belief. A direct-response variant adds offer, proof, guarantee and deadline.
10. **Handle cliffhangers responsibly.** Use open loops only when the next part is linked or pinned. Never use bait with no follow-through.
11. **Include a scoreboard and a weekly 15-minute review prompt.** Track metrics per type (table G), apply the 2x-median rule, and make More/Better/New decisions. Judge results over 90 days and say so to users, so those who "posted a lot with no results" do not quit at week 3.
12. **Suggested pack files:**
    - `systemize/domino-series-planner.md`
    - `systemize/series-blueprints-7-14-30.md`
    - `systemize/weekly-cadence-2h-vs-6h.md`
    - `systemize/repurpose-pillar-to-micro.md`
    - `systemize/scoreboard-and-double-down.md`
    - `systemize/start-here-binge-path.md`
    - plus a `beliefs-ladder.md` worksheet used by both the ideation and scripting files
