# Content Machine 1.0: Entertainment and relatable short-form for coaches, consultants and service businesses

## 1) Key insights

1. **The platforms reward sends and watch time. Entertainment is the best way to earn sends.**
   - DATA: Adam Mosseri, who runs Instagram, said in January 2025: "The top three signals that matter most for ranking are watch time, likes and sends… Likes are slightly more important for connected content, and sends are slightly more important for unconnected content." "Sends" are DM shares. "Unconnected" means people who don't follow you yet, which is where reach comes from.
   - DATA: Meta's published list of Reels ranking predictions includes how likely you are to "watch less than three seconds," to "watch more of a reel than 95% of users who watched reels of the same length," to reshare, to share off Instagram, to follow the author, and to use the reel's audio.
   - What it means: the first 3 seconds are scored directly. Length is judged against reels of the same length, so a short reel isn't penalised for being short.
   - DATA: TikTok says the For You feed uses likes, shares, comments and "video information (captions, sounds, hashtags)," and that follower count is not a direct factor. A new account can reach new people.

2. **People share mainly to say who they are and to stay in touch.**
   - DATA: In the New York Times "Psychology of Sharing" study (2,500 sharers), 68% share to show others who they are, 78% share to stay connected, and 94% think about whether the item is useful to the person receiving it.
   - Two jobs follow from this. A "this is so me" piece gets reposted as identity. A "this is so you" piece gets sent to a friend.

3. **Content that raises the heart rate gets shared. Content that lowers it does not.**
   - DATA: Berger and Milkman (2012) studied New York Times articles and found that awe, anger and anxiety drove sharing, and amusement did too in Berger's later work. Sadness and contentment reduced sharing.
   - Rule: relatable-struggle content should land on amusement or on frustration the viewer feels is justified. It should not land on sadness.

4. **Humour comes from something going wrong that still feels safe.**
   - DATA: McGraw and Warren's "benign violation" theory (2010) says something is funny when it breaks a norm and still feels harmless. Distance, such as a fictional scene, past events or a non-real person, makes the break feel harmless.
   - Rule: aim the joke at the problem, the industry, or your own past self. Never aim it at an identifiable client.

5. **Mistakes only make you likeable after people believe you are competent.**
   - DATA: In Aronson's 1966 study, a highly competent person who spilled coffee was rated the most likeable of four candidates. An average person who made the same blunder was rated the least likeable.
   - Rule: self-deprecating formats such as "expectation vs reality" or "my first offer vs now" only help after your feed shows competence. Pin three authority or proof posts first.

6. **Nostalgia makes people loosen their grip on money.**
   - DATA: Lasaleta, Sedikides and Vohs (2014) ran six experiments. People made to feel nostalgic were willing to pay more and valued money less, because nostalgia made them feel socially connected.
   - DATA: Autobiographical memories cluster around ages 10–30 (Rubin's "reminiscence bump").
   - Rule: aim nostalgia at the years when your ideal client was 10–30.

7. **For services people think hard about buying, a sense of familiarity is not enough. Expertise has to show.**
   - DATA (directional): Studies of "parasocial" bonds (the one-sided feeling of knowing a creator) find they raise trust and purchase intention. The effect is weaker for high-involvement purchases, where people weigh expertise more.
   - Coaching is a high-involvement purchase. Every entertainment piece therefore needs a small, specific detail that only an expert would know. This supports the founder's "dominoes" belief: entertainment opens the door, and education and proof close the sale.

8. **Broad entertainment brings in the wrong audience.**
   - PRACTITIONER (self-reported numbers): Alex Hormozi says entertainment and education audiences "rarely cross over." After moving to narrower educational content he reports RPM (ad revenue per 1,000 views) up 68%, book sales doubled, and opt-ins up 26%, even with fewer views. Another summary of his talks reports that when his team leaned into "edutainment," views rose 2–3x while revenue per view fell by about half.
   - PRACTITIONER (Paddy Galloway, YouTube consultant): from 5,400 YouTube Shorts with 3.3B views in 2023, "Specificity is king."

9. **The opening has to land in under 3 seconds.**
   - DATA (TikTok, from ads): 63% of the highest click-through ads state the key message within 3 seconds. 90% of the ad-recall effect happens within 6 seconds. 40% of the ads people watch through most use text overlays, and a third of them break the fourth wall (speak straight to the viewer).
   - DATA (TikTok and Kantar): 88% of TikTok users say sound is essential. Faster music (above 120 BPM) watches through better.
   - DATA: 77% of users like it when brands use trends.
   - These figures come from ads. Apply them to organic posts by analogy.

10. **Authentic, low-budget video is a strength, not a weakness.**
    - DATA: TikTok's 2026 trend report calls this "Reali-TEA": people want unfiltered stories and behind-the-scenes over polish. 81% say TikTok shows them real-life product use.
    - DATA: TikTok's 2025 report says 50% of users look for content that mirrors their own lives.
    - DATA: TikTok's research in Asia-Pacific found 3 in 4 people skip content that feels over-produced.

11. **Reposting someone else's video is penalised. Commentary is required.**
    - DATA: Since April 2024, Instagram removes accounts that repost content they didn't create from recommendations if they do it 10 or more times in 30 days. A watermark, a crop, or a credit in the caption doesn't count as original. Commentary or a reinterpretation does.
    - This matters most for green-screen and stitch formats.

## 2) Frameworks and templates

**A. Interrupt Theory and the Triple Hook (Kallaway)**
- Source: PRACTITIONER, 1M+ followers.
- How it works: the hook has to give a **Subject**, the **Stakes**, **Speed** (as few words as possible) and **Clarity** (only one possible reading).
- The text, spoken and visual hooks must all say the same thing. If they don't, "it will cause insane confusion."
- On-screen text hook rules:
  - two lines maximum
  - no punctuation except quote marks or brackets
  - specific numbers, so "$2,700" rather than "a few thousand"
- The **"lock-in zone"** is the 5–10 seconds after the hook. In it you confirm the claim and show credibility or proof.
- Template: TEXT `[Subject] + [pain/outcome]` · SPOKEN `one sentence ≤12 words` · VISUAL `the first frame is already moving or shows the subject`.

**B. Gold/Silver/Bronze format study (Brendan Kane)**
- Source: PRACTITIONER, author of *Hook Point*.
- Steps:
  1. Choose one format on one platform.
  2. Collect 10–20 examples with millions of views (gold), 10–20 with hundreds of thousands (silver), and 10–20 under 100K (bronze).
  3. List "what's happening in those high performers that's not happening in those low performers."
  4. Turn the differences into a template.
- Kane also says to make one element dominant on screen. That fits Kallaway's alignment rule: one element leads and the other two support it.

**C. The Jenny Hoyos Shorts spine**
- Source: PRACTITIONER, about 1B Shorts views.
- Structure: **Hook (visual first) → Foreshadow (2 lines, ≤3s) → Body (problem/solution or "but/therefore" storytelling) → Final line written before filming.**
- Her rules:
  - Write at a 5th-grade reading level.
  - Every second counts. Cutting one weak final second raised her retention from 83% to 88%.
- Length by platform:
  - TikTok preferred dense 10–20s videos.
  - Shorts preferred 34s story videos.
  - Reels needed burned-in subtitles because Reels autoplay muted.
- Loops: her average retention (95%) is higher than her scroll-through rate (85%), which means people rewatch.
- Loop template: the last line has to finish the first line. For example, the video ends "…which is why I never—" and the first frame is "Never discount your first client."

**D. Length benchmarks**
- DATA-ish (Paddy Galloway, 2023): Shorts where viewers stayed more than 50 seconds averaged 4.1M views. 50–60s was the strongest length band, but only when retention held.
- DATA: Reels and Shorts now allow up to 3 minutes. Mosseri says Instagram counts absolute seconds watched, so a long reel isn't penalised.
- Recommended defaults:
  - Relatable or IYKYK pieces: 6–15s, built to loop.
  - Skits and "types of": 15–35s.
  - Storytime: 35–60s.
- Pacing: about 2.5 spoken words per second (PRACTITIONER), with a visual change every 2–4 seconds.

**E. The relatability kernel (synthesis)**
- Source: Corporate Natalie's working method (PRACTITIONER). Natalie Marshall is an ex-Deloitte consultant who built a six-figure business on workplace skits. Her skits "all contain a kernel of truth," and she keeps a "running notes page" of real moments.
- Formula: **Broad format + niche-only moment + one expert twist.**
- Fill-in: `[FORMAT] about [moment only someone with my client's problem has lived] + [one line or detail only an expert would include]`.

**F. Buyer Filter Test (rule built from insights 7–8)**
Every entertainment piece has to pass three checks:
1. **Who sends it, and to whom?** It must be your ideal client sending it to a peer, partner or co-founder.
2. **Do you need the problem I solve to get the joke?** Rate filter strength 1–3 and require 2 or more.
3. **What is the next domino?** Name the educational or proof piece it links to.

### Format catalog (22 formats)

Each entry has four parts:
- **Text:** the on-screen text
- **Shots:** the shot list
- **Cap:** the post caption
- **Ex:** a coach or consultant example

1. **POV.** Puts the viewer in the role, which drives identity recognition. The format has been on TikTok since 2019.
   - **Text:** "POV: you're a [ideal client] and [specific moment]".
   - **Shots:** (1) look into the lens and react, 2s; (2) the situation gets worse; (3) a silent payoff face.
   - **Cap:** "[keyword phrase]. Send to the [role] who's done this."
   - **Ex (pricing coach):** "POV: you finally raised your prices and the client says yes without blinking." Silent panic, then celebration.

2. **If you know, you know (IYKYK).** Insider in-joke. Outsiders not getting it is how it filters buyers.
   - **Text:** "[niche-specific detail] 🫠 iykyk".
   - **Shots:** one static shot of a screen or object, with trending audio, 6–8s, built to loop.
   - **Cap:** "If you know, you know."
   - **Ex (agency consultant):** a Slack message reading "quick question?" from a client at 11:58pm.

3. **Things nobody tells you about [X].** Curiosity gap plus insider knowledge people want to pass on. Sits halfway to educational.
   - **Text:** "Things nobody tells you about [hiring/becoming/doing X]".
   - **Shots:** 3–5 numbered truths over B-roll of your real day. Put the sharpest one last.
   - **Cap:** "#3 hit different. Save for when you [moment]."
   - **Ex (career coach):** "…about quitting corporate to coach: you'll miss the free coffee more than the salary."

4. **Me explaining X to Y.** Expert-versus-novice contrast. Shows expertise through comedy.
   - **Text:** "Me explaining [your job/concept] to [person who doesn't get it]".
   - **Shots:** a whiteboard or green screen with diagrams getting more chaotic; cut to Y's blank stare (you in a hat).
   - **Cap:** "They still think I [misconception]."
   - **Ex (fractional CFO):** explaining the role to parents, which ends with "so… you do taxes?"

5. **Types of clients / Types of [ideal client].** Sorting people into types, so viewers identify themselves and tag friends.
   - **Text:** "Types of [founders] on a first [coaching call]".
   - **Shots:** 4–6 characters at 3–5s each, with costume or prop swaps in the same framing. The last one is "my favourite to work with," which describes the ideal client.
   - **Cap:** "Which one are you? Be honest."
   - **Ex (exec coach):** the "I'm fine" one, the 40-slide-deck one, the one who actually does the homework.

6. **Day in the life.** Behind-the-scenes curiosity, with your competence visible in the background.
   - **Text:** "Day in the life of a [niche] coach who [differentiator]".
   - **Shots:** 8–12 clips of 1–2s each: alarm, coffee, a blurred client call, whiteboard, a win notification, shutdown.
   - **Cap:** "What surprised you?"
   - **Ex:** a sales consultant for plumbing companies on a ride-along.

7. **Expectation vs reality.** Something goes wrong but it's safe to laugh at, and the creator is humanised by the slip.
   - **Text:** "Expectation: [the fantasy]" / "Reality: [the messy truth]".
   - **Shots:** a polished staged clip, then a raw clip.
   - **Cap:** "The fix: [one-line lesson] (full breakdown pinned)."
   - **Ex:** "Launching my course. Reality: 3 sales, 2 were my aunts." This leads into a post on pre-selling.

8. **Green-screen reaction.** Borrows someone else's context and adds your commentary, which Instagram's originality rules require.
   - **Text:** "Reacting to [type] advice for [ideal client]".
   - **Shots:** a screenshot of a Reddit thread or headline behind you; point, react, then give 2–3 lines of your take.
   - **Cap:** "Agree or disagree?"
   - **Ex (marketing consultant):** "Spent $10k on an agency, got 2 leads," then "the 3 questions they should've asked."

9. **Two-character skit.** Acts out a recurring conflict built on a kernel of truth.
   - **Text:** "When the client says '[real quote]'".
   - **Shots:** two positions in the frame with outfit swaps, 4–8 lines, and the punch line last.
   - **Cap:** "Based on real events (names changed)."
   - **Ex (brand consultant):** "Can you make the logo pop?"

10. **Trend adaptation.** Viewers already know the template, so it takes a familiar shape with new content. It also feeds the "use this audio" ranking signal.
    - **Text:** map the meaning of the trend onto a moment from your niche.
    - **Shots:** copy the trend's beats exactly.
    - **Cap:** niche keyword.
    - **Ex (fitness coach for dads):** "It's the 'I'll start Monday' for me."

11. **Nostalgia trigger.** Social connectedness and a higher willingness to pay (Lasaleta 2014).
    - **Text:** "If you remember [era detail], you're now [current pain]".
    - **Shots:** an old object, photo or sound, then a cut to today.
    - **Cap:** "Tell me your first [X]."
    - **Ex (money coach for 40-year-olds):** "$20 used to fill the tank AND buy a CD…" This bridges into a budgeting post.

12. **Storytime (multi-part).** Story pull plus a cliffhanger, which is how you build a series.
    - **Text:** "I told a client to [shocking move] (pt 1)".
    - **Shots:** one-take talking head with jump cuts every 2–4s and receipts on screen. Structure: Result → Foreshadow → But → Therefore → Twist → Lesson.
    - **Cap:** "Part 2 tomorrow."
    - **Ex (sales consultant):** "I told a client to fire their best salesperson."

13. **Unpopular opinion.** High-arousal disagreement that stakes out your position and triggers comments.
    - **Text:** "Unpopular opinion: [the belief your method is built on]".
    - **Shots:** a single take, 15–30s, giving 3 reasons.
    - **Cap:** "Fight me in the comments."
    - **Ex:** "You don't need a niche. You need a problem you've solved 10 times."

14. **Nobody / Me.** Viewers see themselves in it and repost it as "this is so me."
    - **Text:** "Nobody: / Me at 2am: [ideal-client behaviour]".
    - **Shots:** one deadpan silent clip, 5–8s, built to loop.
    - **Cap:** "Send to someone who does this."
    - **Ex:** "Me reorganising Notion instead of sending the invoice."

15. **Signs you're a [type].** A diagnostic checklist that drives "this is you" sends.
    - **Text:** "Signs you're a [doer] who got promoted to manager".
    - **Shots:** 4–6 one-second gags or text cards.
    - **Cap:** "How many did you get?"
    - **Ex (leadership coach):** "You rewrite your team's emails at 10pm."

16. **Things I'll never do as a [role].** States your values and filters clients. Close to converting content.
    - **Text:** "Things I'll never do as a [consultant] (and why)".
    - **Shots:** talking head with numbered text.
    - **Cap:** "Which one surprised you?"
    - **Ex (agency):** "Lock you into a 12-month contract."

17. **My first [X] vs now.** A progress story with a self-deprecating slip. Post only after competence is established.
    - **Text:** "My first [sales page/offer/price] vs now".
    - **Shots:** an old screenshot, then the current one.
    - **Cap:** "Year-1 me would never."
    - **Ex:** "$97 'life coaching' vs a $6k programme."

18. **Things my clients say (week 1 vs week 12).** Real quotes that viewers recognise, with the transformation built in.
    - **Text:** "Week 1: '[objection]' / Week 12: '[result quote]'".
    - **Shots:** act out each line, or put the text over B-roll.
    - **Cap:** "Week 12 is why I do this."
    - **Ex (fitness coach):** "I don't have time" becomes "I moved meetings for my workout."

19. **Asking [ideal client] X (interview).** Real voices act as social proof. TikTok flags street interviews as trust builders.
    - **Text:** "Asking freelancers what they charge per hour".
    - **Shots:** short interviews at a co-working space or event, or on Zoom, then your reaction.
    - **Cap:** "What do you charge?"
    - **Ex:** a pricing consultant.

20. **Which one are you? (A/B/C).** Viewers sort themselves and comment.
    - **Text:** "Which [salesperson] are you when they say 'let me think about it'?"
    - **Shots:** 3 quick character takes.
    - **Cap:** "A, B or C?"
    - **Ex:** a sales coach.

21. **Stitch and fix.** Duet or stitch someone else's mistake, then show the fix on screen. A competence demo in an entertainment wrapper.
    - **Text:** "Let me fix this [cold DM/LinkedIn post]".
    - **Shots:** the original clip, then a live rewrite.
    - **Cap:** "Before/after in comments."
    - **Ex:** a copywriting consultant.

22. **The face when.** A micro-expression with zero script. The text carries the joke and the clip loops.
    - **Text:** "The face I make when a prospect says 'I need to ask my spouse'".
    - **Shots:** 1 shot, 4–6s.
    - **Cap:** "Handle it like this → [link to post]."
    - **Ex:** a sales coach.

**Captions (all formats)**
- **On-screen subtitles:** always burn them in.
- **First line of the post caption:** a searchable keyword phrase. TikTok and Instagram both use caption text to understand and surface videos. Mosseri says hashtags no longer increase reach, and Instagram now caps posts at 5 hashtags.
- **Second line:** extend the joke.
- **Third line:** a send prompt that names who should receive it, e.g. "Send to your co-founder."
  - Mosseri advises making content people want to send rather than pushing sharing artificially. So the recipient should be built into the concept, not added as a request.
- **Pinned comment:** link to the next domino.

## 3) What this means for the Content Machine design

1. **Research module: build a "Recognition Bank."** Claude interviews the user and mines sales-call notes, DMs, reviews, ideal-client Reddit or forum threads, and the founder's own "before" life. The goal is 50 moments, each tagged with:
   - Buying stage: Unaware pain / Trying & failing / Considering help / Working together / After
   - Emotion: amusement, frustration, anxiety, awe, nostalgia
   - Filter strength (1–3)
   - Nostalgia era: the years the client was 10–30
   - This replaces "I don't know what to say."

2. **Add a Format Study exercise.** Use Kane's gold/silver/bronze method. The user pastes links or transcripts of 10 top and 10 weak videos in their niche, and Claude outputs a format card for that niche.

3. **Ideation engine: Moments × Formats.** 50 moments × 22 formats gives more than 1,000 candidate ideas. Every candidate is scored and filtered automatically:
   - It must pass the Buyer Filter Test.
   - It must pass the "competence-first" rule: block pratfall formats (#7, #17) until the user confirms 3 pinned proof or authority posts exist.
   - It must pass the safe-humour check: never name or mock an identifiable client.

4. **Scripting: a one-page Short-Form Card.** Every script fills these fields:
   - Text hook (≤2 lines)
   - Spoken hook (≤12 words)
   - First-frame visual
   - Lock-in line (seconds 3–10)
   - Beats (but/therefore)
   - Final line, written first
   - Loop line
   - Caption (3 lines)
   - Pinned-comment link to the next domino
   - Production tier
   - Target length and word count at 2.5 words per second
   - Reading level at grade 5 or below
   - Claude checks that the three hooks say the same thing before it finalises.

5. **Production tiers for non-actors.** Let the user pick a comfort level, and have the generator only output formats at that tier or below:
   - **T0, no face:** B-roll, text and trending audio. Formats #2, #14, #10.
   - **T1, face, no talking:** reactions or lip-sync. Formats #1, #22.
   - **T2, talking head:** green screen or storytime. Formats #8, #12, #13.
   - **T3, multi-character skits:** formats #5, #9.

6. **Systemize: the domino series.** Entertainment is the first domino, never a standalone post. The default weekly series is:
   - Relatable (they get me)
   - Educational (here's why it happens and the fix)
   - Story or proof (client storytime, #12 or #18)
   - Converting (the offer)
   - Recommended mix: about 30–40% entertainment. This is a recommendation based on Hormozi's experience, not a measured figure.

7. **Metrics per content type**, so users stop judging everything by views:
   - **Entertainment:** sends per reach, share of reach from non-followers, and follows per 1,000 views.
   - **Educational:** saves.
   - **Converting:** DMs and clicks.
   - **Audience-quality check:** if views spike but profile visits and DMs from ideal clients don't rise, raise the filter-strength threshold.
   - **Trial Reels:** use them for entertainment experiments. They show the reel to non-followers first, so a test doesn't touch the user's main feed.

8. **Platform defaults the pack should hard-code:**
   - Vertical 9:16 framing.
   - Key message within 3 seconds.
   - Burned-in subtitles plus spoken audio.
   - Music above 120 BPM for B-roll formats.
   - Loopable endings for anything under 15 seconds.
   - Real commentary on green-screen and stitch pieces.
   - Keyword-first captions with at most 5 hashtags.
   - One thing to check: business accounts are limited to licensed commercial music. I didn't confirm that in this research.

**Caveats:**
- The TikTok creative percentages come from ad studies and are applied to organic content by analogy.
- Hormozi's and Hoyos's numbers are self-reported.
- Several Mosseri quotes come through secondary write-ups, not his original posts.
- The research hit the session's web-search limit before I could look up a "viewed vs swiped away" benchmark for Shorts, so I couldn't add one.
