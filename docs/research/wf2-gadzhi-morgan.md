## 1. Identity and business model
- **Who he is:** Iman Gadzhi, born in Dagestan, Russia in 2000 and raised in London. He is an online-business educator. He first sold courses on running a social-media marketing agency, then moved into broader "make money online", mindset and lifestyle content.
- **Platforms:** about 6.0M YouTube subscribers (478 videos), plus a second channel, "Iman Gadzhi Business". He also has about 3M on Instagram, about 4M on TikTok and about 800K on X.
- **What he sells:**
  - Educate / Digital Launchpad, a subscription course library at $37.99 a month or $269.99 a year.
  - Agency Navigator / Agency Accelerator, his agency course.
  - Monetise, a low-ticket bundle sold with deadline pricing. It includes "Ask Iman", an AI clone of him.
  - The 8-Figure Personal Brand Challenge, a 5-day live event for $97.
  - The Eight Figure License, a private program for business owners making $250K+ a year, sold on a call.
  - Other businesses: Flozy (agency software), Hills eyewear, Big Day (electrolyte drinks), co-ownership of Whop, and Consulting.com, which he bought in January 2026.
- **Audiences:**
  - Mass audience: men aged 15–30.
  - Newer target: business owners making $100K–$250K+ a year, served by the "Business" channel and the licensing program.
- **Caveat:** his revenue claims are disputed. Records show his first agency company was dissolved after 9 months, and critics call his courses thin and his marketing predatory toward young people.

## 2. Content philosophy
- **"Creating content by itself does not generate millions."** You have to plan the viewer's path to purchase.
- **Profit per follower, not vanity metrics.** He cites a creator with 4K subscribers earning multiple five figures a month, and a micro-channel of about 20K subscribers that he says can make about $400K a month.
- **"Creators work for YouTube; entrepreneurs make YouTube work for them."** His test: "If a view can't lead to a sale, you're thinking like a creator."
- **The goal is wealth, not fame.** He organises his brand around three pillars: Authenticity, Impact and Intention.
- **Relatability beats expertise.** You don't need to be an expert to give useful insight.
- **"Negative cost of acquisition."** Content should earn money (ad revenue) while also bringing in buyers.
- **YouTube is the "legacy" platform.** Short-form is "dopamine" and fades quickly. People who watch long-form convert better because they trust you more.
- **Consistency:** 439 videos over 9 years. Confidence comes from competence, and competence comes from volume.
- **The 7-11-4 rule** (he cites it; it comes from Google's research on how buyers decide). A buyer needs about 7 hours of your content, across 11 touchpoints, in 4 different places, before they purchase. This is the closest match to your "dominoes" belief.

## 3. His content system
- **3:1 ratio of Growth to Nurture content.**
  - Growth content is broad and curiosity-led, for example "AI tools that make you rich".
  - Nurture content is personal story and day-in-the-life material that deepens the relationship.
- **Two layers of content:**
  - "Superficial" content builds trust and connection through stories.
  - "Depth" content educates and makes money directly.
- **Four content formats:**

  | Format | What it does |
  |---|---|
  | Actionable | Steps the viewer can follow |
  | Narrative | Personal stories that show vulnerability |
  | Theoretical | Explains a concept or framework |
  | Attractor | Shows results and transformations |

- **Listicles as a bridge.** List videos ("7 life lessons…", "3 books…") move short-form viewers into long-form. This comes from a third-party breakdown.
- **Short-form distribution (his own claims):**
  - A 20-person team made 7,000 pieces in 2022. Later he cites a 65-person clipping team.
  - He now runs 30+ accounts producing 100–300M views a month.
  - Accounts are split by content type: business clips for operators, behind-the-scenes and aspirational clips for younger viewers, and controversial takes for a wide reach. His reason: when one feed mixes clip types, viewers engage with one and drop off the next.
  - Every account's caption points back to the main account.
- **Funnel layers:**
  1. Short-form for awareness.
  2. Main accounts plus "Talk with Iman", an Instagram account that answers business questions and qualifies leads.
  3. A YouTube channel mixing growth and nurture videos.
  4. A video sales letter (VSL) and high-ticket call, with WhatsApp and sales-rep follow-up for anyone who doesn't buy.
- **Rehashing old videos.** He swaps titles and thumbnails on existing videos; one went from 52K to 2.5M views. He claims he "could go a year without posting".
- **Team under about $10K a month** for smaller creators: thumbnail designer, full-time editor (turns each long video into 30+ clips), and copywriter (turns raw ideas into scripts). He also lists a strategist role at about $35K a month.
- **His time:** 3–5 hours a month on camera; the team does everything else.
- **Launch series.** Every 3–6 months he releases a cinematic, Netflix-style free series (Digital Renaissance, The Reset, The Rescue), with trailers costing $100K+. He also runs 5-day live challenges. Each one ends with an offer and a deadline.

## 4. Signature frameworks, with coach templates

**a) The Flywheel VSL (he writes it "VSSL").** One cornerstone sales video that sells around the clock without ads. His example got 9.5M views and, he says, $20M in sales. You then make 15–30 feeder videos, and each one links to the cornerstone.
- Steps: pick the one problem your offer solves → make one cornerstone teaching video that leads to the offer → make 15–30 feeder videos that answer doubts and limiting beliefs → link every feeder to the cornerstone in the description and end screen → refresh the packaging over time.
- Template: *Cornerstone: "The [easiest / fastest] way for [audience] to [result] without [pain] (my exact system)". Feeders: "Why [common belief] is keeping you stuck at [state]", "[Objection]? Watch this", "How [client] went from [X] to [Y]". Every feeder ends with: "If you want the full system, watch [cornerstone] next."*

**b) The Report Card Method.** Grade several options against criteria that favour your offer, so your solution looks like the obvious choice without a hard sell. His example graded business models on startup cost, time to first sale, margin, control, scalability and location freedom.
- Template: *"I compared [4 ways to get result: A / B / C / your method] on [cost], [time to result], [effort], [risk], [sustainability], [fits a busy schedule]. Here's the scorecard…" Your method wins on the criteria your ideal client cares about most.*

**c) Two sales before the offer.** Before you pitch, the content has to (1) convince the viewer your type of solution is the best one, and (2) prove they personally can do it. For the second, break the first steps into tiny pieces, like "a domain and an email cost $5" or "choose a niche in 5 minutes".
- Template: *"Sale 1: why [your method] beats [their current approach]. Sale 2: proof that someone like you, starting at [their situation], can do it — step 1 takes [tiny time or cost]."*

**d) Big Idea → 3 points → takeaway.** His structure for long-form videos.
- Template: *"The big idea: [one belief shift]. Point 1: [what], with [sub-point or example]. Point 2… Point 3… Takeaway: [one action to take today]."*

**e) Packaging-first, with the 3-element thumbnail.** A title that qualifies the audience before they click, plus a thumbnail with (1) a genuine facial expression, (2) short readable text that complements the title rather than repeating it, and (3) an image that gives context.

## 5. Hook and script structures
- **Long-form blueprint** (12–25 minutes, from a third-party channel analysis): promise and proof → why this opportunity exists now → 3 options or 1 step-by-step method → tools and templates → call to action.
- **Hook style:** a mystery statement such as "YouTube has a secret second algorithm". The hook names the viewer's problem, never the product's features.
- **Title formula (mass audience):** an "unfairly easy" word ("Laziest", "Copy & Paste", "Beginner-friendly", "With $0") + an exact number ($373/day) + a trend + the year. A business-owner audience needs a different filter.
- **Calls to action (CTAs):**
  - A soft CTA within the first 90 seconds, when the most people are still watching.
  - A strong CTA at the end, for the most qualified viewers.
  - An end screen in the last 20 seconds pointing to the next video.
  - Description links that chain videos together so people watch several in a row.
- **Ad script for Monetise:** selfie video in a car → "If the only thing between you and [result] is the price…" → "I've just opened the most affordable offer I've ever made" → a stack of 10+ items → "only available until 11:59 PM today" → "click below".
- **Example outline for a coach:**
  *Title: "The Laziest Way for Busy Coaches to Book 10 Calls a Week (no ads)". 0–20 seconds: show the calendar as proof and make the promise. 20–60 seconds: why this works now. Soft CTA: "free template in the description". Body: a report card of 3 methods, then a 3-step walkthrough of yours. End: a strong CTA to book a call, then an end screen to the cornerstone video.*

## 6. How content turns into sales

## 7. What to take for Content Machine
