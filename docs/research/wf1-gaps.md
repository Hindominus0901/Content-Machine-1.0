# Gap brief: what the Content Machine research is still missing (5 Oct 2026)

**Method note:** this session's web-search budget was already used up when the task started. Everything below comes from fetching primary pages directly: FTC, the EU AI Act text, YouTube, Meta and Instagram help pages, abstracts retrieved through Semantic Scholar, Pew, Profound and SparkToro. A few items come from memory and are marked "not re-verified". The TikTok AI-label page and the TikTok For You feed eligibility page did not render, so TikTok policy is **not covered**.

**Labels:**
- **[DATA]** = a study, platform data, or a law or platform rule.
- **[PRACTITIONER]** = an expert's opinion or experience.
- **[DESIGN]** = our own inference for the pack.

## 1) Key insights

### G1. Sounding human

1. **Admitting you used AI costs trust, but being caught costs more.** [DATA]
   - Schilke and Reimann (2025) ran 13 experiments, published in *Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes*. People who disclosed AI use were trusted less. This held across different wordings of the disclosure, and whether the disclosure was voluntary or required.
   - Being exposed by a third party was worse than disclosing.
   - The penalty was smaller, but did not disappear, among people who like technology.
   - [DESIGN] So don't hide AI use. Instead, make the AI's real share small: the person supplies the stories, opinions and proof, and the AI does structure and editing.
2. **The word "AI" in an offer lowers purchase intent.** [DATA]
   - Cicek, Gursoy and Lu (2024) tested 1,000+ US adults across 8 product categories. Descriptions that mentioned "artificial intelligence" lowered purchase intent.
   - The effect was strongest for high-risk purchases such as financial and medical ones.
   - Coaching is a high-risk purchase. Keep "AI-powered" out of offer copy.
3. **People can't reliably spot AI text, so getting past detection isn't the real goal.** [DATA]
   - Jakesch, Hancock and Naaman (*PNAS*) found people use flawed cues. From the paper body (not re-verified): they read first-person pronouns, contractions and family topics as signs of a human writer.
   - [DESIGN] The real risk is *generic* text that nobody can attribute to this person. Platforms now push that kind of content down (next point).
4. **Platforms now penalise templated, mass-produced output.** [DATA]
   - YouTube renamed "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content" on **15 July 2025**. Monetised content must not be "mass-produced, generic, repetitive". The policy names "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates… without adding the creator's original, authentic insights or perspective."
   - Series are fine on YouTube if "each video has a distinct storyline, focus, or concept."
   - Instagram demotes Reels that are watermarked, "majority text", or "already posted on Instagram."
   - A template-driven machine is exactly what these rules target. The fix is enforced variation plus at least one original insight in every piece.
5. **Write in spoken language.** [PRACTITIONER] Paul Graham: "If you simply manage to write in spoken language, you'll be ahead of 95% of writers." His test: would you say this sentence to a friend? Then read the whole thing aloud.

### G2. Delivery on camera

6. **Viewers judge competence from a few seconds of body language.** [DATA]
   - Ambady and Rosenthal (1993): strangers rated teachers from silent 2-, 5- and 10-second clips. Their ratings closely matched the ratings of students who had sat through a whole semester, and longer clips did not improve accuracy.
   - Cues for good ratings: smiling and moving. Cues for poor ratings: frowning, fidgeting and looking down.
7. **Bad audio makes the speaker seem less credible.** [DATA, from memory of the paper; abstract not retrieved] Newman and Schwarz (2018, *Science Communication*): identical research talks were rated as weaker research, by less competent researchers, when the audio quality was poor. So buy a microphone before a better camera.
8. **Instagram's own 2026 advice for beginners is to start short and simple.** [DATA]
   - "If you're just getting started, try a 15-second reel."
   - Length bands: 3–15 seconds for one-liners, 15–60 seconds for tutorials, 60 seconds to 3 minutes for storytelling.
   - "A strong opening line of text in the first frame can be just as powerful as a great thumbnail."
   - Instagram's July 2026 editing guide: "Your first video edit doesn't need to be perfect. It just needs to exist."

### G3. Guardrails

9. **The FTC's earnings-claim rule for business coaching is still a proposal.** [DATA]
   - In January 2025 the FTC proposed extending the Business Opportunity Rule to business coaching. Sellers would need "written substantiation" for any earnings claim and would have to show it to consumers on request.
   - As of October 2026, the FTC's rule page shows no final rule.
   - [DESIGN] Behave as if it were already in force. The FTC Act already bans deceptive claims.
10. **The FTC has already gone after coaching schemes built on income claims plus AI hype.** [DATA] In "Operation AI Comply" (September 2024), courts issued temporary restraining orders against three schemes that sold online-business coaching or done-for-you stores with "AI-powered" income promises:
    - Ascend Ecom: at least $25M taken from consumers.
    - Ecommerce Empire Builders: ads promising "$10,000 monthly".
    - FBA Machine: $15.9M.
11. **Health claims need scientific evidence, and testimonials don't count.** [DATA] The FTC's health guidance requires "competent and reliable scientific evidence" for health claims. On testimonials: "It's not enough that a testimonial represents the honest opinion or experience of an endorser." This applies to fitness, nutrition and wellness coaches.
12. **EU AI Act, Article 50, applies from 2 August 2026.** [DATA]
    - Anyone publishing deepfakes (AI images, audio or video of real people) must disclose them. A professional coach counts as a "deployer".
    - AI-generated text that informs the public on matters of public interest must also be disclosed, **unless** it has "undergone a process of human review or editorial control" and a person holds editorial responsibility.
    - What this means for coaches:
      - A script the coach reviewed and published needs no label under this rule.
      - An AI avatar or voice clone of the coach shown to EU viewers does need one.
    - Not checked: whether the EU's Digital Omnibus changes this.
13. **The platforms label AI too. Using AI to help write does not need a label.** [DATA]
    - **YouTube needs no disclosure for** AI used for the "outline, script, thumbnail, title", "idea generation", "caption creation", or even "cloning one's own voice to create voice overs or dubs".
    - **YouTube does need disclosure** when content makes a real person "appear to say or do something they didn't do", or shows a realistic event that didn't happen.
    - **Meta** applies an "AI info" label when it detects industry-standard AI signals or when the user self-discloses. Since September 2024, content that was only *edited* with AI shows the label in the post menu instead.

### G4–G5. Cold start and onboarding

14. **Positioning should start from what clients would otherwise do, not from your skills.** [PRACTITIONER] April Dunford's order is:
    1. Competitive alternatives
    2. Unique attributes
    3. Value
    4. Best-fit customers
    5. Market category

    Her reason for starting with alternatives: otherwise you get "positioning that sounded good in the office, but it didn't work with customers because it wasn't differentiated." For a coach, the alternatives are DIY, YouTube, books, hiring an employee, or doing nothing.
15. **A chatbot interview beats a form.** [DATA] Xiao et al. (*ACM TOCHI*) ran a field study with about 600 people and 5,200 answers. A chatbot that asked follow-up questions drove "significantly higher" engagement than a Qualtrics form, and answers that were more informative, relevant, specific and clear.
16. **Long intakes produce thin answers.** [DATA] SurveyMonkey's analysis of about 100,000 surveys:
    - Time spent per question falls from 75 seconds on question 1 to 30–40 seconds on questions 2–10, and about 19 seconds on questions 26–30.
    - Abandonment rises once a survey passes 7–8 minutes, with completion dropping by 5% to 20%.
    - The 60+ questions the briefs propose would come back shallow.

### G6. Findability

17. **AI answers draw heavily on Reddit, YouTube and LinkedIn.** [DATA, vendor] Profound analysed 680M AI citations from August 2024 to June 2025.

    | AI product | Top cited sources |
    |---|---|
    | ChatGPT | Wikipedia 7.8%, Reddit 1.8% |
    | Google AI Overviews | Reddit 2.2%, YouTube 1.9%, LinkedIn 1.3% |
    | Perplexity | Reddit 6.6%, YouTube 2.0%, LinkedIn 0.8% |

    [DESIGN] YouTube and LinkedIn are the channels a coach controls that AI answers actually cite.
18. **Google says no special AI optimisation is needed.** [DATA] Google (updated 10 December 2025): "There are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, nor other special optimizations necessary." What matters is indexable text and normal SEO.
19. **Being recommended by AI drives direct visits, at least for big brands.** [DATA, vendor] Similarweb data, reported by SparkToro (June 2026): brands that AI recommended got more direct visits (+7.2% and +14.2% in two cases) and more branded searches. Only large brands were studied.
20. **Your website is what AI tools learn about you from.** [PRACTITIONER] Rand Fishkin (August 2026) calls the website "the only place to give your content efforts a permanent home." He warns that AI tools repeat whatever stale information they find about you.
    - Reported but not re-verified: since July 2025, public Instagram posts from professional accounts can be indexed by Google.

### G7. Measurement

21. **Click attribution is breaking down.** [PRACTITIONER] Rand Fishkin (July 2024): platforms penalise outbound links, and traffic increasingly arrives with no source attached. His advice is "lift-based measurement and not attribution." For a coach, that means asking people how they found you and watching the trend in inbound leads over time.
22. **Raw Shorts views are inflated.** [DATA, not re-verified] Since March 2025, YouTube counts a Shorts view on every play and replay. Judge Shorts on "viewed vs. swiped away" and on engaged views instead.

### G8. Platform choice

23. **US reach by platform.** [DATA] Pew (February–June 2025): YouTube 84%, Facebook 71% and Instagram 50% of US adults. Other figures from this page extracted inconsistently, so they are omitted.

## 2) Frameworks & templates

### T1. Say-It-Out-Loud pass (mandatory last step of every script) [DESIGN, built on G1 sources]

```
HUMANIZE — run in order, fix, re-run:
1 SPECIFICS: every 2–3 sentences contain ≥1 of: number, (anonymised) name, time/place,
  verbatim client quote, sensory detail — pulled from Story/Proof/VOC banks.
  None available → ask me. Never invent. Mark gaps [NEEDS DETAIL].
2 SPOKEN: contractions; avg sentence ≤15 words, vary length; no semicolons;
  only words in my Voice Card; Graham test: "would I say this to a friend?"
3 STRIP TELLS: banned words; max ONE "it's not X, it's Y"; no reflexive triplets;
  no "Let's dive in / Here's the thing / In today's fast-paced world";
  no summary-recap ending; no em dashes in spoken lines.
4 POV: one sentence I believe that a competitor wouldn't say (Belief Bank ID).
5 VARIATION GUARD: compare with last 10 rows of CONTENT-LOG; if same hook stem or
  same structure 3× in a row → change one (YouTube "inauthentic content" risk;
  mere-exposure wear-out).
6 READ-ALOUD: I read it once aloud; any line I stumble on → I voice-note what I'd
  actually say; you rewrite to match.
```

**Worked example (Linh, a nutrition coach for mothers aged 35–50):**
- *AI draft:* "In today's fast-paced world, busy moms struggle to prioritize their health. It's not about willpower, it's about systems. Let's dive into three game-changing strategies."
- *After the pass:* "I used to eat my kids' leftover toast standing at the sink and call it breakfast. Most of my clients did too. What fixed it: two boiled eggs in the fridge every Sunday night."

### T2. Delivery modes plus the Shot Card [DESIGN, informed by G2 data]

The user picks one mode at onboarding, and every script is output in that mode.

| Mode | For whom | What the script looks like |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Interview** (least anxiety) | Camera-shy users | 5 questions that a friend asks off-camera, or that the user reads off a phone. The user answers naturally, then cuts the answers together |
| **B. Beat cards** (default) | Most users | Hook and final line word for word (only 2 lines to memorise), plus 3–5 bullet beats. One beat per take, joined with jump cuts |
| **C. Teleprompter** | Ads and converting pieces that contain compliance lines | Word-for-word text in a teleprompter app placed near the lens. Rehearse twice and mark pauses |

**Shot Card (added to every script):**
```
HOOK (verbatim) | FIRST-FRAME ACTION (already moving; eyes on lens; expression) |
ON-SCREEN TEXT (≤6 words) | BEATS | PROOF PROP/SCREEN | FINAL LINE (verbatim) |
B-ROLL LIST | LENGTH TARGET (start ≤15–30s) | RETAKE NOTE
```

**Minimum kit, in this order:**
1. A clip-on or lav mic about a hand's width from the mouth (the audio-credibility data).
2. Face a window for light.
3. Phone rear camera at eye level, 9:16.
4. Quiet, soft-furnished room.
5. One prop from the niche in the background.

**First-frame checklist** (from the thin-slices findings):
- Eyes on the lens.
- Smiling or a clear expression.
- Already mid-gesture or walking.
- No "hey guys" opener.
- No looking down at notes.

**Batch session:** 60 minutes produces 4–6 shorts. Change your top between pieces. Keep the framing identical for a named recurring show.

**Confidence ladder:** maps to the entertainment brief's T0–T3 production tiers. The first 10 videos are practice: count them as done when they're posted, and ignore their views.

### T3. Claims and compliance linter: red, amber, green [DESIGN, built on G3 sources]

**RED — refuse or rewrite:**
- Invented testimonials, results, quotes or statistics.
- AI likeness or voice of anyone other than the user.
- Income or result guarantees.
- Claims to cure or treat a health condition.
- Ad copy that asserts personal attributes ("Are you depressed?").
- Fake scarcity.

**AMBER — needs the user's input before publishing:**
- **Any result number:** "Is substantiation on file? Y/N", plus a typical-results line.
- **Health or fitness outcome:** cite evidence, or reframe as process or personal story.
- **Testimonial:** consent, plus disclosure of any material connection. A free or discounted programme counts.
- **Before/after:** must show the typical result alongside.

**GREEN — fine as written:**
- Process descriptions.
- The user's own story.
- Opinions framed as opinions.

**Results line template:** `"[Client] got [result] in [time]. Most clients see [typical range]; results depend on [factors]."`

**AI disclosure rule:**
- A script the user reviewed → no label. YouTube exempts AI-written scripts; the EU editorial exception covers them.
- Avatar, clone, or realistic synthetic footage → apply the platform's AI label and add a caption note.

**Linh example:**
- "Lose 10 kg in 30 days" → RED.
- Rewrite: "What I changed in my first 30 days after my second baby." This is her own story, a process claim, with no promise to the viewer.

### T4. One onboarding in three layers (replaces the five separate intakes) [DESIGN, built on Xiao et al. and SurveyMonkey]

**Layer 1 — Day 0, at most 10 questions, about 10 minutes, answered by voice:**
1. Who you help and their situation.
2. Their #1 problem, in their own words.
3. What they've tried that didn't work.
4. The result you promise.
5. Your offer, or "none yet".
6. One client story, or your own.
7. One belief of yours that others in your niche disagree with.
8. Two samples of your writing or voice notes.
9. Your main platform.
10. Hours per week you can give to content.

Output: Brand Brain v0, 10 ideas and 3 scripts in the first session, so the user gets a win immediately.

**Layer 2 — week 1–2, triggered when needed:** VOC sprint, Hell/Heaven map, belief ladder, named framework.

**Layer 3 — before the first hard-sell piece:** the Sales Brain (mechanisms, false beliefs, value equation, guarantee, proof bank).

**Interview rules:**
- One question at a time.
- One follow-up probe for specifics ("What exactly did they say?").
- "Skip" is always allowed.
- Unfilled fields are tagged `[EMPTY]` or `[GUESS]`.
- Each skill asks only the questions it needs, at the moment it needs them.

**Cold-start branch 1 — Offer v0** (Dunford's order, 5 questions):
1. What would they do if you didn't exist?
2. What do you do that those options don't?
3. So what? What value does that create?
4. Who cares about that most?
5. What label makes it obvious?

The output is a "founding client" pilot offer.

**Cold-start branch 2 — building proof from zero:**
1. Your own before and after.
2. Show-the-work proof: teardowns and audits of public examples.
3. 3–5 founding clients at a reduced price, in exchange for a documented case study with consent.
4. Messages saying "this helped", used with permission.

Hard-sell pieces stay blocked until at least one proof item exists, or the offer is clearly labelled as a founding pilot.

### T5. Answer Library (findability) [DESIGN, built on G6 sources]

- **Searchable twin:** for every educational piece, phrase the title as the buyer's question, e.g. "How do I price a retainer for a 5-person agency?"
  - Put that phrase in the first spoken line, the on-screen text, and the first line of the caption.
- **Monthly:** one YouTube video or LinkedIn article that answers the top 5 VOC questions.
- **Answer page on the user's website:** who I help, method name, process, prices or ranges, FAQs.
- **Same name everywhere:** use the identical "Person + Method + Niche" wording in every bio.
- **Monthly AI check:** ask ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Perplexity "Who helps [ICP] with [problem]?" and "What is [Method]?". Log whether the user is mentioned.
- **Reddit:** answer questions in niche subreddits within each subreddit's rules, with no links. [PRACTITIONER]

### T6. "Why isn't it working?" diagnostic (run on the last 20–30 posts) [DESIGN, using thresholds from the earlier briefs]

| Break point | Symptom | Likely cause | Module that fixes it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Not shown | Little reach to non-followers | Irregular posting; reposts or watermarks | Cadence floor; native formats |
| 2. Not stopped | Under 60% viewed-vs-swiped; people leave within 3 seconds | Hook or first frame | Hooks; first-frame checklist |
| 3. Not held | Retention drops at the same point every time | Build-up, filler | Retention mechanics; cut |
| 4. Not trusted | Views, but few follows, saves or profile visits | Generic; no point of view or proof; broad entertainment | Trust gate; Buyer Filter Test; T1 |
| 5. Not asked | Follows, but no DMs | No CTA or offer pieces | Converting arc; CTA ladder |
| 6. Not closed | DMs and calls, but no sales | Offer or sales process | Flag as outside content |

**Inputs:** a pasted export or screenshot of post metrics.

**Output:** the one break point to fix, plus 3 actions for the next 2 weeks.

**Attribution question** for every booking form or first DM: "Where did you first hear about me, and what did you watch or read before reaching out?" Log the answers weekly and judge the 90-day trend (Fishkin's lift-based measurement).

### T7. Platform picker (3 questions) [DESIGN]

1. Are your buyers businesses or consumers, and how old are they?
2. Are you better at talking or at writing?
3. How many hours a week do you have?

| Profile | Main platform | Long-form library |
|---|---|---|
| B2B consultants | LinkedIn | YouTube |
| Consumer coaches with a younger audience | Instagram or TikTok | YouTube |
| Older audiences | Facebook | YouTube |

Every profile also adds one owned channel: email.

### T8. Golden run (end to end, Linh)

1. **Day 0:** Layer 1 intake. No offer and no proof, so the Offer v0 and proof-from-zero branches run. Result: a "Founding 5" 6-week pilot.
2. **Ideas:** 30 ideas, generated as VOC themes × angles.
3. **Series:** a 7-day domino series.
4. **Scripting:** the 7 scripts go through T1 and T3, then become Shot Cards in Mode B.
5. **Week 3 diagnostic:** about 1,200 views per post, but 2 follows per 1,000 views and no DMs. That is break point 4. Fix: point of view plus process proof, and add "Founding 5" CTAs.

Ship this golden run as an example file. Reuse the same inputs as the F6 evals.

## 3) What this means for the Content Machine design

1. **Make `humanize.md` (T1) a required final step inside `cm-scripts`.** It reads the Voice Card and the last 10 rows of `CONTENT-LOG.md`. Add "no invented stats, quotes or results" to the router's NON-NEGOTIABLES.
2. **Add `delivery.md` and Shot Card fields to the script template.** Ask for the delivery mode (A, B or C) in Layer 1. Default to Mode B and 15–30 second pieces for the first 10 videos.
3. **Add `guardrails.md`, loaded by every skill,** with the T3 linter. Every output ends with a short "Claims check" listing the RED and AMBER items. Keep the date-sensitive legal notes in `platform-notes.md`:
   - FTC proposal still pending.
   - EU Article 50 from 2 August 2026.
   - YouTube "inauthentic content" policy from 15 July 2025.
4. **Collapse the five intakes into one three-layer `cm-brand-brain` (T4),** with just-in-time questions and voice answers. Success measure: a first-session user leaves with 3 usable scripts.
5. **Add the cold-start branches** (Offer v0 and proof from zero). Gate hard-sell content on having proof.
6. **Add T5 to `cm-ideas` and `cm-series`:**
   - a searchable twin for every educational piece;
   - a monthly pillar piece on YouTube or LinkedIn;
   - an answer-page template;
   - the monthly AI-mention check.
7. **Add a `cm-diagnose` step (T6) to the weekly scoreboard,** plus the attribution question on booking forms. This is the module for users who "posted a lot and got nothing". Run it first for anyone who already has a back catalogue.
8. **Add the platform picker (T7) to onboarding.**
9. **Ship golden runs for 3 personas (coach, consultant, service business)** as `examples/`, and reuse them as the release eval set.
10. **Keep AI out of the user-facing story.** Market the offer as the coach's own method, not "AI-powered" (Cicek). Never generate synthetic testimonials or avatars of other people.
11. **Open question for the founder:** if the target users are outside the US and EU (for example in Vietnam), the platform mix, the Pew figures and the compliance sections need a version for that market. This brief covers US and EU rules only.
