# Research Brief: Educational (Authority) Content for Content Machine 1.0

Tags: **[DATA]** means a platform statement, a study or a dataset. **[PRACTITIONER]** means a creator's or teacher's opinion or experience. Where a claim comes from a secondary summary rather than the original, it says so.

---

## 1) Key insights

1. **Weak educational content costs trust. Good educational content beats ads for trust.**
   - [DATA] Edelman/LinkedIn 2024: about 73% of B2B decision-makers find thought leadership more trustworthy than marketing materials for judging a company. 75% say a piece of it led them to research a product they weren't considering. 86% are more likely to invite strong thought-leadership producers to bid (RFP).
   - [DATA] The 2017 edition: 45% of business decision-makers and 53% of C-suite had been disappointed by a company's thought leadership. 30% and 35% respectively said poor thought leadership led them *not* to award business.
   - Implication: the Machine needs a quality gate, not just volume.
2. **What buyers rate as high quality.**
   - [DATA] Edelman 2024: 66% cite a unique format or style. 55% cite strong data or research.
   - That maps directly to "own a named framework" and "bring proof or numbers".
3. **The opening seconds carry most of the result.**
   - [DATA] Facebook/Nielsen: up to 47% of a video campaign's value was delivered in the first 3 seconds, and up to 74% in the first 10.
   - [DATA] TikTok's official ad guidance: "Introduce your content proposition in the first 3 seconds" and "prioritize your hook in the first 6 seconds". It also recommends featuring a person on screen.
   - [DATA] YouTube Analytics has an "Intro" metric: the % of viewers still watching at 30 seconds. YouTube says a high score means the first 30 seconds matched what the title and thumbnail promised.
4. **On Shorts, the swipe decision is the metric to watch.**
   - [DATA, practitioner-run study] Paddy Galloway's team analysed about 5,400 Shorts across 33 channels.
   - Top performers kept 70–90% of viewers ("Viewed vs. Swiped Away"). Under 60%, distribution collapsed.
   - Shorts averaging more than 50 seconds of view duration averaged about 4.1M views, against about 1.8M for 40–50 seconds.
5. **Platform signals favour saveable and shareable teaching formats.**
   - [DATA] Instagram: Mosseri says the top ranking signals are watch time, likes and sends. Sends per reach matter more for reaching non-followers.
   - [DATA] Socialinsider: Instagram carousels average 0.55% engagement, Reels 0.52%, images 0.37%.
   - [DATA] LinkedIn: native document (carousel) posts had the highest engagement, 7.0% (Socialinsider, 1.3M posts). AuthoredUp measured documents at about 1.45x reach and text-only at 0.88x.
   - [DATA] Context: van der Blom's 2025 report (1.8M posts) shows LinkedIn views down about 50% year on year, so format choice matters more.
6. **Every content unit is Hook → Retain → Reward.** [PRACTITIONER, Hormozi, *$100M Leads*] Long-form is just units strung together. Hormozi's line "Give away the secrets, sell the implementation" is the model for educational content that sells.
7. **Ideas and packaging beat production.**
   - [PRACTITIONER] Galloway: "the idea is more important than everything." Spend 30–40% of work time on ideas. Write 30+ title variants and 3 genuinely different thumbnail concepts.
   - [PRACTITIONER] Ali Abdaal: about 80% of a video's success is title, thumbnail and hook.
8. **Repeated exposure converts; a single post rarely does.**
   - [PRACTITIONER] Jun Yuh's weekly mix is 3 attract, 1 nurture, 2 position, 1 convert, explicitly built on the idea that people need several exposures before they buy.
   - [PRACTITIONER] Matt Gray advises about 60% value content to direct selling, with lead magnets placed inside the content.
   - Both support the founder's domino/series thesis.
9. **Named frameworks are how experts become memorable.**
   - [PRACTITIONER] Codie Sanchez names her internal methods (e.g. "3-6-12 framework", "SOWS Test", "BRIT Test"). This is from a third-party analysis of her content.
   - [PRACTITIONER] Hormozi brands everything ("Value Equation", "Grand Slam Offer").
   - [PRACTITIONER] 37signals' "Out-teach your competition": teaching *is* the marketing.

## 2) Frameworks & templates

### A. Hook systems

**A1. Call-out + Condition for Value**
- Source: Hormozi, *$100M Leads* (secondary summaries). [PRACTITIONER]
- How it works: name who it's for, then promise what they get for staying.
- Hormozi's topic sources (confirmed via secondary summaries):
  - Far past: your old lessons.
  - Recent past: what just happened.
  - Present: insights as they come to you.
  - Trending: where attention already is.
  - Manufactured: do or learn something, then show it.
- Headline levers. This list is from my recall of the book and was **not** confirmed online, so verify before quoting: Recency, Relevance, Celebrity, Proximity, Conflict, Unusual, Ongoing.
- Template: `If you're a [specific person] who [specific situation], [watch/read this] to [specific outcome] [without pain / in timeframe].`
- Example: "If you're an agency owner stuck at $30k a month, this is the one pricing change that got my clients to $50k without adding headcount."

**A2. Three-step hook**
- Source: Kallaway. [PRACTITIONER]
- How it works:
  1. **Context lean-in**: make the topic obvious so the right person self-selects.
  2. **Scroll-stop interjection**: one line starting with "but", "however" or "yet".
  3. **Contrarian snapback**: flip the expectation while staying on topic.
- Rules: short, choppy sentences. On-screen text of 3–5 words. Deliver value within about 4 seconds on short-form.
- Template: `[Common belief/goal in your niche]. But [interjection]. [The opposite thing is what actually matters].`
- Example: "Most coaches think they need more leads. But here's the thing. Your problem is the 70% of leads who ghost after the discovery call."

**A3. Stack the first frame**
- Sources: TikTok guidance [DATA]; Galloway's Shorts analysis [PRACTITIONER].
- On swipe feeds the decision happens around 1 second. Say the promise three ways at once:
  - **Visual**: the result, a prop, or a pattern break.
  - **On-screen text**: 3–6 words.
  - **Spoken**: the first line.
- Feature a person.

**Hook bank (fill-in templates, sorted by audience awareness):**
- Problem-aware: `You're doing [effort] and still [bad result]? It's not [assumed cause]. It's [real cause].`
- Mistake: `Stop [common practice]. It's why [bad outcome].`
- Myth: `"[Popular advice]" is wrong for [audience]. Here's what works instead.`
- Specific result: `How [client type] went from [A] to [B] in [time] with [mechanism].`
- How I would: `If I had to [get result] from zero in [time], here's exactly what I'd do.`
- Teardown: `I reviewed [N] [things, e.g. sales pages or offers]. [N%] made this one mistake.`
- Framework: `The [Name] Method: the [N] steps I use to [result] for every client.`
- Listicle: `[N] [things] that [outcome], ranked from least to most important.`
- Contrarian: `Unpopular opinion: [industry sacred cow] is killing your [result].`
- Comparison (Welsh "X vs Y"): `[Amateur] vs [Pro] [activity]: the difference is [one thing].`

### B. Body structures: eight educational angles

Generate any topic × these eight angles. This merges Justin Welsh's Content Matrix with Dickie Bush's 4A framework [PRACTITIONER].
- **Welsh's eight styles**: X vs Y, Listicle, Observation, Motivational, Actionable, Analytical, Contrarian, Present vs Future.
- **Bush's 4A**: Actionable (here's how), Analytical (here are the numbers), Aspirational (yes, you can), Anthropological (here's why). His most viral pieces combine two, e.g. Actionable + Analytical.

| Angle | Skeleton |
|---|---|
| Problem → Mistake → Fix | Symptom they feel → the mistake causing it → why it fails → the fix → proof/example → next step |
| Myth-bust | Myth → why people believe it → evidence it's wrong → what's true → how to apply |
| Step-by-step | Outcome promise → step 1/2/3 (each with a "do this" plus a "common error") → result |
| Listicle | Promise N items → items ordered weakest to strongest (best last, to reward staying) → summary |
| Framework reveal | Problem → name the framework → each component as a one-line rule → mini case → "save this" |
| Teardown | "I analysed X" → what works (2) → what breaks (2–3) → the rewrite → lesson for viewer |
| "How I would…" | Constraint (zero budget, 90 days) → phase 1/2/3 → what I'd skip → expected result |
| Contrarian take | Sacred cow → the cost of following it → your alternative → proof → when the old advice IS right (credibility) |

### C. Retention mechanics

- **Open loops** [PRACTITIONER]
  - State the payoff early, then deliver it late or in stages.
  - Line: `"…and #3 is the one nobody does, I'll show you in a sec."`
  - Galloway's caveat: put your best moment early, and use the loop for the *next* best thing.
- **But/Therefore** [PRACTITIONER, Parker & Stone]
  - Join beats with "but" or "therefore", never "and then".
  - The Machine should run this as a check that flags any script beat joined by "and then".
- **Re-hooks and pattern interrupts**
  - Short-form [PRACTITIONER]: change something every few seconds (b-roll, text, angle, prop) and add a mid-video re-hook line (`"But here's where it gets interesting…"`).
  - Long-form [PRACTITIONER, leaked MrBeast production guide]:
    - "The first minute is the most important minute."
    - Minutes 1–3: "transition from hype to execution… start showing them."
    - Around minute 3, a "3 minute re-engagement."
    - Minutes 3–6: the next most important stretch.
- **Payoff timing**
  - [PRACTITIONER] Galloway: the intro should restate what the title and thumbnail promised, go "straight to a storyline", and put "the best clip first".
  - [PRACTITIONER] Hormozi: removing about 3 seconds of buildup before the hook took one video from about 40k to 780k views. Single anecdote, reported second-hand.
- **Reward**: end on the strongest takeaway, then send them to the next piece in the series rather than ending with a generic "subscribe". Ali Abdaal's end-screen rule says the same.

### D. Fill-in templates by format

**D1. Short-form talking head (30–60s, Reels/TikTok/Shorts)**
```
[0–3s HOOK] Text: "<3–6 words>"  Say: <Call-out + promise OR lean-in/but/snapback>
[3–8s STAKES] "Most <audience> do <mistake>, which is why <cost>."
[8–40s VALUE] Step/point 1 → 2 → 3 (each 1 sentence + 1 concrete example). Re-hook at ~50%: "But the real unlock is #3."
[40–55s PAYOFF] Best insight + 1 proof line (client result/number).
[55–60s NEXT] "Part 2 tomorrow: <next domino>" OR "Comment <WORD> and I'll send the <framework PDF>."
```
**Worked example (sales consultant):**
- Hook: "Your discovery calls aren't failing at the close. They fail in the first five minutes."
- Stakes: "Most consultants open with their credentials, so the prospect spends the call judging them."
- Value:
  1. Open by restating their problem in their own words.
  2. Ask "What happens if this isn't fixed in 90 days?"
  3. Only then show the plan. Re-hook before step 3: "and step 3 is the one that doubles close rate."
- Payoff: "One client went from 18% to 34% close rate in a month with this."
- Next: "Tomorrow: the exact question I ask to handle 'I need to think about it.'"

**D2. Carousel (Instagram/LinkedIn, 8–10 slides)**
```
S1 Cover: <N> + <outcome> + <for whom> (≤10 words). Design it LAST.
S2 Confirm payoff: "Most <audience> <mistake>. Here's the fix:"
S3–S8 One idea per slide: Rule (bold) + 1-line why + 1 example.
S9 Summary/cheat-sheet slide (built to be saved/sent).
S10 CTA: "Save this + send to a <role> who needs it" / "Follow for part 2: <next>".
```
- [DATA] Socialinsider found the highest engagement at 10 slides, with a dip after 3 slides and a recovery at 8 or more.
- Optimise for sends. Make the summary slide sendable.

**D3. LinkedIn/X text post**
```
Line 1 Hook (≤12 words)  |  Line 2 Re-hook (raises stakes/specificity)
Context: 1–2 short lines (your proof/credibility).
Body: numbered list or 3-beat story; 1 idea per line; whitespace.
Takeaway: 1 bolded sentence.
CTA: question that invites a reply OR pointer to lead magnet.
```
- Story/offer variant, Justin Welsh's PAIPS [PRACTITIONER]: Problem → Agitate → Intrigue → Positive future → Solution (CTA to newsletter or offer).
- Long newsletter or script variant, Dan Koe's PASTOR: Problem → Amplify → Story/Solution → Testimony → Offer → Response. Ray Edwards created PASTOR; a secondary source reports Koe uses it in more than 80% of his newsletters. [PRACTITIONER]

**D4. Long-form YouTube (8–20 min)**

Galloway's packaging rules, done first:
- 30+ title variants, under about 60 characters.
- 3 thumbnail concepts, 5 or fewer words, 3 or fewer elements, passes a 1–2 second glance test.

```
0:00 HOOK — restate title/thumbnail promise; show end result first (Ali Abdaal HIVES: Hook).
0:10 PROOF — why listen (results/credential) (Hormozi Promise→Proof→Plan; Abdaal "Intro").
0:20 PLAN — "We'll cover A, B, C — C is where most people get it wrong" (open loop).
1:00–3:00 Execute point A fast (stop telling, start showing).
~3:00 RE-ENGAGE — story, case study, surprising data, or demo.
3:00–end Points B, C (But/Therefore transitions; mini-payoff each ~60–90s).
END — Strongest takeaway → "Watch <next video in the series>" + contextual lead magnet.
```
- Repurposing [PRACTITIONER]: Dan Koe reportedly records one written newsletter as the YouTube video, then turns its points into social posts. That makes the YouTube long-form the "anchor" piece of each domino series.

### E. Making value feel like a free sample of the paid offer

- **Secrets free, implementation paid.** [PRACTITIONER, Hormozi] On Threads he adds: "The information was never the asset… execution is what people actually pay for."
- **Operational rule for the Machine.** Each educational piece:
  1. Teaches the *what* and *why* completely.
  2. Gives one *how* step in full, so the viewer gets a real win.
  3. Names the remaining steps (the full framework), so the viewer sees the whole system.
  4. Ties the gap to the offer: done-for-you, accountability, customisation, speed.
- **Contextual lead magnet.** [PRACTITIONER, Matt Gray] Offer the framework as a PDF or checklist *from inside the content* ("Comment SYSTEM"). Gray claims email converts about 37x better than social; that is his figure and unverified.
- **Weekly mix.** [PRACTITIONER, Jun Yuh] 3 attract, 1 nurture, 2 position, 1 convert. Most educational pieces fill attract and position; the convert slot references the same framework.

### F. Building the creator's own named framework (IP)

**Steps:**
1. List the 3–7 things you *always* do with clients to get the result, in order. Use client calls and SOPs as raw material.
2. Turn each into a verb-led rule ("Diagnose", "Price", "Package").
3. Name the whole thing using Hormozi's MAGIC naming from *$100M Offers*: Magnet, Avatar, Goal, Interval, Container word (method, system, blueprint). Use 3–5 of the five.
4. Make it visual: an acronym, numbered steps, or a 2x2. The visual becomes the cover slide.
5. One framework yields many pieces: a reveal post, a deep dive per step, myth-busts per step, client teardowns *using* it, and "how I would" videos with it. That is the seed of a domino series.

**Worked example (fractional CFO for agencies):** "The 90-Day Profit Lock System for Agency Owners".
- Steps: **L**ift prices → **O**ffload low-margin clients → **C**ap scope → **K**eep 20% profit-first.
- Series: 1 reveal carousel, 4 step videos, 1 teardown ("I audited 10 agency P&Ls"), 1 myth-bust ("more clients ≠ more profit"), 1 convert post ("we install LOCK in 90 days").

### G. Anti-info-dump trust test

Every piece must pass all seven before output:
1. **One idea, one person.** A single named reader and a single takeaway.
2. **Specific beats vague.** Include a number, a name, a timeframe or a mechanism.
3. **Proof element.** A client result, your own data, a screenshot, or a short story.
4. **Point of view.** State what you believe that others in the niche don't. This matches the Edelman 66% finding on unique format or style.
5. **Story or example.** The aspirational or anthropological layer, not only the actionable one.
6. **Bridge.** It references the creator's named framework or offer.
7. **Next domino.** It points to the next piece in the series.

Reason for the gate: generic information gets views but earns no attribution to the creator, and poor thought leadership actively loses deals (Edelman 2017 [DATA]).

## 3) What this means for the Content Machine design

1. **Ship an "Authority Engine" module as separate files:**
   - `hooks.md`: the hook bank, A1–A3, sorted by awareness level.
   - `structures.md`: the 8 angles.
   - `formats.md`: the D1–D4 templates.
   - `retention.md`: the C mechanics.
   - `trust-gate.md`: the G checklist, run as a self-scoring rubric before any script is output.
2. **Build an IP Builder interview as step 0.** Before any educational content, the LLM interviews the user (their process, client results, beliefs others disagree with) and outputs:
   - one named framework (via MAGIC),
   - 3–5 contrarian beliefs,
   - a proof bank of results, numbers and stories.

   Every later prompt pulls from this file. It turns "doesn't know what to say" into a fill-in exercise.
3. **Idea generator = Topics × Angles.** Topics are the framework's steps plus audience pains from Research; angles are the 8 structures.
   - 5 topics × 8 angles = 40 ideas per sprint (the Welsh matrix logic).
   - Score each idea on: the right buyer, a specific promise, a proof available, and a link to the offer.
   - Following Galloway, put effort into ideas, not polish.
4. **Generate in dominoes, not single posts.** Default output is a *series*:
   - 1 long-form anchor (YouTube or newsletter),
   - 5–7 short-form or carousel pieces, one per framework step or myth,
   - 1–2 "position" proof pieces,
   - 1 convert piece.

   Each piece ends by pointing to the next. Tag pieces by Jun Yuh's attract/nurture/position/convert labels so the weekly calendar auto-balances (default 3-1-2-1).
5. **Every script output has the same fields:**
   - Hook (3 variants: call-out, lean-in/snapback, result-first)
   - On-screen text (≤6 words) and visual note
   - Body with a marked re-hook and payoff
   - Proof line
   - CTA or next domino

   Galloway's practice is generating many options and picking one, so the default output is 3–5 hook variants per script, not 1.
6. **Built-in quality checks to run as LLM self-critique:**
   - Hook delivers the promise within the first 3 seconds, or 10 seconds on long-form.
   - No "and then" transitions.
   - One idea per slide.
   - Contains a number or proof.
   - Passes the trust gate.
   - Packaging (title/cover) written last.
7. **Platform defaults:**
   - Short-form 30–60s, with 50s+ when the content earns it (the Galloway Shorts data).
   - Carousels 8–10 slides with a sendable summary slide (Mosseri on sends).
   - LinkedIn: document carousels and text posts with whitespace, plus a weekly carousel (AuthoredUp/Socialinsider reach and engagement data).
   - YouTube: one anchor every 1–2 weeks. Matt Gray runs 1 YouTube video per week plus daily LinkedIn; set the default lower for non-experts.
8. **Keep it no-brainer.** Each template ships with one worked example for a coach, one for a consultant and one for a service business, so users can pattern-match instead of reading theory.
