## 0. Core packaging laws (they apply to every section)

1. **Packaging comes before production.** "Attention happens before quality." He treats packaging as "80% of the importance" and says it is "worth spending an extra 30 minutes" on a video that takes about 15 hours to make. Titles get written before the script.
2. **Write many options, then cut.** He writes 50–60 titles, shortlists 6 and ranks them, and makes 50–100 thumbnail concepts across 3 styles. Then he A/B tests the best title against 3 thumbnails. A video ranked 9–10 of 10 gets re-packaged; one ranked 1–2 is left alone.
3. **Retitle toward the viewer's outcome or psychology, not his own brag.** For example, "How I Work 4 Hours a Day (and Make $730,000/Month)" became "how to work less (and earn more)" (621K). This happened 9 times across title changes.
4. **Exact, odd numbers beat round ones:** 14,479 calls; 138,427 subscribers; $264,000/month; 1,460 days.
5. **The parenthetical does one of two jobs:** it removes the objection ("without going viral") or adds a bonus ("and earn more").
6. **Re-run winners.** He reuses a winning title on a second video, re-dates yearly posts ("…in 2026"), and re-runs the birthday "I'm X" list every year.
7. **Name every system, ritual and level, and reuse the names everywhere** (section 7).
8. **Every piece gets one lead magnet matched to its topic.** The CTA names "the complete [asset] I use to [result named in this piece]" (section 6).
9. **Score content by cash and applications, not views.** His top cash video "is like our 42nd most popular video… 100K plus of cash collected."
10. **Treat casing as a style choice to test, not a rule.** 72% of his top 60 YT titles are lowercase, and newsletters have been lowercase since 2024. Title Case returned on YT in mid-2026.

## 2. Thumbnail-text rules and examples

### Rules
1. **2–5 words**, in a bold geometric sans. Mostly white lowercase, one brand accent colour (his is lime). Since 2026 the line often ends in a period ("you're not behind.").
2. **Never repeat the title.** The thumbnail adds the emotional or contrarian layer, or the proof number. Only one of the 16 viewed overlaps ("work less").
3. **Pick one device per thumbnail:**
   - (a) a before → after number joined by a dashed arrow
   - (b) numbered multi-panel progression (7 rainbow strips; $0 / $10K / $1M)
   - (c) a hand-drawn journey curve whose labelled nodes are the video's steps
   - (d) a framework artifact (whiteboard, laptop showing the framework, notebook template, wall of printed frameworks)
   - (e) a named-system lockup (small "the 2-day" / giant "PERSONAL BRAND" / small lime "blueprint.")
   - (f) a back-to-camera or over-the-shoulder shot of a board, which makes viewers curious about what is on it
   - (g) a cinematic lifestyle frame with a 2-word overlay and a hand-drawn arrow
4. **Face:** calm, neutral or contemplative, often not looking at the lens. No shock faces. Black tee, warm natural light, a desaturated palette plus the accent colour.
5. **His three validated styles** (EP142): "panel thumbnails", "solo screen… with a bit of text", and "talking head with some sort of graph behind".
6. **Process:** 50–100 concepts across the 3 styles, then test 3 distinct thumbnails against the best title. Re-test when views per hour taper. The discipline video was flat for about 22 days, then took off after a switch to a panel thumbnail reading "how to get addicted". It is now at 547K.
7. **Template:** [2–5 word claim the title doesn't make] + [one device] + [calm face or back-to-camera] + [one accent colour].
