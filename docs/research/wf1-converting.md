# Content Machine 1.0: Converting Content Research Brief

Every claim below is labeled **[DATA]** (a study, platform data, or a platform/legal rule) or **[PRACTITIONER]** (expert opinion or experience). Example names, niches and numbers are made up for illustration.

---

## 1) Key insights

1. **Copy doesn't create desire. It points existing desire at your offer, and how far you have to go depends on awareness.** Schwartz: *"The more aware your market, the easier the selling job, the less you need to say."* His rule for the opening line depends on what the reader already knows:
   - Most aware: start with the product and the offer.
   - Product or solution aware: start with the desire.
   - Problem aware: start with the problem and sharpen it into a specific need.
   - Unaware: start by identifying with the reader. Don't mention the product. [PRACTITIONER, Schwartz 1966]
2. **Most coaching and consulting markets are at sophistication stage 4–5.** Schwartz's five stages:
   1. A simple claim works.
   2. You have to enlarge the claim.
   3. You need a mechanism.
   4. You have to elaborate the mechanism.
   5. You have to identify with the prospect, with the mechanism moved to the subhead.

   "I help you grow your business" is a stage-1 claim in a stage-5 market, which is a main reason people post a lot and get nothing back. [PRACTITIONER; the stage diagnosis is our inference]
3. **The mechanism has two parts and must be named.** Georgi: the problem mechanism explains *why they failed before* ("not their fault"), and the solution mechanism logically follows from it. In his words, "they had 99% of it right before, but there's just that one percent that was missing." [PRACTITIONER, Georgi/RMBC]
4. **Write the hook last.** Georgi writes the lead last "about 90% of the time." Once the body exists, "it's easy to add in unique teasers, curiosity bullets." [PRACTITIONER]
5. **One belief knocks over the others, which is the founder's domino idea.** Brunson's Big Domino: *"If I can make people believe that [new opportunity] is the key to [result they desire most] and is only available through [my vehicle], then all other objections… become irrelevant."* Prospects hold three kinds of false belief: about the **vehicle** (will this approach work?), **internal** (can *I* do it?) and **external** (will outside factors stop me?). [PRACTITIONER]
6. **A series is the proven way to launch.** Jeff Walker's "Sideways Sales Letter" is a sequence of free content pieces before the offer:
   - PLC1 Opportunity: define a problem and solve it.
   - PLC2 Transformation: deliver real value.
   - PLC3 Ownership: define the next problem, which the offer solves.
   - Then the offer.

   This is the domino series, already field-tested. [PRACTITIONER]
7. **Proof matters more as the price goes up.** In a Spiegel/PowerReviews study, showing reviews raised conversion up to 190% for lower-priced products and up to **380% for higher-priced ones**. The **first 5 reviews** had the biggest impact. This is e-commerce data; applying it to coaching is an extrapolation. [DATA]
8. **Specific numbers read as more credible.** People treat a precise number as a sign the speaker is confident (Jerez-Fernandez et al., 2014). Precise numbers in ad claims lowered consumer skepticism (Xie & Kronrod, *J. of Advertising*, 2012). [DATA]
9. **Risk reversal increases purchases more than it increases returns.** A meta-analysis of 22 papers found lenient return policies raised purchases, with a smaller effect on returns (Janakiraman, Syrdal & Freling, *J. of Retailing*, 2016). This is retail data, applied here to guarantees. [DATA]
10. **The message has to land in the first 3 seconds.** TikTok's ad-creative guide reports:
    - Over 63% of the highest-CTR videos state the key message or product in the first 3 seconds.
    - 33% of the highest view-through ads break the fourth wall (speak straight to camera).
    - 40% of top ads use text overlays.
    - Vertical video gets about 25% higher 6-second watch-through.

    Meta reports 1.23× brand recall when the brand appears in the first 3 seconds (via a secondary source). [DATA]
11. **Content that looks editorial gets read.** Ogilvy: making ads look like editorial pages attracts "about 50 per cent more readers." [PRACTITIONER, legacy claim] The limit: paid advertorials must be clearly labeled "Ad" or "Advertisement"; the FTC rejects "Sponsored by" and "Promoted" as disclosures. [DATA, policy]
12. **Give far more than you ask.** Hormozi's give:ask ratio is about 3.5:1, based on TV (47 minutes of content to 13 minutes of ads per hour) and Facebook (about 4 posts per ad). A CTA needs two parts: **what to do** and **a reason to do it now**. [PRACTITIONER]
13. **Platform rules shape the CTA system.**
    - Instagram private replies: **one** DM per comment, sent **within 7 days** of the comment. Follow-ups are only allowed **within 24 hours after the user replies**. So the first auto-DM must ask a question. [DATA, policy]
    - Facebook demotes "engagement bait" such as "Comment YES if…". Requests for advice or recommendations are exempt. [DATA, policy]
14. **Compliance risk is real for this audience.**
    - FTC: if a testimonial's results aren't typical, the ad must make clear what generally expected results are.
    - Fake or AI-generated testimonials are banned (rule effective 21 Oct 2024, up to about $52k per violation).
    - In Jan 2025 the FTC proposed extending the Business Opportunity Rule to **business coaching**, requiring written substantiation for earnings claims. Check its current status.
    - Meta ads can't assert or imply personal attributes: "Do you have diabetes?" is prohibited, "Depression counseling" is allowed. [DATA, policy]
15. **Treat AIDA and similar models as checklists, not laws.** A review of more than 250 papers found "little empirical support for any of the hierarchies of effects." Use them as scripting checklists. [DATA]

## 2) Frameworks & templates

### A. Foundations (the system's engine)

**RMBC (Stefan Georgi)**
- **Research.** Answer 7 questions, using the market's own words (forums, reviews, DMs, sales calls):
  1. Who is the customer?
  2. What are their pains?
  3. What outcome do they want?
  4. What are they already using?
  5. What do they like about it?
  6. What do they dislike about it?
  7. Are there horror stories?
- **Mechanism.** One sentence for the problem mechanism and one for the solution mechanism.
- **Brief.** Write each answer as *ready-to-paste copy*, not bullets: audience, 1–3 fears, one big promise, why existing solutions fail, the two mechanisms, the product, and the background and discovery story.
- **Copy.** Seven parts: Lead → Background story → Problem mechanism → Solution mechanism → Product build-up and reveal → Close (value, price, guarantee, CTA) → FAQs.
- [PRACTITIONER. Georgi says FAQs add 10–15% conversions; that is his own claim.]

**Value equation (Hormozi, *$100M Offers*)**
- Value = (Dream outcome × Perceived likelihood) ÷ (Time delay × Effort and sacrifice).
- Each converting post should push on one of these terms:
  - Case studies → likelihood.
  - Speed or "first win" content → time.
  - "Done-with-you" or "without X" → effort.

**Hormozi ad and CTA structure (*$100M Leads*)**
- Ad = **Call-out → Value → CTA**.
- Call-out types: **Label** ("Agency owners:"), **Yes-question**, **If-then**, **Ridiculous result**.
- Lead magnet types: **Reveal a problem** (diagnosis), **Sample or trial**, **One step of a multi-step process**.
- CTA = action + reason to act now.

**Copy checklists**
- PAS (Problem–Agitate–Solve).
- AIDA (E. St. Elmo Lewis, 1910).
- 4Ps (Picture–Promise–Prove–Push).
- Use them to check a draft is complete, not as the structure itself. [PRACTITIONER]

### B. Format templates, each mapped to an awareness level

| # | Format | Awareness level | Main belief or value term it moves |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Story-selling (Epiphany Bridge) | Unaware → Problem | Identification |
| 2 | Editorial "educational that converts" | Problem → Solution | Problem mechanism |
| 3 | Unique-mechanism reveal | Problem/Solution (soph. 3–5) | Vehicle belief |
| 4 | Belief shift (old → new → offer) | Solution | Vehicle / internal / external |
| 5 | Case study / client story | Solution → Product | Perceived likelihood |
| 6 | Behind-the-scenes of results | Product | Likelihood, effort |
| 7 | Objection crusher | Product | Whichever term the objection hits |
| 8 | Offer / launch post | Most aware | Offer + urgency |
| 9 | DR ads (talking head, UGC, short VSL, static) | Matched to the audience | All |

**1. Epiphany Bridge story (Brunson)**
- Steps: Backstory → Desire → Wall (what failed) → Epiphany → Plan → Transformation → Bridge to the reader.
- Template: *"[Time/place]: I wanted [desire]. I tried [common solution]; it [failed because ___]. Then [specific moment] showed me [new belief]. I changed [one thing]. Result: [specific number + timeframe]. If you're [identity], the lesson is [new belief]. [Soft CTA]."*

**2. Editorial / "educational that converts" (advertorial logic, organic)**
- Steps: Counterintuitive hook → why the common advice fails (problem mechanism) → the real cause → named fix with 3 steps (the *what*) → one proof line → CTA for the *how*.
- Template: *"Most [avatar] think [problem] comes from [blamed cause]. It doesn't. It's [problem mechanism]. Here's the [Named Method]: 1) __ 2) __ 3) __. [Client] used step 2 to get [specific result]. Comment [KEYWORD] for the full checklist."*

**3. Unique-mechanism reveal (Georgi two-part)**
- Template: *"Why [common solution] keeps failing [avatar]: it fixes [symptom], but the real cause is [problem mechanism]. That's why [Named solution mechanism] works: it [logical link]. Proof: [number]."*
- At sophistication stage 5, open with identification ("If you're the agency owner who…") and put the mechanism second.

**4. Belief shift**
- Make one post for each false belief (vehicle, internal, external).
- Template: *"I used to believe [old belief]. Then [event/evidence]. Now I know [new belief]. Which means [implication for reader]. That's exactly what [offer] is built on. [CTA]."*

**5. Case study (proof stack)**
- Steps:
  1. Before: specific situation, in the client's own quoted words.
  2. What they tried and why it failed.
  3. The turning point: the mechanism applied.
  4. The result: a precise number plus a timeframe.
  5. What is typical (FTC requirement).
  6. Who it's for and not for.
  7. CTA.
- Stack proof types in one piece: a number, an artifact (screenshot or doc), the client's voice, and a third-party signal.

**6. Behind-the-scenes of results**
- Template: *"Here's what week [N] actually looks like inside [program]: [real artifact: call notes, dashboard, template]. Most people think [effort assumption]; it's really [smaller effort]."*
- This raises perceived likelihood and lowers perceived effort.

**7. Objection crusher**
- Template: *"'[Objection, verbatim from DMs or sales calls]'. Fair. Here's what's true: [reframe]. [Proof]. And if it doesn't work: [risk reversal]. [CTA]."*
- Tag each objection with the value-equation term it attacks (time, effort, likelihood, or price).

**8. Offer / launch post (most aware: lead with the offer)**
- Template: *"[Offer name]: [dream outcome] in [timeframe] without [sacrifice]. You get: [stack]. Guarantee: [specific risk reversal]. Why now: [real deadline/cap]. [Action] → [where]."*

**9. CTA ladder**
- **Keyword → DM.** Post CTA: "Comment AUDIT and I'll send you…". The auto-DM (one allowed) should be: *"Here's the [asset] 👉 [link]. Quick q, what's your biggest [X] right now?"* The question opens the 24-hour follow-up window.
- **Lead magnet.** Use a "reveal a problem" diagnostic (scorecard or audit) for services.
- **Booking a call.** For high ticket, use a qualifying question in the DM, then the booking link.
- **Low-ticket offer** or **webinar.** Run the webinar as the Perfect Webinar: one big promise, three "secrets" that each break one false belief, then the stack and close.

**10. Direct-response ad scripts**

*Talking head, 30–60 seconds:*

| Time | Section | What goes there |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3s | Call-out | The key message (required by TikTok's data) |
| 3–15s | Problem | Problem plus problem mechanism |
| 15–35s | Solution and proof | Named solution mechanism plus one proof line |
| 35–50s | Offer | Offer and value |
| 50–60s | CTA | Action plus reason to act now |

- Shoot it straight to camera, vertical, with text overlays.

*UGC-style:*
- These must be **real clients**; synthetic testimonials are illegal.
- Prompt the client with: "Before, I was ___ / I'd tried ___ / What changed was ___ / Now ___ / If you're ___, do ___."

*Short VSL, 90–180 seconds (RMBC compressed):*
1. Lead: pain, promise, tease of the mechanism.
2. 15-second backstory.
3. Problem mechanism.
4. Solution mechanism.
5. Offer reveal.
6. Guarantee and CTA.

Put the FAQs on the landing page.

*Static ad:*
- Headline: benefit plus mechanism.
- Primary text: PAS or 4Ps.
- CTA.
- Avoid personal-attribute "you" claims. For example, write "A 12-week plan for busy executives" rather than "Are you overweight?"

### C. Worked examples
Illustrative consultant: **Dana, a pricing consultant for agency owners.** Her mechanisms:
- Problem mechanism: the "Scope-Creep Tax", unbilled extras that quietly eat margin.
- Solution mechanism: the "Fenced Retainer".

**Example 1: Mechanism reveal plus belief shift (organic Reel, problem aware)**
> **Hook (on screen):** "Raising your rates won't fix your margins."
> "Most agency owners think low profit = low prices. I thought so too. I raised rates 30% and margins *didn't move*. The real leak was the Scope-Creep Tax: the 'quick' revisions and extra calls nobody bills. In my last 12 client audits, it averaged [X hours]/month *(real data only)*. The fix is a Fenced Retainer: 1) define deliverables as outputs, not hours; 2) price a change-order menu; 3) review scope every 30 days. Comment **FENCE** and I'll DM you the change-order menu template."

**Example 2: Talking-head ad (solution aware, cold traffic)**
> "Agency owners: if you're fully booked but still not taking home a profit, it's not your rates. *(call-out + key message in under 3s)* It's unbilled scope creep. [Client name] cut 11 unbilled hours a week in 30 days with one change: a Fenced Retainer. *(specific proof; typical result: '[typical range] for most clients')* I'll audit your current retainer for free and show you exactly where the hours leak. That's the 'reveal a problem' lead magnet. I only do 10 a month, so tap 'Book' now."

## 3) What this means for the Content Machine design

1. **Make RMBC the required onboarding.** Before any converting content, the pack runs a "Sales Brain" interview that produces one file:
   - Avatar quotes in their own words.
   - Three fears.
   - The big promise.
   - Existing solutions, with likes, dislikes and horror stories.
   - Problem and solution mechanisms, both **named**.
   - The Big Domino sentence.
   - The three false beliefs (vehicle, internal, external), each with its counter-belief.
   - The offer scored on the value equation.
   - The guarantee.
   - The CTA ladder.

   Every template reads from this file. That is what fixes "they don't know what to say."
2. **Diagnose sophistication.** Three questions:
   - Have prospects seen this promise before?
   - Have they tried alternatives?
   - Are competitors already naming methods?

   At stage 3 or higher, block output that has no named mechanism. At stage 5, use identification-led hooks.
3. **Tag every piece with an awareness level** and pick the format from the table in section 2B. The weekly plan should cover all five levels, not only "educational."
4. **Build the domino series into the planner.**
   - Default: a 7-piece converting arc, structured as a Sideways Sales Letter plus the three false beliefs:
     1. Story (unaware)
     2. Mechanism reveal (problem aware)
     3. Vehicle belief shift
     4. Case study
     5. Internal belief / objection crusher
     6. Behind-the-scenes / external belief
     7. Offer post (most aware)
   - Keep about 3.5:1 give-to-ask across the calendar. Every piece still gets a soft CTA; only the offer post makes a hard ask.
5. **Use a hook-last workflow.** Generate the body, then 10 hooks: 4 Hormozi call-out types, plus Schwartz's opener for the tagged awareness level, plus a stated key message within 3 seconds. The user picks one.
6. **Add a proof and specificity linter.** It should:
   - Flag round or vague numbers.
   - Flag claims with no proof asset.
   - Flag a missing "typical results" line next to standout results.
   - Flag earnings or income claims (stricter for business coaches).
   - Flag Meta personal-attribute phrasing in ads.
   - Flag any request to invent testimonials, and refuse it.
7. **Run a proof bank.** The Spiegel data shows the first 5 proof points matter most. The pack should push users to collect 5 specific, consented client results (with numbers, timeframes and artifacts) early, using a client-interview script that follows the UGC prompts in section 2B.10.
8. **Ship the CTA system as copy-paste assets.**
   - Keyword-DM flows, with the first DM always asking a question (because of Instagram's 7-day / 1-message / 24-hour rules).
   - On Facebook, avoid "comment YES if" bait.
   - Templates for each lead magnet type.
   - A booking-call qualifier.
   - A Perfect Webinar outline.
9. **Ad module.** Build a hooks × body matrix (Hormozi tests 30 hooks × 10 bodies; scale down to about 5 × 2 for small budgets). Provide talking-head, UGC and short-VSL scripts with timing marks, plus static copy variants. Advertorial landing pages get an automatic "Advertisement" label.
10. **Add a compliance note** to the pack README: FTC endorsement and review rules, the native-ad disclosure rule, the pending coaching earnings-claim rule, and Meta's personal-attributes policy.
