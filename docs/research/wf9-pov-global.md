# Global scout: POV and relatable short-form creators for Content Machine 1.0

## Method and caveats

- **How I checked:** Follower counts come from the public TikTok profile pages on 2026-10-05. Video dates come from the TikTok video ID, and play counts from the public video page on the same day. Recent YouTube uploads come from each channel's public RSS feed.
- **How I got "on-screen hook" and "what happens":** I sampled 6 to 8 frames of each public video and read the burned-in text overlays and captions. Rows marked *(caption only)* were not watched, so only the title or caption is confirmed.
- **What didn't pan out:** I found no agency-life or fitness-coach skit creator at a "standout" scale who was clearly active in 2025-26. Will Aitken, the best coach-who-sells-via-skits example, now has 0 public TikTok videos. Both are on the bench below. Agency owners and fitness coaches can apply the formats below directly.

## 1. Corporate Natalie (Natalie Marshall)

**Who she is**
- Notre Dame alum and ex-Deloitte consultant.
- Started in 2020 by satirising work-from-home; a "Day in the Life" video went viral.
- Forbes 30 Under 30 (2023) and LinkedIn Top Voice (2023).
- Co-hosted the *Demoted* podcast with Corporate Bro from January to August 2025.

**Her process**
- Ideas come from her own experience or from friends and followers, logged in a running notes page on her phone.
- Films twice a week to keep a backlog.
- Shoots in her home office or a borrowed office.
- Her stated aim: saying the thoughts "everyone is thinking but is too afraid to say."
- Engagement dropped when she strayed off-niche into makeup tutorials.

**Character:** dry, deadpan, politely passive-aggressive. Her polarity is against performative office culture (pro-WFH, anti-RTO theatre).

A [Sep 30, 2026 Short](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/umgYl2aJE8s) is a paid integration for Claude aimed at "small business owners" (*caption and description only*).

**How it connects to sales**
- The audience is corporate workers, which is sold to B2B brands (Dell, Adobe Acrobat, HR tech, AI tools).
- She sells speaking; one TikTok caption pitches her to marketing departments.
- She founded the creator-led agency Expand Co-Lab.
- She keeps sponsors "organic, on-brand" by placing the product inside the skit.

**What a coach can borrow**
- "[Type of person] who [behaviour]:" with the POV camera.
- "Using [industry lingo] in real life."
- Quoted promise, then "The reality:".
- Green-screen Notes list "What your ___ says about you."
- A notes-app idea bank and batch filming twice a week.

**What NOT to copy**
- Pure relatability with no path to an offer. Her money comes from brand deals, which a coach can't count on.
- Punching down at a generation. A Gen Z feedback clip from *Demoted* drew backlash in 2024, and your buyers may be that generation.
- Drifting off-niche.

**Dream follower ∩ ICP:** corporate women roughly 25-40 who are fed up with office theatre. That is the buyer for career coaches and "escape corporate" business coaches.

## 3. Erika Kullberg

**Who she is**
- Former corporate attorney who founded Plug and Law.
- Started with a bet to post daily for six months; NBC reported 3.9M followers in her first month.

## Fill-in templates (each tagged with its stage and its match in the existing 22-format catalog)

**T1. "[Type] who [behaviour]:" POV doorway monologue** (from Natalie #2; catalog: POV / types of clients)
- On-screen text: "[Type of person in my buyer's world] who [one exaggerated behaviour]:"
- Shot: phone at desk height as "you." Character leans in, 4-6 lines, ends on a line that escalates.
- Coach example (business coach): "Client who says they're 'not ready to raise prices' while fully booked:"
- Lines: "I just don't want to scare anyone," "I'll raise it next year," "I can't believe I'm working Saturdays again."
- Stage: Likable. CTA: a comment prompt ("Tag the friend who's fully booked and still undercharging").
- VN twist: "Học viên nói 'em chưa sẵn sàng tăng giá' trong khi đã kín lịch:"

**T2. "Using [industry lingo] in real life"** (Natalie #3; catalog: me explaining X to Y)
- Text: "Using [coaching / agency / gym] lingo in real life."
- Shot: 4-5 home micro-scenes, one phrase each, ending on a blunt mash-up line.
- Coach example (agency owner): "Let's put a pin in dinner," "Can you send me revisions on these dishes?", "Scope creep, babe."
- Stage: Likable (in-group belonging).

**T3. Quoted promise, then "The reality:"** (Natalie #1; catalog: expectation vs reality)
- Text: "'[Advice people repeat to my buyer]'" then "The reality:"
- Shot: a single 5-10s visual payoff with no dialogue.
- Coach example (fitness coach): "'Just eat less and move more'" then "The reality:" (standing at an open fridge at 11pm after a 10-hour workday).
- Stage: Likable. It builds your polarity, so pick a side.

**T4. Green-screen Notes list "What your ___ says about you"** (Natalie #5; catalog: green screen)
- Text: "What your [buyer behaviour] says about you:" over a phone Notes list of 5 items, one verdict each, with the last item funniest.
- Coach example (career coach): "What your LinkedIn headline says about you:" (Passionate professional / Ex-[Big Co] / Open to work / Emojis only / Your actual result).
- Stage: Likable to Admirable. Pin a comment: "Drop yours, I'll rate it."

**T5. "We're [buyer identity]. We…" walk-and-talk self-roast** (Corp #1)
- Shot: walking selfie with 5 stereotype confessions about your shared identity. End on an interruption ("my client's calling").
- Coach example (coach for coaches): "We're coaches. We tell clients to rest and answer DMs at midnight. We have 4 unfinished courses…"
- Stage: Likable. Roast yourself and the shared identity, never the buyer.

**T6. Paper-label multi-role skit "[Event] be like:"** (Corp #4; catalog: skits)
- Props: paper signs (CLIENT, CLIENT'S SPOUSE, INNER CRITIC, YOU). One person plays every role with hard cuts.
- Coach example (business coach): "Discovery calls be like:" The client says "Love it!", the Spouse sign says "We need to think about it," the Inner Critic sign says "Who do you think you are?", and You say "So… next steps?"
- Stage: Likable. Teaches the objection without lecturing.

**T7. "Real [situation] sent in by a follower" reenactment** (Corp #3)
- Text: "This is a real [DM / email / call] sent to me by a [community name]." (anonymised, with permission)
- Shot: play both sides, end with your one-line fix.
- Coach example (consultant): "A real client email sent in by a Profit Pirate": "Can you just do a quick 15-min audit, unpaid?" Fix: the reply they should send.
- Stage: Likable to Admirable. Series name plus submission CTA.

**T8. Gatekeeper vs insider ("they don't know that I know")** (Erika #1/#3; catalog: skits)
- Beats: the gatekeeper says no, a whisper aside ("watch this"), the specific rule or tactic, the gatekeeper caves, then "Who told you?" and "[Your name / guide]."
- Coach example (career coach): the recruiter says "That's the top of the band," the aside is "she doesn't know I've seen the posted range," and the candidate negotiates. "Who taught you that?" "The free salary script in my bio."
- Stage: Admirable. Every claim must be accurate. Use generic uniforms, not real logos.

**T9. Two paths with an on-screen counter** (Erika #2)
- Text: a counter at the top (revenue / kg / hours / salary). Person A (wig) takes the slow, common path; Person B takes your method. Jump in time, then "Who told you about this?"
- Coach example (fitness coach): "Hours spent at the gym" vs "progress." A does random workouts 6 days a week; B does 3 structured sessions plus sleep.
- Stage: Admirable.

**T10. "SAY THIS TO [authority]! FROM A [profession]"** (Mike #1)
- Shot: selfie. Their line in a blue bubble, your scripted reply in a green bubble, then 2-3 numbered rules. Under 20 seconds.
- Coach example (career coach): "SAY THIS TO YOUR BOSS! FROM A CAREER COACH." Boss: "Can you take this on too?" You: "Happy to. Which current priority should I deprioritise?"
- Stage: Admirable.
- VN twist: "NÓI CÂU NÀY KHI SẾP NHẮN 11 GIỜ ĐÊM!"

**T11. "Bet you thought this was [unprofessional / wrong]!" myth reversal** (Mike #3)
- Shot: a rapid list of 4 items, with an optional recurring "skeptic" foil character.
- Coach example (consultant): "Things you think are unprofessional that are totally fine: raising prices mid-contract with notice, saying no to scope creep, a 48h reply time…"
- Stage: Admirable.

**T12. "Are you a real ___?" credibility reply** (Mike #2)
- Shot: reply-to-comment sticker, "YES.", one proof, one funny beat.
- Coach example: "'Are you even a real coach?' Yes. 9 years, 300 clients, and here's the one thing I got wrong in year one."
- Stage: Admirable (a trust repair).

**T13. "N signs you're [state my buyer is in]"** (Jeff #2/#3)
- Shot: one fixed set with your product visible, numbered talking-head list under 75 seconds.
- Caption: free resource, then low-price offer, then community.
- Coach example (business coach): "5 signs you've outgrown doing everything yourself."
- Stage: Likable to Admirable. The easiest format for non-actors.

**T14. "N questions to ask your [partner / team / client]"** (Jeff #5)
- Coach example (leadership coach): "5 check-in questions to ask your team this Friday."
- Stage: Likable. Makes a highly saveable post.

## Guardrails mapped to the founder's rules

- **Right buyer:** every hook names a moment from the buyer's world. Natalie wins with corporate archetypes; Corp's best numbers are sales-identity jokes.
- **Character-led:**
  - Each creator has fixed traits and a polarity: dry anti-RTO (Natalie), "revenue is god" (Corp), consumer vs corporation (Erika), know-your-rights (Mike), pro-boundaries (Jeff).
  - The money-flex and generic-humour posts are their weak spots: Corp's movie remixes reached about 15K plays against millions for the identity jokes.
- **Stage fit:**
  - Likable: T1-T7, T14.
  - Admirable: T8-T12.
  - T13 bridges the two.
- **Gaps for the pack:** no verified agency-life or fitness-coach standout. Adapt T1, T5, T6 and T9 to those niches.
