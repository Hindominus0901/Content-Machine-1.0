## 1. The protocol's core, as the founder wrote it

### 1.1 Purpose: demand → product → bridge, through root-cause insights

> "we are not trying to navigate through all that we're trying to channel demand into our products so we need to know what is the demand here what is the product here and how can we build a bridge" (P1)

> "we try to seek patterns because if we know and understand the patterns we can leverage on that" (P1)

Research finds the **root cause** behind the audience's patterns: why they behave, say and think the way they do. That root is the insight. It lets demand that already exists be channeled into the product. (P1, P4)

| Word | Meaning (L) | Finance example (P2) |
|---|---|---|
| Pattern | Something many people in the audience do, say, feel or believe, seen in more than one place | "Money is just for covering expenses" |
| Why | The reason behind a pattern, one level down | They were never shown money doing anything else |
| Root | The deepest why that still explains the pattern, specific to this audience | A mindset: money is for survival, not for growth |
| Insight | A root a copywriter can build on, which the audience would recognize but has never put into words | "You don't have a money problem; you have a money-flow problem" |
| Demand | What the audience already wants, in their own words | A life that feels secure and better |
| Bridge | How the product connects the demand to the root | Learn to deploy money flow, and life gets better |

**Research is not:**
- an internet search and a list of references (P4);
- collecting everything (P2);
- a summary of what competitors say about the audience (L).

> "most researching agents they just pull up some internet search some references some website and they see that as done but that is not done" (P4)

**What counts as good:**

> "how do i know it's good i know this is when they give me lots of ideas and insights those that i can use to leverage on my content copy and um, any something that differentiate" (P1)

### 1.2 Before starting: inputs, the checklist and the scope

**Inputs from the client (P1):**
- the offer: what it is, what it does, the price;
- who buys today, and who they wish bought;
- the client's own read on why people buy and why they don't;
- any primary material: call notes, emails, reviews, surveys (L).

If the client can't be reached, start from the public offer page and write "no client input" at the top of the brief.

**The checklist, filled in before searching:**

> "we need to know demography, psychography, behaviorography, and uh, culture. Something like because like macro stuff, uh, and also the standards on what would the research cover." (P3)

| Layer | What to fill in (L) |
|---|---|
| Demographics | Age range, gender split, location, income or company size, role, life or business stage |
| Psychographics | Values, beliefs, identity ("I am the kind of person who…"), fears, desires, frustrations, what they're proud of |
| Behavior | What they do about the problem today; what they've tried and why it failed; how they buy; where they spend time; what triggers a search |
| Culture (macro) | Norms of their world; their language; economic, social and technology forces; what is taboo to say |

The context (P1, P2; the fields are L):
- **Market:** size and direction; how aware they are; what they've heard too many times.
- **Product:** what it does; the mechanism; price and terms; the proof that exists.
- **Company:** who they are; what they're known for; what they can and can't promise.
- **Competitors:** 3–5 named, each with its promise, price, angle, what its ads and pages say, and what it doesn't do.

**The scope (P2):** write **3–5 questions this research must answer**, before any searching. Each question names who will use the answer. Example: "Why do owners keep referrals as their only channel? (for the ad hook)" (L).

### 1.3 Find the audience first, then use the best source available

> "we need to know where the audience who are the audience first and then we'll decide we know we, then we know where they would sit" (P1)

**Step 1, find the audience (P1):**
1. List the people who have the problem.
2. Pick the one most likely to buy at this price (L).
3. Pick **3–5 places** where they clearly talk: social media (P5), communities, reviews, and comments under competitors' ads and posts.
4. Read a little in each place first. Drop places full of sellers, students or bystanders (L).

**Step 2, source order:**

> "i will actually talk to client if we cannot we will talk to some audience if we cannot then the first thing i do is that i will go with some comments of post in certain topics that relating to that" (P1)

> "besides secondary research, primary research is good as well, but if they are none, then secondary research should be enough." (P4)

1. **Primary, the client.** Ask: who are your best customers and why did they buy? What do they say on the first call? Why do people say no? What have they tried before you? (L)
2. **Primary, the audience.** Ask: what happened that made you look for help? What have you tried? What would make this solved for you? Why do you think it is still a problem? (L)
3. **Secondary, comments and posts.** Search in their words, not the industry's (L):
   - "[problem] + I tried"
   - "why do I [problem]"
   - "[problem] + frustrated"
   - "anyone else [problem]"
   - "[competitor] + worth it"

> "social media is uh, where we can take a lot of data and stuff from there are also many places that you can get those right and it's actually easy to find if you know" (P5)

**Capture rules (L).** Capture every item the same way:
- the **exact words**, copied, never paraphrased;
- **the link and the date**;
- **the place**;
- **who is speaking, by role only** (never a name or username).

Follow the house rules on privacy and site terms. The relevant ones from house-rules.md:
- People quoted are pseudonyms.
- Nothing is invented: a missing fact becomes `[NEEDS: …]`.
- In copy, buyer quotes are paraphrased, never printed as quotes.
- A testimonial needs written permission.

### 1.4 The why loop: the heart of the work (P4)

> "instead of like we trying to dig up lots of dirt we will ask what below all those dirt … And then we will keep on asking why, why, why until we find the root cause of everything." (P4)

> "we need to know where to find what to find how to find and then we need to compile all those into one question why are those happening and then from that we keep on where to find what to find how to find and keep on keep on the cycle until we cannot pull anything else and we analyze it out and we ask more and more to seek the most truthfully truth" (P4)

**The six steps of each turn:**
1. **Plan:** where to look, what to look for, and how. (P4)
2. **Collect and find patterns:** what people do, feel and think when they meet the problem, the product or the outcome. Look hardest for the **common beliefs that hold them back**. (P1, P2)
3. **Check each pattern:** keep it only if it shows up **in more than one place, from different people**. One post, one viral thread or one variable is not enough. Note any evidence against it (L). (P3, P5)
   > "yes one variable isn't enough you, you should check the pattern shows up somewhere else" (P5)
4. **Ask why** the pattern happens, compiled into **one question**. (P4)
5. **Go back to step 1 with that question.** Look for the answer **in the audience's own words, not in an expert's opinion**. (P4; the second sentence is L)
6. **Analyze and conclude** once the loop stops. (P2)

**The four stop tests** (P4: "until we cannot pull anything else"; the tests are L):
- two turns in a row bring nothing new;
- the answer is specific: it would not be true of any audience;
- the root explains more than one pattern;
- asking why once more only gives a generic answer, such as "people want more money".

> "not content with some shallow and generic answer we need to know the root of the root" (P4)

**Shallow and root, side by side (L):**

| Shallow (not done) | Root (done) |
|---|---|
| "Owners want more clients" | "Owners don't trust any number they didn't define themselves, because every vendor counted in its own unit" |
| "People are bad with money" | "They learned that money is for survival, so spending it to grow feels like risk" |

**Write each loop down** as a chain: pattern → why → why → … → root, with where each step was seen. (L)

### 1.5 The agent qualifies its own work before the human sees it (P5)

> "a agent will qualify that from the perspective of a copywriter and researcher first and then it will present to me I want the last thing presenting to me must be in the most polished and accomplished way possible." (P5)

Read the brief twice and fix every "no".

**As a copywriter:**
- Does it give me many ideas I can use in content and copy? (P1)
- Does something here set this client apart from competitors? (P1)
- Could I write an ad hook from each insight? (L)

**As a researcher:**
- Does each insight go to the root, with its chain of whys written out? (P4)
- Does each pattern show up in more than one place, from different people? (P3, P5)
- Is anything shallow, generic, unchecked or contradicted? (P2, P4)
- Is every quote in the audience's own words, with its link? (L)
- Does it answer the 3–5 scope questions? (P2)

**Presenting:**
- The conclusion comes first, in plain words.
- Each insight stands on its own, with its chain and its evidence.
- No working notes and no jargon.
- Gaps are named in their own section.
- One clean document.

> "the part where human comes in is after all everything comes in and we need to qualify the results and outcome" (P3)

### 1.6 The brief format (playbook §5)

The sections, in order:
1. **The conclusion:** demand, product, bridge, in 3–5 sentences.
2. **Root-cause insights (3–5).** Each has a pattern with the places it was seen and links; the why → why → why chain; the root; what it means for content and copy (hook idea, what to say, what to avoid); and evidence against.
3. **Key takeaways:** 5–7.
4. **Voice of customer:** 10–20 lines, grouped by pattern.
5. **Context:** the market; competitors (promise, price, angle, and "what none of them say"); how people handle it today.
6. **The customer:** the four layers, answered.
7. **Still unknown:** each gap, and who can answer it.
8. **Sources:** each place, what was read there, and the date range.

The Content Machine version is in §5 below.

### 1.7 Time (P2, P5)

> "Time, it should take like less than an hour. Or an hour maybe. Because we have AI now" (P2)

> "you can go deeper than an hour. Depending on the complicated or complex um, nature of the problem" (P5)

- Simpler problems with a clear root (health, wealth): about an hour.
- Psychological, social or other human problems: as long as the loop needs.
- **"Depth decides when it is done, not the clock."**

### 1.8 Mistakes to avoid (P2, P4), and when to ask a human (L)

> "The common mistake is that they want to research everything and at the same time they don't want to research anything at all. … they do not seek if it is validating or not … they don't set up the standard checklist beforehand. And after that, they do not do any analytics. Or they they don't have any conclusion" (P2)

The mistakes:
- Researching everything and ending up with nothing.
- Not checking whether information is valid or usable.
- No checklist before starting.
- No analysis afterwards.
- No conclusion.
- Stopping at a search and a list of links.
- Being content with a shallow or generic answer.

Stop and ask a human when:
- the product, price or buyer is unclear;
- the places where the audience talks block automated reading;
- two patterns contradict each other and the evidence doesn't settle it.

### 1.9 Lessons from the agency's own runs worth carrying over (L; from purpose.md, sop.md, backer-verifier, all superseded)

- **Public-only research failed.** Four public-only proof runs scored 13–17 of 20 and none passed; the lead rated the method 5.5/10. The fix the lead added on 2026-10-02 was **3–5 conversations with real buyers**, because "public posts cannot stand in for them when the buyer is quiet online." For a coach, this means **their own clients are the strongest source**. The cheap primary tools in §4 matter more than any browsing.
- **Wrong-person quotes.** Backers the producer called verified failed on re-open: a vendor, a tool promoter, a poster with no stated role, people who said the opposite. **"Is the author the buyer?" is the most important keep test** in social listening.
- **One run covers one buyer.** "A URD written for three buyers is written for none."

## 2. What changes for a DIY coach in Claude or ChatGPT

### 2.1 Who plays which role [CM]

| Founder's protocol | Content Machine |
|---|---|
| The CEO (qualifies at the end) | **The coach** (a 10-minute sign-off) |
| The client (primary source #1) | **The coach**: their offer, what clients say on calls and in DMs, plus "before any calls" hypotheses |
| The audience (primary source #2) | **The coach's clients, past clients, warm leads, and people who DMed but didn't buy** |
| Secondary | **Social listening + competitor scan** (Browse mode or Paste mode) |
| The research agent | Claude or ChatGPT running the Research skill |

### 2.2 What stays exactly the same

- The purpose line: demand → product → bridge. The insight is the root.
- **The checklist before searching:** four customer layers, the context, and 3–5 scope questions. Each scope question names the piece of the machine that will use the answer.
- **Find the audience first, then 3–5 places.** One brief per buyer.
- **Source order:** coach → clients → secondary. Secondary alone is enough when there is no primary.
- **Capture rules:** exact words, link and date, place, role only.
- **The why loop:** six steps, the >1-place-and-different-people check, and the four stop tests. The chain is always written down.
- **Self-qualification** as copywriter and researcher before the coach sees anything. The conclusion comes first, and gaps are named.
- The mistakes list. "Depth decides when it is done, not the clock."
- Nothing invented: anything inferred is tagged `[AI inference]`, and anything missing is `[NEEDS: …]`.

### 2.3 What gets simpler (Quick mode, the default) [CM]

| Founder or agency version | Quick mode for the coach |
|---|---|
| Checklist filled in by the lead | The AI pre-fills it from the coach interview and marks every field **known / hypothesis / unknown**. The coach confirms in about 3 minutes. |
| 3–5 competitors with ads and pages | **3 alternatives the buyer actually compares the coach to.** These include books, YouTubers, courses, doing it alone and doing nothing. |
| 3–5 places | **3 places** (Browse mode or Paste mode) |
| 3–5 insights, 10–20 VoC lines | **3 insights, 10+ VoC lines** (v1). The brief grows weekly. |
| Lint scripts, pseudonym ranges, commits | A **privacy self-check** printed at the end of every output, with pseudonyms S-/C-/V-001. The coach's Research Bank (Notion or Sheet) replaces the repo. |
| The CEO re-opens 5 random Chrome lines | **The coach re-opens 3 random quotes** before signing off. |
| The backer verifier (author history, six questions) | The **keep test on the post itself:** the role must be stated there. Seller, seeding and bot accounts are discarded. |
| The hour as one sitting | **Research Sprint** (about 60–90 minutes of the coach's time in setup week), then a **Research Drip** of 10 minutes a week. The loop keeps turning on the weekly cadence, so depth comes from time rather than from one long session. |

### 2.4 What moves to optional Deep mode [CM]

Deep mode is for coaches in psychological, social or relationship niches, which the founder says need longer, and for coaches before a launch. It adds:

- **3–5 live 5-whys calls** with clients (§4b). This is the agency's own lesson.
- **The competitor scan batch** (§6.3): Meta Ad Library longest-running ads, competitor pages, and 1–2★ reviews, ending in "what none of them say" and "what they're unwilling to do".
- **5+ places**, at least 2 of them on different platforms.
- **3+ independent people per insight.** The founder's rule is ">1 place, different people"; this stricter bar comes from the superseded framework.md §5.
- **Awareness and sophistication diagnosis:** what they've heard a hundred times becomes the "don't say" list.
- **UMP/UMS candidates:** the named hidden cause and the named new fix. These become signature-mechanism candidates.

### 2.5 Three starting situations [CM]

| Situation | Route |
|---|---|
| **A. Has clients and records** (DMs, call recordings, intake forms) | Transcript mining first (§4b), then social listening to fill gaps |
| **B. Has clients, no records** | Buyer Mirror form and the short survey (async, sent on Day 0), plus 1–2 short calls, then social listening |
| **C. No clients yet** | The "before any calls" interview. Every answer is labeled **HYPOTHESIS**, and social listening tests the 3 most important first. Prospect DMs and comments count as primary. The brief says "no client input" at the top, as the playbook requires. |

### 2.6 Time budget for 1–2 hours a week [CM]

| When | Coach's time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Day 0 | 15 min | Coach interview by voice (§4a). Survey and Buyer Mirror sent (§4b). |
| Days 1–3 | 20–30 min | Browse batch supervised, or Paste-mode collection (§6) |
| Day 3–7 | 15 min | Survey answers pasted and mined. Brief v1 delivered. Coach qualifies it. |
| Weekly (inside the Friday review) | 10 min | Paste new DMs, comments and call notes into the VoC bank. The AI advances one open why question. |
| Monthly re-plan | 20–30 min | One full loop turn on the hottest open pattern. Brief v+1. |
| Quarterly / pre-launch | 60 min | Deep mode refresh: competitor scan plus a re-run |

**Tension with "first win in session 1".** The founder says marketing starts with research. The machine also promises a ready-to-film piece in the first session. Proposal: Day 0 produces a **Quick-start brief**, labeled as hypothesis, from the interview plus one 20-minute browse or paste pass. That is enough to script week 1. Brief v1, built on survey data, then locks weeks 2–4 of the 30-day plan. *This needs the founder's decision.*

### 2.7 Three tool modes [CM]

1. **Browse:** Claude in Chrome reads pages in the coach's own logged-in browser (§6.1).
2. **Search-assist:** the AI's web search builds a reading list and click-ready search links. The coach opens them. This works in ChatGPT and in Claude without Chrome.
3. **Paste:** the coach copies comments, or takes cropped screenshots, into labeled paste blocks, and the analysis prompt does the rest (§6.4).

ChatGPT users will mostly use modes 2 and 3; the plan already rates its agent mode as unreliable. In Vietnam, most buyer talk lives in Facebook groups, TikTok and Zalo, so **Paste mode is the realistic default for the VN edition**.

## 3. How research outputs feed the rest of the machine

| Research output | Feeds | Rule [CM] |
|---|---|---|
| **Lexicon:** their nouns, verbs and repeated phrases, each with a count of different people | **Signature keywords** | A candidate must be said by 3+ different people (or by the coach's own clients) in 2+ places. It must name something the coach can own: a framework, mechanism, enemy or identity. It must **not** already be owned by a competitor (check the competitor grid). The root insight's own wording is often the best candidate: the UMP gets a name for the hidden cause, the UMS a name for the fix. The coach picks 1–3, and the Edge Check then enforces them. |
| **Buyer Mirror answers;** "why me" and its 5 whys; the beliefs, prejudices and enemies the audience blames; the culture layer's taboos | **Character Core** | "Why me over others" says which trait, value or principle to amplify. The audience's own blamed old way becomes the coach's **enemy**, so the polarity is grounded in what buyers already resent rather than invented. Taboos ("what is taboo to say") become "say the quiet truth" stances. |
| **Patterns** (limiting beliefs) → **roots** → **insights** | **Belief-shift chain** (the dominoes) | Each pattern becomes one belief-shift piece: "you think X (pattern) → because Y (why) → really Z (root) → so W (bridge)". Tag each as a vehicle, internal or external false belief. **The root that explains the most patterns (stop test 3) is the Big Domino candidate** and anchors the series. Each signature belief appears 3+ times in 30 days. |
| VoC lines tagged **scene**, trigger, pain L2/L3, horror story; "day in the life" | **Recognition Bank** (entertainment) | Every concrete scene (time, place, object, what was said) becomes a moment. Tags: buying stage, emotion, filter strength. Only moments from the **buyer's** world pass the Buyer Filter. Never mock an identifiable person. |
| **Objections** (ranked by number of different people), **failed solutions and horror stories**, **trigger events**, **what they paid before**, **buying criteria** | **Converting + Launch module** | One objection becomes one MOFU/BOFU piece and one launch belief-shift post: "an objection on a call = content failed to do its job". Horror stories become "why everything else failed" and case-study series. Triggers become the "why now" and the launch timing. What they paid before informs price and slots. Buying criteria become the proof needed, and gaps raise a **proof-gap alert**. |
| **Demand in their words** (desires, plus surface L1 pain) | **Lead magnets and comment-keyword bait posts** | The lead magnet promises the surface demand in their words and quietly delivers the bridge to the root. The comment keyword can be a signature keyword. |
| **Money phrases** (vivid lines with heat); each insight's hook idea | **Hook bank** | Phrases from public posts are reused as *language*, never presented as a testimonial (house rule 3). Clients' words are quoted only with written permission. Pain L2 lines suit pain hooks ("hook the pain, not the person"). The awareness and "heard too many times" list feeds the don't-say list. |
| **The 3 roots and the patterns they explain** | **Pillars** | Each root is an intellectual territory, giving one pillar and one weekly 20–40 min pillar recording. The guided-interview questions for the recording are the chain's why questions: "Why do [buyer] keep [pattern]?" The coach answers on camera, which is authentic and un-fakeable. |
| **Competitor grid:** "what none of them say" and "unwilling to do" | **Edge Check (Value, Character & Polarity)** | "Only-you-can-say-it" is tested against the grid. A script that repeats a competitor's overused claim is flagged. |
| A **research digest** of about 120 words | **Brand Brief** (≤600 words) for ChatGPT Tasks and Claude scheduled tasks | Tasks can't read Project files. The digest carries: the conclusion, 3 insights in one line each, the signature keywords, the top 3 objections, 5 paraphrased money phrases, and the words to avoid. |

**One chain feeding the whole machine.** This persona is illustrative: invented, not data.
- **Buyer:** first-time tech managers.
- **Pattern:** "I just fix it myself, it's faster", seen in a survey answer, a YouTube comment and a forum thread.
- **Why chain:** they don't trust the team's output → their worth was always measured by their own output → nobody showed them what a manager's output is.
- **Root:** they still score themselves by what their own hands produce, so delegating feels like disappearing.
- **Insight:** "You're not bad at delegating; you're scared of becoming invisible."

What that one chain produces:
- **Signature keyword candidate:** "invisible work".
- **Pillar:** the player-coach trap.
- **Belief-shift post:** "Delegating isn't losing your edge."
- **Recognition moment:** "9pm, quietly rewriting your report's PR."
- **Lead magnet:** "The 1-page 'what my output is now' scorecard."
- **Objection piece:** "'I don't have time to train them' — here's the math."

## 4. Primary research for a coach (EN + VN)

### 4a. Interviewing the coach (about 15 minutes by voice; one question at a time; written in their words)

**AI rules:**
- Write the answers word for word; don't summarize while listening.
- Skip what's already known.
- Label each answer **CLIENT-SAID** (they're recalling what a client said) or **COACH-BELIEF** (it's the coach's view).
- At the end, mark the **3 answers that most need checking against social listening**. These are tested first.

**Part A: the offer in five lines**

| # | EN | VN |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What do you sell, in one sentence? Format, length, price. | Bạn đang bán gì, nói gọn trong một câu? Hình thức, thời lượng, giá. |
| 2 | Who is it for, and who is it NOT for? | Gói này dành cho ai, và không dành cho ai? |
| 3 | How does it work: your steps, or what you do differently? | Cách bạn làm gồm những bước nào? Điều gì khác với cách người khác đang làm? |
| 4 | What proof do you have: results, numbers, stories, feedback clients allowed you to use? | Bạn đang có bằng chứng gì: kết quả, con số, câu chuyện, phản hồi khách đã cho phép dùng? |
| 5 | Who or what do clients compare you to: other coaches, courses, books, YouTube, doing it alone, doing nothing? | Khách thường so sánh bạn với ai hoặc cái gì: coach khác, khoá học, sách, kênh YouTube, tự mày mò, hay cứ để đó? |

**Part B: the buyers** (adapted from interview-guide.md)

| # | EN | VN |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Who buys fastest? Describe the last two people who said yes quickly: their situation, and what had just happened to them. | Ai là người chốt nhanh nhất? Kể về 2 người gần nhất đồng ý nhanh: hoàn cảnh của họ, chuyện gì vừa xảy ra với họ. |
| 2 | What do they say in the first two minutes of a call, or in their first DM? Exact phrases if you remember. | Trong 2 phút đầu buổi gọi, hoặc tin nhắn đầu tiên, họ hay nói gì? Nhớ được nguyên câu thì càng tốt. |
| 3 | What had they already tried before you? What did it cost, and how did it end? | Trước khi tìm đến bạn, họ đã thử những gì? Tốn bao nhiêu, và kết cục ra sao? |
| 4 | Which objection loses you the most sales? Word for word. | Lời từ chối nào làm bạn mất nhiều khách nhất? Họ nói nguyên văn thế nào? |
| 5 | Why did the last two people who didn't buy say no? The reason they gave, and the reason you think it really was. | Hai người gần nhất không mua: lý do họ đưa ra là gì, và theo bạn lý do thật là gì? |
| 6 | Who else is in the decision: a spouse, parent, partner, boss? What do they ask? | Ngoài họ ra còn ai cùng quyết định: vợ/chồng, bố mẹ, cộng sự, sếp? Những người đó hay hỏi gì? |
| 7 | When you ask what a really good year looks like, what do they say, in their words? | Khi bạn hỏi "một năm thật tốt" trông như thế nào, họ trả lời ra sao, bằng chính lời của họ? |

```
   every QUOTE is 15 words or fewer; every NOTE is labeled.
```

### 6.2 Why-loop turn N (Browse or Paste)

```
Why loop, turn [N]. Same buyer, same safety and keep rules as before.
The question: [WHY QUESTION from last turn].
Look for the ANSWER in the buyer's own words, not in experts, coaches or articles.
Places: [..]. Search phrases (their words): [..].
Keep lines that explain WHY, not more examples of the pattern.
At the end:
(a) The chain so far: pattern → why → why → … each step with its IDs and places.
(b) The four stop tests, each PASS or FAIL with a reason:
    1. two turns in a row brought nothing new
    2. the answer is specific: it would not be true of any audience
    3. the root explains more than one pattern (name them)
    4. asking why once more only gives a generic answer (write that generic answer)
(c) If they pass: the root, and the insight in one line the buyer would recognize but has never said.
    If not: the next WHY question and where to look.
```

**How to read the stop tests [CM interpretation; flag for the founder].** The playbook lists the four tests but doesn't say whether all must pass. Proposed reading:
- **Done:** tests 2 + 3 + 4 pass.
- **Saturated:** test 1 alone means you're out of material, not that you've found the root. Write the chain as OPEN and name the gap.
