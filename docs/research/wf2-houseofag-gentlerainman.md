# A) ALEX GARCIA — "House of AG" (@house.of.ag)

### 1) Identity and business model
- **Background:**
  - Former college football player at FIU and Texas State (didn't graduate).
  - Ran a social-media agency for Austin bars and restaurants on Sixth Street.
  - Worked at The Hustle (on its podcast and paid acquisition), then Gumroad, then The Kollective (an Austin gym), where he ran its social shows.
  - Grew X/Twitter from about 100 to 40K+ followers with "50 threads in 50 days" (his own claim, podcast ep. 018).
- **Platforms:**
  - IG @house.of.ag (about 207k) and @alexgarcia_atx.
  - YouTube @AlexGarciaBuilds (9.09K subscribers). Tagline: "building a billion dollar company while training to win the CrossFit games."
  - Sweat Equity podcast with Brian Blum (124 episodes since October 2023).
  - Marketing Examined newsletter on beehiiv, which claims "200k founders, marketers, and creators".
- **What he sells:**
  - **Agency:** "We're currently creating 20+ social shows for brands" (apply at /brand-show). Named clients include JockoFuel and TYR Wodapalooza; he targets 7–10 figure brands.
  - **6-week creative strategy bootcamp**, limited to 10 brands, booked via a Calendly call.
  - **Cut30**, a 30-day short-form bootcamp he co-runs with Colin Landforce and Oren John. "Nearly 3,000 students", 100% refund if you complete it and aren't satisfied.
  - **Greenscreen 101**, a $97 course (listed against $197).
  - A free 3-day "First 10K Views" challenge and a free idea tool.
  - Newsletter sponsorships (beehiiv, Motion, Richpanel).
- **Audience:** product, DTC and lifestyle brand founders and marketers, plus creators. Cut30 explicitly also targets experts, service businesses and personal brands.

### 2) Content philosophy
- "Social media is the new TV, and there are endless channels." Stop playing "the algorithmic lottery" hoping one video blows up. Build **social shows / signature series** instead.
- His chain of reasoning: **shows create familiarity, familiarity creates expectations, and meeting expectations repeatedly forms a habit.** This is very close to the founder's "dominoes" belief.
- "Content is just communication at scale." Every piece must build a **narrative** in the viewer's mind.
- "The aim should never be volume. The aim should always be impact."
- Distribution has shifted from the social graph to the **interest graph**: "specificity is the strongest signal you can send." "Distribution is the castle, storytelling is the moat, and content is the bridge in."
- Cut30's line: "There is no such thing as a boring business, just boring content."
- On reach content: top-of-funnel must be relatable, "Holy shit, that's so me." Show the aspirational journey, but ground it in a context the viewer shares ("There are way more busy dads than CrossFit bros").

### 3) Content system
- **Narrative**, then **3 content pillars**, each pillar tied to a **feeling**, then every idea passes through **brand filters**, then **feedback retros** (weekly, bi-weekly or monthly).
- **Funnel mix** (from his time at The Kollective): **60% top-of-funnel** (wide reach), **30% middle** (valuable, with product placement), **10% bottom** (drives a specific action). The split shifts with each quarter's goal.
- **Series test.** Every series idea must pass three questions:
  1. Can we repeat it week after week?
  2. Can it scale for months?
  3. Can we ship it fast?
  - His proof case is "Cold Hard Truth": rapid-fire questions asked to someone in a cold plunge. It was The Kollective's best performer "by a landslide".
- **Show levels:**
  - *Beginner:* challenges, interviews, trivia, man-on-the-street. Low pre- and post-production.
  - *Intermediate:* more characters, product integration.
  - *Advanced:* docu-series, reality, game shows.
  - Each recurring show keeps four things consistent: **location/set, theme, format, character**. His examples are Hot Ones, and the Brooklyn Coffee Shop account, which reached 52k followers on 29 episodes.
- **Cadence and testing** (Cut30 co-founders):
  - "Publish 100 videos" to find **content-market fit**: content you like to make, that the algorithm favors, and that the audience consumes.
  - Spend 80% of effort scaling what works and 20% testing new topics and formats.
  - Test each new category 2–4 times before scaling or ditching it.
  - Roughly 1 in 10 videos becomes a hit, "it's math."
- **Production workflow:**
  - Shoot event content as "one big shoot" for evergreen pillars.
  - Example: TYR Wodapalooza used 4 shooters plus a dedicated **live editor** and produced 25+ pieces in 4 days, each turned around in about 1.5 hours.
  - Monthly "content sweeps" (full shoot days) built around collabs.
- **Repurposing and growth:**
  - Carousels repackage winning videos, and they can "go nuclear" because they enter the Reels algorithm.
  - Repost viral videos as Instagram **Trial Reels** shown only to non-followers. His example: a 1.3M-view video reposted got about 100k views and about 500 new followers.
  - IG collabs in three buckets: in-house talent (trainers/team), big-name members/customers, outside influencers.
- **Choosing a level:** ask three questions about doubling volume in the next 30 days. Do operations break? Does quality drop? Does the team's skill match? Pass 2 of 3 and you move up a level. Pass only 1 and you spend 30–60 days building infrastructure first. His summary: "Execute optimistically. Strategize pessimistically."

### 4) Signature frameworks, with coach templates
**a) Social Narrative.** Your narrative sits where Mission, What You Believe and What People Will Rally Behind overlap, stated in 3–5 words. His example is a car detailer whose narrative is "Ride With Confidence".
> Coach template: "My mission is to help [who] [outcome/feeling]. I believe [contrarian belief]. People rally behind [shared feeling or frustration]. → Narrative: '[3–5 words]'." Every pillar must prove this narrative without saying it outright.

**b) Feelings and Brand Filters.** The pipeline is Narrative → Content Idea → Feeling → Filter. Find the filters by answering, as a team: How should someone describe your account? How do you want it to position you? What are three words for your strategy? Turn the common answers into 1–2-word filters with a short definition. His JockoFuel example used "F*** Yeah", "Perspective Shift" and "Mentor-like". An idea that fails a filter is dropped or refined.
> Coach template: Pillar 1 [___] = feeling [___]; Pillar 2 [___] = [___]; Pillar 3 [___] = [___]. Filters: "[Filter 1]: does this make them [reaction]?", "[Filter 2]: …", "[Filter 3]: …".

**c) Signature Series / Social Show.** From his YouTube descriptions, the three parts are:
1. A repeatable concept that scales with minimal friction.
2. A uniqueness factor nobody can copy.
3. Character-driven content that builds affinity.

His full layered playbook goes: narrative → pillars → concept (a "four-element test", whose exact elements I could not verify) → repetitive elements that "turn familiarity into a hook" → format → set → characters → opening 3 seconds. The playbook itself sits behind his opt-in.
> Coach template (Cut30's premise sentence): "In every episode, **[me / my client]** does **[repeatable action]** to **[specific subject]** for **[clear audience benefit]**." Examples: "Every Monday I rate one real [industry] sales page out of 10 so you know what to fix in yours." Or: "The Client Hotseat: 60 seconds, one real problem, one fix." Keep the set, opening line, title card and verdict format the same every episode.

**d) 7 Content Archetypes.**
1. The Resource (educational).
2. The Everyday Identity (lifestyle).
3. The Becoming (aspirational).
4. The 1% Better (inspiration).
5. The Voyager (documenter).
6. The Performer (entertainment).
7. The Make Belief (fictional).

Pick yours with three questions: What emotion should attach to the brand? How do you want to be perceived? What role do you play in the audience's life? Beginners pick **one** archetype and test formats until they find outliers.
> Coach default: Resource plus Voyager (documenting the journey of building clients' results), with Performer saved for reach.

**e) The 4 Level-1 formats** (from his "Content That Every Brand Should Make" issue; very likely the same four as his June 2026 "4 formats" video, though that is my inference):
1. **One-shot video:** a title hook (on-screen text) plus a visual hook. Lo-fi version: one scene with a text overlay. Hi-fi version: a constant action running alongside the text. Vignettes over-optimize the angle, the aesthetic or the action.
2. **Ten-shot video:** a quick story told across about 10 fast shots, either with a text overlay or with narration matched shot by shot. The title hook gives context and opens a curiosity gap that the visuals close.
3. **Carousel:** for followers, to repackage winners, and as a possible breakout.
4. **"How To / Why We Do It":** gather top comments, FAQs, comments on competitors' posts and AnswerThePublic questions. Answer each with your positioning, differentiation or contrarian take.

**f) Cut30's "What to post" engine.** Written by the Cut30 editorial team, not Alex personally.
- **Four idea engines:** story, rating, breakdown, scenario.
- **Choosing a post:** pick the job (reach, prove expertise, connect, make tangible, help buy), then the evidence, then the angle. Make it visible with an object or document, and attach a person.
- **Ten business post types:**
  1. Rate the choices customers compare.
  2. Break down a real example.
  3. Give a guided tour of hidden work.
  4. Answer a question through a specific scenario.
  5. Turn a result into an explanation.
  6. Tell a founder story built around a decision.
  7. Run a named series.
  8. Make a launch visible.
  9. Document progress around a mission.
  10. Post the direct offer, stating who it's for and who should skip it.

### 5) Hook and script structures
- "Script every video, but think in hooks and bullet points." The first 2–3 seconds set the hook; the bullets that follow are quick value hits.
- **Title hook plus visual hook.** Repeated show elements act as the hook. The opening 3 seconds is a designed layer of the show.
- **Example hook lines** (Cut30 article): "I install these every day. This is the version I would never buy." / "Here is what I would change about this quote before approving it." / "The customer request that made us stop selling our bestseller." / "Crocs shut down my business." / "We have 21 days to open this restaurant, and the oven still has not arrived."
- **Example outline 1, breakdown (greenscreen):**
  1. Put the evidence on screen, such as a public sales page.
  2. Give a judgment ("this loses buyers at line 2").
  3. Explain why.
  4. Say what it changes for the viewer.
  5. CTA: "send me yours".
- **Example outline 2, ten-shot narrated story:**
  1. Title hook: "The call that made me stop offering free sessions."
  2. Shot list mapped line by line to the script.
  3. Tension, then decision, then outcome.
  4. Close tied back to the narrative.
- **Measurement:**
  - *Engagement metrics* (views, likes, average watch time, replays) test whether hooks and structure work.
  - *Impact metrics* (save-to-like, share-to-like, comments, DMs) test whether it's the right content for the right people.
  - Oren John's winner rule is a 10:2 like-to-save ratio. He also asks whether a video would get shared in a work Slack channel.

### 6) How content turns into sales
- Each YouTube video links a **topic-matched playbook opt-in** (/show, /storytelling, /interestgraphs, /playbook). That feeds the newsletter.
- The newsletter monetizes through sponsorships, agency applications, the bootcamp (by call), Cut30 and the $97 course.
- Free lead magnets: the 3-day challenge and the idea tool, which asks for "a few sentences on your business".
- **Email-list playbook** (his video): build bridges from content to the newsletter, use a squeeze page (he claims conversion went from 20% to 70%), content collabs, "magic links" and referral programs. "Social is rented land; email is owned land."
- On-content CTAs are low-pressure: "apply", "get the playbook", plus "free game" audience submissions on the podcast.

### 7) What to take for Content Machine
1. **Series-first design.** Narrative → pillars → series premise sentence → fixed repeatable elements. This turns "dominoes" into a concrete method.
2. A **filter step** an AI can run on every generated idea: narrative fit, intended feeling, and 2–3 named filters.
3. The **60/30/10 funnel mix** as the default ratio. It maps to reach, trust and conversion.
4. The **archetype picker**, plus "pick one archetype as a beginner".
5. The **four idea engines** and **ten post types.** They are directly usable for a service business ("your raw material is diagnosis, judgment, process, decisions, scenarios, results").
6. **Title hook plus visual hook.** One-shot and ten-shot formats any phone user can make.
7. **Impact metrics** over vanity metrics. The "publish 100 / 80-20 / test each format 2–4 times" habit loop.
8. The **relatable top-of-funnel reframe exercise:** find the bigger, relatable version of your aspirational story.

### 8) What does NOT fit a DIY coach
- Brand-scale production: crews, a live editor, casting characters, sets, fictional "world-building" (the Make Belief archetype), docu-series.
- He wants volume ("publish 100") and data literacy. A solo coach needs a lighter minimum viable cadence.
- His examples are mostly DTC and product brands; adapting them to a solo service provider needs translation.
- Growth hacks like Trial Reels are platform-dependent and short-lived.
- Splitting off a dedicated interest-cluster account could dilute a solo coach's effort.

# B) MARK SATTERFIELD — "gentlerainman"

### 1) Identity and business model
- **Background:**
  - Marketing consultant since 1992, previously an executive at PepsiCo and Kraft.
  - Director of Career Services at Emory's business school; adjunct marketing lecturer at Georgia Tech.
  - BA in English Literature from WashU.
- **Books:** he claims 10–14. They include *The One Week Marketing Plan* (BenBella, 2014), *The Affluent Marketing Blueprint*, *Velvet Rope StorySelling*, *The Velvet Rope Playbook*, *The Luxury Agent Playbook*, *The Gilded Revival*, *The Power of Exclusivity*, *Unique Sales Stories* (2010) and *Power Prospecting* (2002).
- **Niche:** service professionals selling to **affluent clients**: wealth advisors, luxury real estate, consultants, concierge medicine, interior design, attorneys, private aviation, and coaches.
- **Offer ladder:**
  - Free book, *Affluent Marketing Blueprint* (you pay shipping).
  - Podcast and YouTube, both free.
  - **Whales Not Minnows** video newsletter and training vault: $97 a month, with paid programs bundled in.
  - Programs: Recognized Expert Formula ($295), Quiet Follow-Up System ($297), Velvet Rope Affluent Client Attraction System ($695).
  - **Private Strategy Session:** $495, booked through a questionnaire, a $150 refundable deposit and his personal review. Guarantee: "if not valuable, no charge."
  - **Platinum Coaching:** $4,000 for 3 months, or 3 payments of $1,500.
  - Corporate training from $12,500 a day, plus sales-page copywriting.
- **Scale:** small reach, high price.
  - YouTube: 9.33K subscribers. Recent episodes get **8–80 views**; his older best is about 14K.
  - TikTok: 471 followers.
  - **The Velvet Rope Playbook** has 321 episodes since August 12, 2025, published daily on weekdays. He variously claims it is the "#3", "#4" or "top 10" marketing podcast.

### 2) Content philosophy
- "I don't teach mass-market tactics. I don't believe in chasing leads." / "Stop chasing. Start attracting." / "Whales Not Minnows."
- "Visibility creates awareness, but trust creates appointments."
- He separates **marketing activities** from a **marketing system**. Every piece should have a specific job, and not every tactic needs to produce a client immediately.
- **Against "tip-based authority":** tips position you as a teacher to be compared. Clients pay for *judgment*: "clarity, certainty, perspective." Move "from explaining → interpreting, answering questions → reframing them."
- He calls Instagram's "hidden job" a **credibility check**. A referred prospect quietly looks you up and may never follow or like anything, so your profile should reinforce the referral.
- "Telling people you're an expert and allowing them to see your expertise are two very different things."
- He favors one central idea repeated through many stories (Rosser Reeves' USP) over constantly reinventing the marketing.

### 3) Content system
- **Format:** story-led podcast episodes of 15–20 minutes, published daily Monday to Friday, mirrored to YouTube as full videos. A short-form presence exists (TikTok and IG reels), but it's minor.
- **Recurring episode formula:**
  1. Open with a question or tension.
  2. Introduce **two named, contrasting characters**, typically composite or illustrative (e.g., "Sophie Laurent, estate-jewelry advisor" vs "Graham Holloway, marine insurance").
  3. Deliver one central lesson.
  4. Give 3–5 practical steps or numbers.
  5. Set a simple exercise.
  6. End with a CTA.
  - His stated promise: "walk away with at least one idea you can actually use."
- **Repurposing:** one useful idea becomes a video, email, social post, podcast and article. He frames this as escaping "Marketing Quicksand": build marketing around **one useful idea**, put it in front of the right people, and give **one logical next step**.
- **Long-form email nurture:** a twice-monthly briefing, built around one question about affluent buyers per issue.

### 4) Signature frameworks, with coach templates
**a) The five-stage marketing system** ("The Activity Trap"): **Attention, Relevance, Credibility, Differentiation, Follow-up.**
> Coach template: For each piece of content, label its job: "This post's job is [Attention / Relevance / Credibility / Differentiation / Follow-up] for [avatar] who is at [stage]." Then check each week that every stage has at least one piece.

**b) Credibility Library.** Write 15 content pieces that *demonstrate* rather than claim: client stories, specific examples, observations, and how you talk about a problem.
> Template: "5 client situations I've solved: [__]. 5 things I notice that others miss: [__]. 5 mistakes I'd never let a client make: [__]."

**c) The two persuasive stories:**
- **Cost of a Cheap Fix Story** addresses risk.
- **Peer Validation Story** addresses relevance.
> Template A: "[Client type] chose [cheaper/easier option]. Here's what it really cost them: [hidden cost]. What we did instead: [__]."
> Template B: "If you're a [peer profile] dealing with [specific situation], here's what [similar client] did and what changed: [__]."

**d) The "So What?" test.** After every feature statement, ask "so what?" until you reach the benefit the prospect cares about.
> Template: "I do [feature] → so what? → which means [benefit] → so what? → so you can [outcome they want]."

**e) Curiosity gap and headlines.** The gap is the space between what someone knows and what they now want to know. He uses a two-sentence engagement test. The headline triggers are curiosity, specificity, self-interest, loss aversion and the unexpected. "A great headline… simply has to earn the next 10 seconds."

**f) Lead magnet rules:**
- The **subject and promise matter more than the format**. He uses a 3-question test (questions not public).
- **Format ladder:** a low-friction PDF for first interest, video to show expertise and personality, a webinar to move the most interested toward a conversation.
- "Your lead magnet is not your marketing funnel." It has to plug into follow-up, messaging, authority-building and conversion.
- He teaches a "lead magnet headline formula", but the exact wording is only in the video.

**g) Quiet Follow-Up** ("The Waiting Game", "Follow up without being a pain"):
- Interest is not the same as readiness.
- Stop "just checking in". Send useful ideas, articles, case studies and observations, and use what the prospect already told you.
- Keep messages short and make the next step obvious.
- Use "when" language so the prospect can picture the outcome.
- Aim to "become more valuable while they wait."

**h) Curated Personality.** Show the parts of yourself that reveal how you think, what you believe and what working with you is like. It's fine to sometimes turn off the wrong prospect.

**i) Repositioning toolkit:**
- A flagship offering.
- "Credential projects".
- Changing the professional circles where people encounter you.
- Deleting marketing that reinforces your old positioning.
- A point of distinction that works as a "front door" to your broader services.

**j) Post-sale reassurance video sequence.** His example is his surgeon's pre- and post-op videos. Automated videos after purchase reduce uncertainty and drive referrals.

**k) The One Week Marketing Plan** (2014). A "set it and forget it", niche-tailored system you can build in about 5 business days for about $300. The publisher copy confirms only that summary. The day-by-day steps in the book are not verified here.

### 5) Hook and script structures
- **Hooks are almost always questions or contrarian reframes:**
  - "Is spending $10,000 to acquire one new client outrageously expensive or an absolute bargain?"
  - "Why should an affluent prospect believe you know what you're doing?"
  - "The riches aren't always in the niches."
  - "Why more leads may be the last thing you need."
- **Titles** use a branded label, a colon and a promise: "The Format Trap: …", "The Magnet Effect: …", "The Savile Row Strategy: …".
- **Example outline: a coach's version of his episode**
  1. Cold open with a question your buyer asks.
  2. "It depends…" reframe.
  3. Character A makes the common mistake.
  4. Character B takes the contrasting path.
  5. The turn, where the insight lands.
  6. Three practical moves.
  7. A ten-minute exercise.
  8. CTA: "Comment [KEYWORD]".
- **Example outline: short-form cut of the same episode (about 60 seconds)**
  1. Hook question.
  2. One-line story of the mistake.
  3. One reframe sentence in his "this isn't X, it's Y" pattern.
  4. One action.
  5. "Comment COACH".

### 6) How content turns into sales
- **CTAs:**
  - "**Comment COACH**", answered with information about working with him.
  - Two CTAs matched to readiness: **MORE** sends people to the general site, **CONFIDENCE** to a focused sales page. "The more specific the prospect's interest, the more focused the destination should be."
- **Path:** podcast or video → GetWealthyClients hub → free book → $97-a-month newsletter (which bundles $295–$297 programs to push the subscription) → $495 strategy session, gated by an application → $4K coaching.
- **Prospects apply:** qualification questions filter "tire-kickers". His "Authority Call" is a 3-part close (details not public).
- **Offline channels** he still recommends: intimate dinners for 10–12 rather than big sponsorships, a 3-letter direct-mail sequence, and referral prompts ("give clients something specific to talk about").

### 7) What to take for Content Machine
1. The **five-stage job labels** map cleanly onto the founder's two outcomes: Attention → Reach; Relevance, Credibility, Differentiation and Follow-up → Trust → Purchase.
2. **Judgment over tips.** This is the core recipe for converting content that looks educational ("Here's what matters, and here's what doesn't").
3. The **Credibility Library (15 demonstrations)** and the **two story types**, as fill-in generators.
4. The **"So What?" test** and the **curiosity two-sentence test** as built-in QA passes on every script.
5. The **story-episode template** (contrast characters → lesson → steps → exercise → keyword CTA), which works across podcast, long video, email and short clips.
6. **"Comment KEYWORD"** plus **MORE/CONFIDENCE** CTAs; the quiet follow-up email and DM scripts; the post-purchase reassurance sequence.
7. **Profile-as-credibility-check audit:** look at your bio, website and LinkedIn as someone who was just told "you should talk to this person."

### 8) What does NOT fit a DIY coach
- Daily 15–20-minute story episodes are not a reach engine; his own recent episodes get double-digit views. The pack should treat them as nurture and trust assets, not discovery.
- The affluent and luxury framing ("velvet rope", exclusivity) suits only premium coaches.
- He relies on referrals, direct mail and events.
- **Named composite characters** need an "illustrative story" label in the pack so users don't present fiction as real testimonials or proof.
- He has almost no native short-form craft (hooks, pacing, editing). For that, pair him with Alex Garcia and Cut30.

- **Alex Garcia** covers **reach and series design**: narrative, pillars, feelings and filters, signature series, formats, hooks and metrics.
- **Mark Satterfield** covers **trust and conversion**: each piece's job, demonstrating judgment, persuasive stories, copy tests, lead magnet and follow-up, and keyword CTAs.
