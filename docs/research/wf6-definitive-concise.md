# Brief: Definitive, concise, character-led voice (and how to detect the opposite)

Research only. No files were created or edited. Labels: **DATA** means peer-reviewed or empirical. **PRACTITIONER** means a writer's, creator's or editor's craft advice. **EDITORIAL** means my own synthesis or inference for the Content Machine.

---

## 1) Key insights

**I1. Confidence persuades, and audiences read confidence from wording. (DATA)**
- **Confidence heuristic.** Two fictional advisors got the same number of stock calls right. About two-thirds of participants still preferred the overconfident one and judged them more knowledgeable (Price & Stone 2004).
- **Answer first.** In paired decision tasks, the person holding the strong evidence usually proposed an answer first. That move may itself be the cue that persuades. The effect ran through words, not body language (Pulford et al. 2018).
- **Powerless speech.** Hedges, hesitations and tag questions lower credibility and persuasion. This holds in courtroom testimony (Erickson et al. 1978), in persuasion experiments (Blankenship & Holtgraves 2005) and in a meta-analysis (Burrell & Koper 1998, cited in Oba & Berger).
- **Certainty words lift engagement.** Brand posts with words like "always", "everything" and "forever" got more likes, comments and shares on Facebook and Twitter. The mechanism is that the brand seems more powerful. The effect is stronger for audiences with high power-distance beliefs (Pezzuti, Leonhardt & Warren 2021).
- **Present tense.** Present tense ("this works") persuades more than past tense ("this worked") because it signals certainty. Evidence: 8 studies plus NLP on more than 500k reviews (Packard, Berger & Boghrati 2023).

**I2. Calibration beats raw confidence once you are caught wrong. Be confident in delivery and honest about the odds. (DATA)**
- **Errors cost confident people more.** A wrong claim damages a confident witness more than an unsure one. After an error, the less confident witness can look more credible (Tenney et al. 2007; 2008).
- **People don't dislike uncertain advice.** Gaertig & Simmons (2018) ran 11 studies with about 4.8k participants. People did not penalize uncertainty expressed as probabilities, ranges or "more likely". What hurt was the advisor saying "I'm not sure", or saying "probably" after acting confident.
- **EDITORIAL rule: "Absolute about yourself, calibrated about the world."**
  - State values, standards and choices with no hedge at all.
  - State outcomes and predictions as ranges, conditions or odds, delivered confidently.

**I3. Not all hedges are equal. (DATA)**
Oba, Boghrati & Berger (*Journal of Consumer Psychology*) found two things:
- High-likelihood hedges ("probably", "likely", "I'd bet") beat low-likelihood ones ("might", "could", "possibly").
- Personal hedges ("I'd argue", "it seems to me") beat impersonal ones ("arguably", "it seems"), because owning the claim reads as confidence.

So "in my experience" is not poison. Impersonal, low-likelihood fog is ("it could potentially be argued…").

**I4. Expertise changes the dial. (DATA)**
Non-experts gain persuasion by expressing certainty. Recognized experts can gain attention by admitting a small doubt (Karmarkar & Tormala 2010).
- **EDITORIAL:** new coaches should go maximally definitive. Established names can afford one "here's where I might be wrong" line.

**I5. "Most people think X. They're wrong" works only if you refute X. (DATA)**
O'Keefe's meta-analysis (107 studies, about 20k participants) ranks them this way:
1. Two-sided messages that refute the other side persuade most and boost credibility.
2. One-sided messages come next.
3. Two-sided messages that raise the other side without refuting it come last.

A stance hook has to name the opposing belief and then take it apart with a reason and proof. This also matches Hormozi's "tell the whole truth" (founder reference).

**I6. Concise reads as smarter and is understood better.**
- **(DATA)** Needlessly complex wording lowers how intelligent readers judge the author to be (Oppenheimer 2006).
- **(DATA, older and single-sourced, so treat as directional)** American Press Institute figures: with average sentences under 8 words readers understood about 100%; at 14 words, about 90%; at 43 words, under 10%.
- **(PRACTITIONER)** Recommended averages:
  - Ann Wylie: about 15 words.
  - AP's Cappon: 16.
  - Jack Hart: 17 or less.
  - GOV.UK: cap at 25.
- **(PRACTITIONER)** The Heaths: "If you say three things, you don't say anything." Krug: cut half the words, then half of what's left.

**I7. Being definitive is not bragging, and fake humility is worse than either. (DATA)**
- Humblebragging lowers liking and perceived competence more than plain bragging, because it reads as insincere (Sezer, Gino & Norton 2018).
- Boasting persuades when trust cues are present and backfires without them (Packard, Gershoff & Wooten 2016).
- **EDITORIAL:** a strong claim needs a trust cue next to it: a number, a story or a receipt.

**I8. Certainty spreads; uncertainty pulls replies. (DATA, weaker)**
- In a 2026 preprint (about 2.3k English X posts), uncertain posts got about 82% more replies. This replicates an earlier Arabic-language study (Soufan 2026; arXiv 2603.00082).
- An older LIWC analysis found tentative wording correlated negatively with retweets.
- **EDITORIAL:** put the certain claim in the body and an open question in the CTA.

**I9. Speaking voice. (DATA)**
- Speakers trying to persuade talk louder and vary their volume. This raises perceived confidence and works even when listeners notice it, without hurting perceived sincerity (Van Zant & Berger 2020, JPSP).
- Confident speech has falling intonation, a faster rate and fewer, shorter pauses. Rising intonation ("uptalk") signals doubt (Jiang & Pell 2017).

**I10. Polarize on ideas, stay receptive with people. (DATA)**
"Conversational receptiveness" markers make people seem better partners in disagreement and make their messages more persuasive. The markers are acknowledgement ("I understand"), subjectivity ("I believe") and hedges like "sometimes". Wikipedia editors who used them drew fewer personal attacks (Yeomans et al. 2020).
- **EDITORIAL:** be definitive in posts and receptive in comments and DMs.

**I11. How AI text hedges. It's not "I think". It's being stance-less.**
- **(DATA)** ChatGPT essays use far fewer interactional markers (hedges, boosters, attitude markers) than students' essays. The result is an impersonal, expository tone (Jiang & Hyland 2025).
- **(DATA, small sample, ChatGPT-3.5)** "May" appeared about 4.5 times per 1,000 tokens versus about 1.4 in human essays, roughly three times as often. Boosters like "clearly" and "definitely" appeared zero times (Shalevska).
- **(DATA)** Instruction-tuned models write denser text, with more nominalizations and more participial clauses. The gap from humans is larger than for base models (Reinhart et al., PNAS 2025). Excess "style words" ("delve" and similar) mark LLM-edited text (Kobak et al.).
- **(PRACTITIONER)** Wikipedia's AI Cleanup list of tells:
  - "serves as" or "represents" instead of "is"
  - "not just X, but Y" constructions
  - the rule of three
  - vague attribution ("some argue")
  - inflated significance ("pivotal")
- **(PRACTITIONER)** Contrarian templates are now cheap AI output (McTighe). A stance only works if it is anchored in proof or a story the AI can't supply. This lines up with the founder's thesis and Nik Setting's "intention can't be copied".

**I12. Vietnam context.**
- **(DATA)** Vietnam scores 70 on Hofstede power distance; the US scores 40. Pezzuti found the certainty effect is stronger under high power-distance beliefs.
  - **EDITORIAL inference, not tested in Vietnam:** definitive phrasing may land at least as well with VN audiences.
- **(PRACTITIONER)** The filler "kiểu" reads as confusion and as "leaving yourself a way out" (Kenh14).
- **(PRACTITIONER)** The main VN AI tells are stock phrases, Western-style long sentences and wrong forms of address (xưng hô) (findskill.ai).

## 2) Methods and templates

### 2a. Strip list: English (EDITORIAL compilation; categories B and H from Zinsser and the Wikipedia AI list)

| Category | Strip or flag | Replace with |
|---|---|---|
| A. Low-likelihood hedges | might, may, could, possibly, perhaps, maybe, potentially, arguably, to some extent, somewhat | Delete. If it's truly uncertain, use a condition ("works if…"), odds ("7 in 10 clients") or "likely" |
| B. Minimizers (Zinsser) | a bit, a little, sort of, kind of, rather, quite, pretty much, fairly, in a sense, more or less | Delete. "Be tired," not "a little tired" |
| C. Empty intensifiers | very, really, super, truly, actually, literally, incredibly | A stronger noun or verb, or a number |
| D. Throat-clearing | "So today I want to talk about…", "I just wanted to share…", "Let me start by saying…", "In this video…" | Start with the claim |
| E. Self-undercutting | just, only, "I'm no expert but", "hopefully this helps", "if that makes sense", "I could be wrong but", "I'm not sure but" | Delete. For real uncertainty: "My bet: X. Here's what would change my mind." |
| F. Tag questions / seeking validation | right?, you know?, don't you think?, isn't it? | A period. Save questions for the CTA |
| G. Ownerless openers | "it seems", "it could be argued", "in my opinion" before facts you know | State it, or own it: "I'd argue…" |
| H. AI fog | "it's important/worth noting", "plays a crucial/pivotal role", "serves as", "in today's fast-paced world", "navigate", "delve", "unlock", "landscape", "journey", "various factors", "everyone's journey is unique", "results may vary", "studies show" (no source), "Moreover/Furthermore/Additionally" as openers, "In conclusion" | "is" or "does", one concrete fact, a named source, or delete |
| I. Spoken fillers | um, uh, like, you know, basically, I mean, so yeah | A silent pause |

| Loại | Cắt hoặc gắn cờ | Thay bằng |
|---|---|---|

| E. Câu hỏi đuôi xin xác nhận | đúng không ạ?, phải không nè?, không biết mọi người thấy sao | Dấu chấm. Để câu hỏi ở CTA cuối bài |
| F. Mùi AI | không chỉ… mà còn…, đóng vai trò quan trọng/then chốt, trong bối cảnh/thời đại…, góp phần, mang lại giá trị, hãy cùng khám phá, hành trình, có thể nói, điều quan trọng cần lưu ý, tóm lại | Động từ cụ thể, con số, câu chuyện |

### 2c. Assertive phrasing patterns (DATA-backed where marked)

1. **Answer first.** Give the verdict in the first sentence (Pulford; BLUF).
2. **Present tense.** "This works", not "this worked for me" (Packard et al.).
3. **"X is Y."** Use plain copulas, not "serves as" or "represents" (AI-tell fix).
4. **Owned and high-likelihood when uncertain.** "I'd bet", "I'm 80% sure", "likely", never "might possibly" (Oba et al.; Gaertig & Simmons).
5. **Odds instead of self-doubt.** "Most clients see it in 6–8 weeks; some take 12", not "I'm not sure how long" (Gaertig & Simmons).
6. **Conditional scope.** "If you sell B2B, do X. If not, skip this." It stays honest and still polarizes.
7. **Imperative plus reason.** "Do X. Because Y."
8. **Proof next to the claim.** A number, name, scene or receipt (Packard trust cues; McTighe proof-of-work).

### 2d. Sentence-length and one-idea rules (PRACTITIONER norms; VN syllable figure is EDITORIAL)

- **One post, one big idea.** Write it in 15 words or fewer before drafting (Heaths' core idea).
- **One sentence, one idea.**
- **Sentence length:**
  - Average 15 words or fewer (Wylie).
  - Hard cap 25 (GOV.UK).
  - Hooks 12 words or fewer in EN, about 15–18 âm tiết in VN.
- **Rhythm:** add one punch sentence of 1–5 words per paragraph (Provost's rhythm advice, via Wylie).
- **Spoken:** each line fits in one breath, and the hook lands in about 3 seconds.
- **Delivery marks:** end statements with falling intonation and write ↓ in the script. Mark one emphasis word per line for volume change (Jiang & Pell; Van Zant & Berger).

### 2e. Stance statement templates, with a guardrail for each

Every stance must pass the **3D test**:
- **Disagree:** a sensible peer could disagree.
- **Defend:** you can defend it in two sentences.
- **Demonstrate:** you have proof or a story only you could tell.

Templates (sources in parentheses):
1. "[Practice] is dead. Here's what replaced it for me." The claim must be your own switch, with a date or number.
2. "Stop [X]. Do [Y] instead." Y is mandatory.
3. "Most [audience] think [X]. They're wrong. [Reason]. [Proof]." Refutational, per O'Keefe.
4. "You don't have a [X] problem. You have a [Y] problem." Max one per post, because "not X, but Y" is an AI tell.
5. "I will never [practice]. Here's why." An absolute about your values, which is character (founder thesis; Soo Wei Goh).
6. "If you [condition], [verdict]. If not, ignore this."
7. "[Industry] wants you to believe X. I've seen Y." The enemy frame (Matt Gray).
8. "What I got wrong about [X] for [N] years." Shows calibration and the whole truth (Tenney; Hormozi).
9. "[Belief] is the excuse we use to avoid [hard thing]."
10. "Unpopular opinion: …" is saturated. Use it at most once a month.

### 2f. Certainty without arrogance: the calibration ladder (EDITORIAL, built on Tenney, Gaertig & Simmons, Oba et al., Sezer, Yeomans)

| Claim type | How to say it | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Your values, standards, choices | Absolute, no hedge | "I don't take clients who skip homework." |
| Patterns from your own evidence | Quantified ownership | "In 140 discovery calls this year, the ones who stalled had no offer." |
| Predictions or outcomes for others | A range or odds, confident delivery | "Most see it in 6–8 weeks. Some take 12. A few never do, and here's why." |
| Never | Certainty about other people's worth, guaranteed outcomes, humblebrags, "I'm not sure but…" | — |

**Use "in my experience" only when** the claim rests on your sample alone and the audience might over-generalize. Even then, prefer the quantified version ("in 140 calls…"). Never put it before an instruction or a value statement.

When you are wrong, say so publicly. Calibration protects credibility (Tenney).

### 2g. Concision techniques

1. **Cut by half, twice** (Krug). Apply it to captions and hooks.
2. **One-sentence test** (Heaths). If the audience remembers one line, which one is it? Put it first.
3. **Delete the first sentence.** It is usually throat-clearing.
4. **Hedge-delete test** (Paul Graham). Delete every qualifier. If the sentence becomes false, narrow it with a condition or number instead of putting the fog back. Graham's line: "Useful writing is bold, but true."
5. **De-nominalize.** "Make a decision" becomes "decide". "Tiến hành thực hiện việc đánh giá" becomes "đánh giá".
6. **"Is" test.** "Plays a key role in" becomes "is" or "does".
7. **Specificity swap.** Replace one abstract noun per paragraph with a number, a name or a scene.
8. **Read aloud.** Anything you can't say in one breath gets split.

### 2h. Fixing AI hedging (instruction block for the pack's prompts, paraphrased)

- Use no hedge words from lists A–H unless a probability, range or condition follows.
- Write at least one owned claim per post ("I…" or "You…"). Use present tense, active voice, and "is" rather than "serves as".
- At most one "not X, but Y" and no reflexive groups of three.
- Every claim gets a trust cue from the user's Proof Bank. Never invent statistics; leave [brackets] instead.
- End the body on a definitive line. Questions go only in the CTA.

### 2i. Definitive Voice Checklist (12 points, pass at 10 or more)

1. The first sentence is the claim or verdict, with no warm-up.
2. The hook is 12 words or fewer (VN about 18 âm tiết) and has zero hedges.
3. The post has one big idea, which you can state in 15 words or fewer.
4. At least one sentence a sensible peer could disagree with (3D test).
5. That stance is owned ("I", "you") and backed by a number, story or receipt.
6. Values are stated as absolutes. Outcomes are stated as ranges or conditions.
7. No list A–F words in claims. No "I'm not sure". No "just" or "hopefully".
8. No AI fog (list H/F): no "serves as", "important to note", "journey", stacked "not just X but Y".
9. Average sentence 15 words or fewer, none over 25, at least one punch line of 5 words or fewer.
10. Present tense and active voice in the key claims.
11. Opposing views are refuted, not merely mentioned. The person is never attacked.
12. Spoken version: no "um/like/kiểu", falling intonation marked, no uptalk on claims.

## 3) Implications for the Content Machine (EDITORIAL)

1. **Add a "Voice Lint" pass after scripting.** It runs the EN and VN strip lists (2a/2b), flags each hit, rewrites using 2c, and reports three numbers: hedges per 100 words (target 0 in hooks, 1 or fewer in the body), average sentence length, and count of AI tells.
2. **Onboarding should build two banks.**
   - **Stance Bank:** 3 big ideas, the enemy, what you're "quietly intolerant of", and 5 "I always / I never" value absolutes.
   - **Proof Bank:** numbers, client stories, before/after receipts.
   Stance without proof triggers the AI-slop and arrogance failure (I7, I11).
3. **Put the calibration ladder (2f) into the scripting rules.** Values are absolute, evidence is quantified, outcomes come as ranges. Ban "I'm not sure". Never fabricate statistics; bracket them.
4. **Edge Check.** Either add a 5th pillar, *Conviction*, built from checklist items 4–6 and 11, or score it under Authenticity. Also add an "AI-tell" sub-check to Authenticity, since stance-less text is the main AI signature.
5. **Expertise dial setting.**
   - Newer coaches: maximum certainty.
   - Established coaches: allow one "where I might be wrong" line (Karmarkar & Tormala).
6. **Ideation.** Generate stance hooks from templates 2e, then filter them with the 3D test. Down-rank generic contrarian lines that lack a personal anchor.
7. **CTA design.** Certain body, open question at the end, to harvest replies (I8, weak data).
8. **Video scripts.** One sentence per line, ↓ marks for falling endings, one emphasis word per line, and a "pause instead of um/kiểu" coaching note (I9).
9. **Community replies module.** Use receptive language in comments and DMs ("I hear you… I'd still argue…"). Polarize on ideas, never on people (I10).
10. **VN edition.** Ship the VN filler and AI-tell lists, a xưng hô selector, and syllable-based length rules. Note that the high power-distance context may favor certainty (inference), but warmth particles stay.
11. **Founder thesis tie-in.** Definitive wording is the delivery layer. Character (values, enemy, vision) is the source. The lint pass should fail any post that is definitive but has no owned stance or proof. That combination is exactly the cheap AI contrarian content the founder warns about.
