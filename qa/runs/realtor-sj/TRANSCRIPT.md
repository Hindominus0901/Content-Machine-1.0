A local lender knows the towns and the agents, and when the listing agent calls with a question on a Saturday, somebody answers. I have a couple I trust. I'm not going to name them in a post, but ask me.

2. Get the lender before you fall for a house.
Most people do it backwards. They find the house first and then scramble for paperwork while three other offers come in. If your lender is already lined up, your offer can go in the same day you walk the house. That's often the whole difference. The other way, the seller's side asks who's the lender and the answer is "we're working on it." That offer goes to the bottom of the pile. Lender first, then the house. It sounds boring. It's how an offer gets taken seriously.

3. Your lender is one third of the plan, not all of it.
This is step one of what I call the bridge buyer plan. Step two is a Saturday seeing the towns: three neighborhoods, I drive, we get lunch. Step three is the offer strategy: the pre-inspection first, then an escalation clause, so you can beat other offers without overpaying by a mile. Most Philly renters have never even been to these towns, so step two is where they find out if they're a Cherry Hill person or a Haddonfield person. Skip step one and the other two don't hold.

None of this is magic. It's order. Lender, towns, offer. In that order, you stop learning on your first three or four offers.

If you're renting in Philly and the word "pre-approval" makes your stomach drop, start with the lender questions. They're on page one of my first-time buyer checklist for South Jersey.
