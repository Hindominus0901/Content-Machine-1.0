**Machine:** ◆ Content Machine · Weekly Talk
Question 1 of 5. Tell me about the first time you saw a buyer lose a bid and think it was their fault. Where were you?

**Rachel:** Okay so this one's easy. Priya. We were in my car outside a house in Haddonfield, it was raining, and the listing agent had just texted me that they went with another offer. Third one. Priya had her sunglasses on in the rain, which is how I knew. And Sam kept saying "maybe we just can't afford it over here" and Priya said "maybe we're just bad at this."

And I said, it's not you, it's the offer. Because it really wasn't. Their price was fine. Their lender was fine. What they did was write an offer on a house they'd seen for eleven minutes, with an inspection they hadn't done, and a number somebody else beat by a little. They lost to a better strategy, not a better person.

So I told them, let's stop bidding for a minute. Let's look at the next house the way we'd look at it after we owned it. Before the offer. We'll get the inspector in first, find out what's wrong, then write the offer with an escalation clause, so we beat the other number without paying double.

**Machine:** Got it. Next: Question 2 of 5. What do people believe about losing bids that you'd want them to stop believing?

**Rachel:** That it's about money. Everybody thinks the winner just paid more. Sometimes, sure. But more often the winner was cleaner. Fewer conditions, a lender the listing agent knows, an offer that was easy to say yes to. If you're a first-time buyer from Philly, you don't know that. Nobody tells you. You just see "lost again" and you take it personally.

So the one thing I'd want them to know: you can look at the house before you bid on it. Nobody tells you that. I say it every week and every week somebody in the comments goes "wait, you can do that?"

**Machine:** Got it. Next: Question 3 of 5. Say the plan out loud, as if the person is sitting next to you.

**Rachel:** Three steps. One, a pre-approval with a local lender, somebody who picks up on a Saturday. Two, a weekend neighborhood tour, three towns, I drive, we have lunch. Three, the offer strategy. Pre-inspection first, escalation clause in the offer. And, park that, I could talk about the closing costs forever but that's a different video.

Honestly, though, and this is probably the thing nobody says, the second step is where it changes. They go from "I think I should buy a house" to "I want to live in Haddonfield." It's not a spreadsheet thing. It's a Saturday thing.

Okay, actually, scratch that, I'd say it's for anybody who rents in Philly and has lost a bid. That's the person.

**Machine:** Got it. Next: Question 5 of 5. What is the one thing they should do this week, even if they never call you?

**Rachel:** Pick one house you already lost, or one you're looking at on Zillow right now. Write down what you'd check if you could walk through it with an inspector. Roof, basement, the furnace, the electrical panel. That's it. Then ask yourself: would I still bid? And would I bid differently?

If you do that once you'll never again write an offer on a house you saw for eleven minutes. That's the whole thing. One house. This week. Look.
