**◆ Content Machine · Weekly Talk 1/5**
This week is about the hour you never do. Tell me about a time your own plan fell apart. When was it, and what broke first?
NEXT → tap the small mic and tell it like you'd tell a buddy.

**Dan:**
Okay so this is kind of embarrassing cause I'm supposed to be the guy. So after the birthday party thing I was doing the garage thing, 30 minutes, and it was going good, and then January 2024 I decided I was gonna get serious. Like serious serious. Old trainer me came back. I wrote myself a whole plan, an hour a day, 5 days a week, like I used to write for people at the gym. Push day, pull day, legs, the whole thing, I had it in my notes app with little check marks. And for like 3 weeks it was great, I was up at 5, I was in the garage, I felt like a stud honestly.

And then February the stomach bug went through the house. Lily first, then Owen, then me, then the dog threw up too for some reason, unrelated. And Jess picked up extra shifts cause half the nurses on her floor were out with the same thing. So I'm solo, everybody's sick, I'm doing laundry at like 2 in the morning, and by Thursday I'd missed 6 sessions. And I remember sitting on the edge of the tub holding a bucket, and I'm looking at my notes app with all the empty check marks, and I literally thought, well, I blew it. Like the exact same thing that guy at the gym said to me in the parking lot. I just couldn't keep up with it. And I'm the trainer. I know better. I knew better and I still did the all or nothing thing to myself.

And here's the part that gets me. I took like 3 weeks off after that. Not cause I was sick, I was fine after a few days. I took 3 weeks off cause I felt like I'd already failed, so why bother. That's the thing I want dads to hear. The plan didn't break cause of the flu. The plan broke cause it only had one setting. Full. There was no setting for the house is on fire. And every plan out there is like that, it's built for the good week, and dads don't get a lot of good weeks. So you miss 3 days and the plan says you failed, and you believe it.

So what broke first? Honestly, my head broke first. The body was fine. The schedule broke, sure, but what actually killed it was me looking at those empty check marks and deciding I was a guy who quits. And I wasn't. I was a guy whose kids had the stomach bug.

**◆ Content Machine · Weekly Talk 2/5**
Got it. Next: what did you do the first time you went smaller on purpose? Where were you, and what did it look like?
NEXT → same mic, as long as you want.

**Dan:**
Okay so this is like a month after that. March 2024, work sends me to Indianapolis for 3 days for training on our new dispatch software, super exciting stuff, I know. And the whole drive over I'm like, okay, the hotel has a gym, I'm gonna get back on it, I'm gonna do the hour, restart the plan. And I get there and the hotel gym has a piece of paper taped on the door that says closed for maintenance. And old me, like even the week before me, would've said well that's it, can't train, I'll start Monday. Which, funny, cause that's my brother in law's whole thing, I start every Monday.

But I'm standing in the room, the TV's on, some home renovation show, and I've got my backpack, and I just kind of looked at it. And I put like 6 of those little hotel water bottles in it and I did 20 minutes. Between the two beds. Backpack squats, push ups with my feet up on the bed, holding the backpack out in front of me walking up and down the hallway between the beds like an idiot, getting down on the carpet and back up without my hands. 20 minutes. I was sweating, the guy in the next room probably thought I was moving furniture.

And I remember sitting on the end of the bed after and thinking, that counted. That actually counted. It wasn't the hour. It wasn't the plan. But I didn't go to zero. And that was the first time I got it, like really got it, that the thing that kills you isn't doing a small workout. It's doing nothing cause you can't do the big one.

So that's where the minimum week came from. That trip. I came home and I wrote it on a sticky note and put it on the garage door: 2 times 20. That's the floor. It's not the plan, it's the floor. The plan is 3 times 30. But if the week goes sideways, if somebody's sick, if Jess is on 3 shifts in a row, if I'm in a hotel, I do 2 times 20 and I'm still in. I'm still a guy who trains. And honestly, since that sticky note went up I don't think I've had a zero week. I mean I'm sure I've had bad weeks. But not a zero.

And the funny thing is, it made the normal weeks easier too. Cause the pressure's off. When you know the worst case is 20 minutes twice, you stop being scared of the week.

**◆ Content Machine · Weekly Talk 3/5**
Got it. Next: when you tell a dad that 30 minutes is enough, what does he say back? Give me his exact words if you remember them.
NEXT → tap the mic.

**Dan:**
Oh man. Every time. Number one is "that can't be enough." Like they literally don't believe me. The guy at work, the "I used to be in shape" guy, he said "I'd rather wait till I can do it right." And I was like buddy, how long have you been waiting. He didn't love that. And in the dads group chat somebody wrote "30 minutes doesn't do anything," with like the eye roll emoji.

And look, I get it. I get why they think that. The whole industry told them more is better. At the gym the hour was like a status thing. Guys would brag about two hour sessions. The guy who lives at the gym is the guy everybody looks up to. So if you come in and say, hey, I do 30 minutes, it sounds like you're not serious. It sounds like a cop out.

But here's what I tell them. The hour isn't real for you right now. It's not real. You don't have it. You have a kid who wakes up at 6 and a job at 7:30 and a wife on a 12 hour shift. So the choice isn't 30 minutes or an hour. The choice is 30 minutes or zero. And when you put it like that, nobody picks zero. Nobody.

And the other thing they say, which I actually love, is "I just want to keep up with my kids." That's the goal they come back to when I ask what they actually want. Not abs. Nobody says abs, at least not out loud. They want to keep up. And you don't need an hour to keep up with an 8 year old. You need to not quit. That's it. You need to show up 3 times a week for a long time. The guys at the gym who were still there years later, they weren't the two hour guys, the two hour guys burned out by spring. It was the boring guys. Tuesday, Thursday, Saturday, a half hour, done, go home. Those guys kept showing up for years. I didn't count them or anything, it's just what I saw on the floor.

So when a dad says that can't be enough, I say, enough for what? Enough to look like the guy on the program cover, no. Enough to carry your kid on your shoulders back to the car after the game without your back going? Yeah. That's enough.

**◆ Content Machine · Weekly Talk 4/5**
Got it. Next: walk me through your minimum week like I'm a dad standing in your garage. What's in it, and what do you leave out?
NEXT → tap the mic.

**Dan:**
Okay so you're standing in my garage. There's one kettlebell, a pull-up bar in the rafters, a bench I got off a neighbor, and a bike with a flat tire that's been flat since like July. That's the gym.

A normal week is 3 times 30. A minimum week is 2 times 20. And the 20 is the same every time, I don't want anybody thinking. First you get down on the floor and get back up without your hands, a few times, that's your warm up and it's also kind of the test. Then you pick something heavy up off the ground, the kettlebell, properly, with your legs and your hips, not your back. Then you push, push ups, from your knees is fine, nobody's watching. Then you pull, the bar, or a row if you can't do a pull-up yet, I couldn't. Then you carry something heavy and walk, like you're carrying groceries from the car, cause you are, every week. And that's it. Floor, lift, push, pull, carry. 20 minutes. Done.

What I leave out. No running in the minimum week. No fancy equipment. No 15 minute warm up with the foam roller and the bands, if you have 20 minutes you're not spending 15 of it warming up. No ab circuits. No meal plan, I'm not touching food in the minimum week, you've got enough going on. And no guilt. That's the main thing I leave out. If you did the 2 times 20, you did the week. You don't get partial credit, you get full credit.

Oh and this is the driveway thing. So on Jess's shift days, which are usually Tuesdays, I've got both kids after school and I can't be in the garage with the door closed. So I do my 20 in the driveway. Kettlebell on the concrete, the kids ride their bikes in circles around me, sometimes my daughter does the floor thing with me, she thinks it's a game. And the neighbor across the street, retired guy, his lawn looks like a golf course, he walks over one time with his coffee and he goes, "What are you training for?" Like am I doing a marathon or something. And I said, "Saturday." And he kind of laughed, but I meant it. Saturday's when I'm at the park, at soccer, carrying a kid on my shoulders back to the car, getting down on the grass for the stupid relay race. That's the event. That's what I'm training for. Saturday.

And I think that's the part dads get when they see it. You're not training for a show. You're training for Saturday.

Got it. Last one: a dad watching this starts every Monday and quits by Thursday. What do you want him to do this week?

**Dan:**
Tonight. Not Monday. That's the first thing. Don't start Monday, Monday's the problem. Tonight, after the kids are down, open the family calendar, the shared one on your phone, the one your wife actually looks at, and put in 3 slots of 30 minutes like they're dentist appointments. Not when you'd like to work out. When you actually can. If that's 5:30 in the morning, okay. If it's 8:45 at night after bedtime, okay. If it's your lunch break in the parking lot, okay. Put them where they survive.

Then grab a sticky note and write your minimum week on it. 2 times 20. Put it somewhere you'll see it, the garage door, the bathroom mirror, I don't care. And this week, at some point, something's gonna go wrong. Kid gets sick, work blows up, you sleep through the alarm. That's fine. You look at the sticky note, you do the 20, and you're still in. You didn't blow it. Never zero.

And I'd tell him, look, I'm not gonna promise you abs. I'm not gonna promise you a number on the scale, I don't do that. I'm promising you that if you do it this way, you'll still be doing it in March. And the guy who's still doing it in March is the guy who can keep up with his kids in June. That's the whole thing.

Oh and don't buy anything this week. Don't go buy a home gym. Use the floor, use a backpack, use the stairs. If you're still doing it in a month, buy one kettlebell. That's it.

I think that's it. Done.
